1

The Extra Step: Isolate Before You Substitute

In Lesson 3 one equation already read y=\ldots or x=\ldots. In a system like x+2y=11,\ 3x-y=5 neither variable stands alone, so substitution needs one new step first: isolate a variable yourself, then continue exactly as before.

Choose well: look for a coefficient of 1 or -1

Scan both equations for a coefficient of \mathbf{1} or \mathbf{-1} and isolate that variable: it takes one step and never creates a fraction. In x+2y=11,\ 3x-y=5, x in Equation 1 gives x=11-2y and y in Equation 2 gives y=3x-5. Isolating the 2y or the 3x instead would force \dfrac{11-x}{2} or \dfrac{5+y}{3}.

Isolating in one step

Move the other term to the other side by doing the opposite to both sides. If the variable is left with a -1 in front, like -y, multiply every term by -1: every sign flips.

!
-y=8-3x is not finished. Flip every sign: y=-8+3x, which is y=3x-8. Writing y=8-3x is the most common slip.
2

The Full Five-Step Process

Step 1
Isolate
a variable in one equation (coefficient 1 or -1)
Step 2
Substitute
that expression, in brackets, into the other equation
Step 3
Solve
for the remaining variable
Step 4
Back-substitute
into the Step 1 expression
Step 5
Check & state
both values in the original equations; write (x,\ y)

For x+2y=11,\ 3x-y=5: x=11-2y, so 3(11-2y)-y=5, giving 33-7y=5, y=4; then x=11-2(4)=3. Both originals check, so the solution is (3,\ 4).

Example 1

All five steps

Solve the system \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{2x+y=12}, \textcolor{#c27a0e}{x-4y=-3} completely, showing all five steps.

Step 1: Isolate a variable (look for a coefficient of 1 or -1).
In 2x+y=12 the y has coefficient 1, so isolate y in Equation 1: one step, no fractions.
\boldsymbol{y=12-2x}
Steps 2–3: Substitute into the other equation, then solve it.
Substitute into the other equation x-4y=-3:
x-4(12-2x)=-3
x-48+8x=-3
9x=45, so \boldsymbol{x=5}
Steps 4–5: Back-substitute, check both originals, and state the ordered pair.
y=12-2(5)=2
Check: 2(5)+2=12 ✓   5-4(2)=5-8=-3 ✓
The solution is \boldsymbol{(5,\ 2)}.
Example 2

Working through the stages

Solve the system \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{x+5y=17}, \textcolor{#c27a0e}{4x-y=5}, working through the stages below.

(a) Steps 1–2: isolate a variable, then substitute into the other equation.
x in Equation 1 has coefficient 1: x=17-5y.
Substitute into 4x-y=5:
4(17-5y)-y=5
(b) Steps 3–5: solve, back-substitute, check, and state (x,\ y).
68-20y-y=5
68-21y=5\ \Rightarrow\ 21y=63\ \Rightarrow\ y=3
x=17-5(3)=2
Check: 2+5(3)=17 ✓   4(2)-3=5 ✓
Solution: \boldsymbol{(2,\ 3)}
Example 3

Only one easy choice

For \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{5x+y=21}, \textcolor{#c27a0e}{2x-3y=5}, only one variable in the whole system has a coefficient of 1 or -1.

(a) Which variable will you isolate, and in which equation? Why?
Isolate \boldsymbol{y} in \boldsymbol{5x+y=21} (Equation 1). It is the only variable in the system with a coefficient of 1, so it comes out in a single step as y=21-5x with no fractions. Isolating anything else would give a denominator of 5, 2 or 3.
(b) Solve the system completely.
y=21-5x; substitute into 2x-3y=5:
2x-3(21-5x)=5
2x-63+15x=5
17x=68\ \Rightarrow\ x=4
y=21-5(4)=1
Check: 5(4)+1=21 ✓   2(4)-3(1)=5 ✓
Solution: \boldsymbol{(4,\ 1)}
Example 4

A coefficient of -1: watch your signs

Solve \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{3x-y=8}, \textcolor{#c27a0e}{x+4y=7}. The variable you isolate has a coefficient of -1: watch your signs.

Solve and check.
From 3x-y=8: -y=8-3x, so y=3x-8 (every sign flips).
Substitute into x+4y=7: x+4(3x-8)=7
x+12x-32=7\ \Rightarrow\ 13x=39\ \Rightarrow\ x=3
y=3(3)-8=1
Check: 3(3)-1=8 ✓   3+4(1)=7 ✓
Solution: \boldsymbol{(3,\ 1)}

When no coefficient is 1 or -1

Sometimes every choice makes a fraction. That is fine: isolate anyway, keep the fraction in brackets when you substitute, then multiply every term by the denominator. The fraction disappears and the numbers come out whole.

Example 5

A fraction that clears

Solve \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{2x+3y=14}, \textcolor{#c27a0e}{5x-4y=12}. No coefficient here is 1 or -1, so isolating creates a fraction; carry it carefully and it will cancel by the end.

Solve and check.
Isolate x in 2x+3y=14: x=\dfrac{14-3y}{2}.
Substitute into 5x-4y=12: 5\left(\dfrac{14-3y}{2}\right)-4y=12
Multiply every term by 2: 5(14-3y)-8y=24
70-15y-8y=24\ \Rightarrow\ 70-23y=24\ \Rightarrow\ 23y=46\ \Rightarrow\ y=2
x=\dfrac{14-3(2)}{2}=\dfrac{8}{2}=4
Check: 2(4)+3(2)=14 ✓   5(4)-4(2)=12 ✓
Solution: \boldsymbol{(4,\ 2)}

Step 5: check in the original equations

Check in the equations you were given, not in your rearranged one: if the isolating step had a slip, the rearranged equation has the slip too. A pair that balances only one equation is not the solution.

✓

Key ideas

Isolate first. When neither equation reads y=\ldots or x=\ldots, make one yourself, then carry on exactly as in Lesson 3.
Choose well. A coefficient of 1 or -1 means one step and no fractions. Look at the coefficients, not which letter comes first.
Watch the signs. -y=8-3x becomes y=3x-8: every sign flips. The number in front of a bracket multiplies every term.
Fractions clear. If every choice makes a fraction, multiply every term by the denominator. Then check both values in the original equations and state (x,\ y).
