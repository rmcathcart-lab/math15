The Three Solving Methods

A solution to a system is the ordered pair (x,\ y) that satisfies both equations at once. Three methods find it; all three must agree.

Method
When to use it
Watch out for
GraphingLesson 1
Both equations are already y=mx+b, or rewrite easily; you want to see the answer.
A graph only estimates: it is unreliable unless the lines cross at integer coordinates.
SubstitutionLessons 3, 4
One equation is solved for a variable, or some variable has coefficient 1 or -1 so you can isolate it cleanly.
Substitute into the other equation (using the same one gives 0=0); back-substitute and check in both originals.
EliminationLessons 5, 6
Both equations are in Ax+By=C form and no coefficient is 1 or -1.
Opposite coefficients: add. Matching coefficients: subtract, which flips the sign of every term, the constant too. If nothing matches, multiply one or both equations first.

Choosing a method: the fastest road

Ask the questions in order. The first “yes” tells you the fastest method. Every road leads to the same answer; some just get there faster.

Example 1

Graphing

The system y=x+1 and y=-2x+7 is graphed below; the lines cross at integer coordinates.

(a) State the solution: x= ____, y= ____
x=\boldsymbol2, y=\boldsymbol3. The two lines meet at the grid point 2 right and 3 up.
(b) Write the solution as an ordered pair, and check it in both original equations.
Solution: \textcolor{#7b4fc4}{(2,\ 3)}
Check in y=x+1: 2+1=3 ✓
Check in y=-2x+7: -2(2)+7=-4+7=3 ✓
It satisfies both equations, so it is the solution.

Example 2

Classifying

Without graphing, state whether each system has one solution, no solution, or infinitely many.

(a) y=6x-1y=6x+5
Both slopes m=6; intercepts -1 and 5 differ. Parallel lines: no solution.
(b) y=2x+7y=-4x+1
Slopes 2 and -4 are different. Lines cross once: one solution.
(c) y=-3x+6y=-3x+6
Same m=-3 and same b=6. One line drawn twice: infinitely many.
(d) y=x-9y=-x-9
Slopes 1 and -1 are different. One solution (they cross at (0,\ -9)).

Examples 3 and 4

Substitution

Solve each system using substitution. In (4), isolate a variable first.

3. y=4x-3    2x+y=15
Substitute 4x-3 for y in 2x+y=15:
2x+(\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{4x-3})=15
6x-3=15\ \Rightarrow\ 6x=18\ \Rightarrow\ x=3
y=4(3)-3=9
Solution: \textcolor{#7b4fc4}{\boldsymbol{(3,\ 9)}}
Check: 2(3)+9=15 ✓
4. x+3y=19    2x-y=3
Isolate y in 2x-y=3: y=2x-3.
x+3(\textcolor{#c27a0e}{2x-3})=19
x+6x-9=19\ \Rightarrow\ 7x=28\ \Rightarrow\ x=4
y=2(4)-3=5
Solution: \textcolor{#7b4fc4}{\boldsymbol{(4,\ 5)}}
Check: 4+3(5)=19 ✓

Examples 5 and 6

Elimination

Solve each system using elimination. In (6), multiply each equation by a suitable constant to eliminate y.

5. 3x+2y=18    x-2y=-2
The y-terms are opposites (+2y, -2y), so add:
(3x+x)+(2y-2y)=18+(-2)
4x=16\ \Rightarrow\ x=4
Back-substitute in x-2y=-2: 4-2y=-2
-2y=-6\ \Rightarrow\ y=3
Solution: \textcolor{#7b4fc4}{\boldsymbol{(4,\ 3)}}
Check: 3(4)+2(3)=18 ✓
6. 4x+3y=23    3x+2y=16
To eliminate y, make both y-terms 6y: \times2 and \times3.
8x+6y=46
9x+6y=48
Matching coefficients, so subtract: -x=-2\ \Rightarrow\ x=2
3(2)+2y=16\ \Rightarrow\ 2y=10\ \Rightarrow\ y=5
Solution: \textcolor{#7b4fc4}{\boldsymbol{(2,\ 5)}}
Check: 4(2)+3(5)=23 ✓

Example 7

Word problem: tournament tickets

A youth soccer tournament sold 150 tickets, some at the student price of $6 and some at the adult price of $10. Total ticket sales were $1 220.

(a) Let x be the number of student tickets and y the number of adult tickets. Write a system of two equations that models this situation.
Tickets counted: \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{x+y=150}
Money collected: \textcolor{#c27a0e}{6x+10y=1\,220}
(b) Solve the system to find the number of student tickets and the number of adult tickets sold.
From the first equation y=150-x:
6x+10(150-x)=1\,220
6x+1\,500-10x=1\,220
-4x=-280\ \Rightarrow\ x=70, so y=150-70=80.
\textcolor{#7b4fc4}{70} student tickets and \textcolor{#7b4fc4}{80} adult tickets were sold.
Check: 6(70)+10(80)=420+800=1\,220 ✓

Challenge 1

Choosing a method

Consider the system  5x-3y=19    2x+y=12.

(a) Decide whether substitution or elimination is faster here, and explain your choice in one sentence.
Substitution. In 2x+y=12 the variable y has coefficient 1, so it isolates in one step as y=12-2x with no fractions.
(Elimination also works well here: 3\times the second equation gives +3y, the opposite of -3y.)
(b) Solve the system using the method you chose.
y=12-2x, substitute into 5x-3y=19:
5x-3(12-2x)=19
5x-36+6x=19
11x=55\ \Rightarrow\ x=5
y=12-2(5)=2
Solution: \textcolor{#7b4fc4}{\boldsymbol{(5,\ 2)}}
Check: 5(5)-3(2)=25-6=19 ✓
Challenges 2 and 3

Mixed skills

2. A rectangular deck has a perimeter of 60 m. Its length is 6 m more than twice its width.
(a) Let L be the length and W the width. Write a system of two equations that models this situation.
Perimeter: \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{2L+2W=60}
Length description: \textcolor{#c27a0e}{L=2W+6}
3. Shop A charges a $30 diagnostic fee plus $45 per hour of labour; Shop B charges a $55 diagnostic fee plus $45 per hour of labour.
(a) Write an equation for the total cost at each shop in terms of the hours h of labour.
Shop A: \textcolor{#1f5bd6}{C=45h+30}
Shop B: \textcolor{#c27a0e}{C=45h+55}
2(b) Solve the system to find the length and width of the deck.
Substitute L=2W+6 into 2L+2W=60:
2(\textcolor{#c27a0e}{2W+6})+2W=60
4W+12+2W=60
6W=48\ \Rightarrow\ W=8
L=2(8)+6=22
Length =\textcolor{#7b4fc4}{22} m, width =\textcolor{#7b4fc4}{8} m.
Check: 2(22)+2(8)=44+16=60 ✓
3(b) Without solving, classify the system and explain using the slopes and intercepts.
(c) What does your answer to (b) mean about the two shops’ pricing?
(b) Both lines have slope 45 but intercepts 30 and 55, so they are parallel and never meet: the system has no solution.
(c) The two shops never charge the same amount for a job. Their hourly rates are identical, so Shop B is always $25 more expensive, no matter how many hours the repair takes.

Perimeter is the distance all the way around: two lengths and two widths, so 2L+2W=P. Don’t write L+W=60: that is only half the fence.
✓

Key ideas

The solution is the one point on both lines: where the graphs cross. Whatever method you use, check it in both original equations.
Pick the fastest road. Lines already drawn: read the crossing. A variable alone or with coefficient \pm1: substitute. Matching or opposite coefficients: eliminate (subtract or add). Nothing matches: multiply first.
How many solutions? Different slopes: one. Same slope, different b: none (parallel; algebra ends false, like 0=8). Same slope, same b: infinitely many (0=0).
Word problems. Define two variables, write one equation per fact (count and value, sum and comparison, perimeter 2L+2W=P), solve, and answer in words.
