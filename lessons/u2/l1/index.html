Powers, Exponent Vocabulary & the Product Law

3\times3\times3\times3 is a lot to write, so we write 3^{4}. Today we review powers from Grade 9: their parts, their values, and where the brackets go. Then we build the first exponent law just by counting factors.

I can…
  • name the base, the exponent and the power, and write a power as a repeated multiplication
  • evaluate powers, and explain why (-3)^{2} and -3^{2} are different
  • evaluate a power with a variable base by substituting the whole value, in brackets
  • use the Product Law a^{m}\times a^{n}=a^{m+n}: same base, add the exponents
  • multiply terms with coefficients: multiply the numbers, add the exponents

What Is a Power?

An exponent counts repeated multiplication: 3^{4}=3\times3\times3\times3=81. The base can be a variable too: p\times p\times p=p^{3}.

t^{k}
baset: the factor that is repeated
exponentk: how many times the base is used as a factor
powert^{k}: the whole thing, base and exponent together

Base, exponent, repeated multiplication

6\times6=36

x\times x\times x\times x\times x

2

Evaluating Powers

To evaluate a power, expand it, then multiply it out. Watch two things.

Order matters. 2^{5}=2\times2\times2\times2\times2=32, but 5^{2}=5\times5=25. The base is the factor; the exponent counts it.
Brackets matter with negatives. (-3)^{2}=(-3)(-3)=9, but -3^{2}=-(3\times3)=-9.
With an odd exponent the difference disappears: (-2)^{3}=-2^{3}=-8.

What does the exponent reach?

The exponent reaches only what is inside the brackets. With no brackets, it reaches only the number right under it, and a minus sign in front stays outside the power.

When does the difference disappear?

Each extra factor of a negative number flips the sign. Watch the two kinds of power side by side as the exponent grows.

!
Even exponent: (-3)^{2} is positive but -3^{2} is negative. Read the brackets before you calculate.
!
Odd exponent: they agree. (-2)^{3}=-8 and -2^{3}=-8.
Checking at home? The unit test is no-calculator, but the calculator follows the same rule. (−)3x²ENTER gives −9: it squares only the 3. To square −3, use brackets: ((−)3)x²ENTER gives 9.
Example 2

Order and brackets

Evaluate each power without a calculator.

(a) 2^{5}
2\times2\times2\times2\times2=\boldsymbol{32}
(b) 5^{2}
5\times5=\boldsymbol{25}
(c) (-3)^{2}
(-3)(-3)=\boldsymbol{9}
The bracket makes −3 the base.
(d) -3^{2}
-(3\times3)=\boldsymbol{-9}
Only the 3 is squared.
(e) (-2)^{3}
(-2)(-2)(-2)=\boldsymbol{-8}
Three negative factors.
(f) -2^{3}
-(2\times2\times2)=\boldsymbol{-8}
The minus stays outside.
Think Without multiplying anything out: is (-1)^{100} positive or negative? What about -1^{100}?
(-1)^{100}=\boldsymbol{1}: 100 negative factors pair up into positives. -1^{100}=-(1^{100})=\boldsymbol{-1}: the minus is outside the power.
3

Powers with Variable Bases

x^{3} means x\times x\times x. It can’t be simplified until we know x. To evaluate it, substitute the whole value (sign included, in brackets), then evaluate: if x=4, then x^{3}=(4)^{3}=64.

!
Negative value? Brackets first. If x=-6, write x^{2}=(-6)^{2}=36. Writing -6^{2} would give -36.
Example 3

Substitute, then evaluate

Evaluate each power for the given value of the variable.

(a) a^{3}, a=2
a^{3}=(2)^{3}
=2\times2\times2=\boldsymbol{8}
(b) b^{2}, b=5
b^{2}=(5)^{2}
=5\times5=\boldsymbol{25}
(c) c^{4}, c=-2
c^{4}=(-2)^{4}
=(-2)(-2)(-2)(-2)=\boldsymbol{16}
(d) d^{3}, d=-1
d^{3}=(-1)^{3}
=(-1)(-1)(-1)=\boldsymbol{-1}
4

The Product Law

Expanding shows what happens when the bases match: 2^{3}\times2^{2}=(2\times2\times2)(2\times2)=2^{5}, since there are 3+2=5 factors. The exponents add.

Product Law  a^{m}\times a^{n}=a^{m+n}
Keep the base, add the exponents. The bases must be identical: it does not apply to 2^{3}\times5^{2}.
!
Keep the base. 2^{3}\times2^{4}=2^{7}, not 4^{7}. Only the exponents change.
!
A letter on its own has exponent 1: n=n^{1}, so it adds 1 to the count.
Example 4

Same base: add the exponents

Use the Product Law to simplify. Evaluate where possible.

(a) 3^{2}\times3^{4}
3^{2+4}=3^{6}
=\boldsymbol{729}
(b) 6^{3}\times6^{1}
6^{3+1}=6^{4}
=\boldsymbol{1\,296}
(c) m^{2}\times m^{5}
m^{2+5}=\boldsymbol{m^{7}}
Needs a value for m to evaluate.
(d) n^{4}\times n\times n^{2}
n^{4+1+2}=\boldsymbol{n^{7}}
Remember n=n^{1}.
Think Jordan wrote 2^{3}\times2^{4}=4^{7}. What went wrong?
Jordan multiplied the bases too. The base stays 2: 2^{3}\times2^{4}=\boldsymbol{2^{7}}=128, while 4^{7}=16\,384.
5

Applying the Product Law with Coefficients

A coefficient is the number out front: the 3 in 3x^{2}. Handle the numbers and the powers separately:

3x^{2}\times5x^{4}=(3\times5)(x^{2}\times x^{4})=15x^{6}

The coefficientsmultiply, like ordinary numbers: 3\times5=15.
The powerssame base, so the exponents add: x^{2+4}=x^{6}.
!
No number in front? The coefficient is 1: 7b^{2}\times b^{3}=(7\times1)\,b^{2+3}.
Example 5

Numbers multiply, exponents add

Use the Product Law to simplify each expression.

(a) 4x^{2}\times2x^{3}
(4\times2)x^{2+3}
=\boldsymbol{8x^{5}}
(b) 5a^{3}\times3a^{2}
(5\times3)a^{3+2}
=\boldsymbol{15a^{5}}
(c) 2y\times6y^{4}
(2\times6)y^{1+4}
=\boldsymbol{12y^{5}}
(d) 7b^{2}\times b^{3}
(7\times1)b^{2+3}
=\boldsymbol{7b^{5}}

Key ideas

In 3^{4}: the base is 3 (the repeated factor), the exponent is 4 (how many factors), and the power is the whole 3^{4}=3\times3\times3\times3=81.
Order and brackets matter. 2^{5}=32 but 5^{2}=25. (-3)^{2}=9 but -3^{2}=-9. With an odd exponent they agree.
Variable base: substitute the whole value, in brackets. If c=-2, then c^{4}=(-2)^{4}=16.
Product Law: a^{m}\times a^{n}=a^{m+n}, same base only. With coefficients, numbers multiply and exponents add: 3x^{2}\times5x^{4}=15x^{6}.
