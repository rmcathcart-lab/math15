Why Scientific Notation?

Some numbers are awkward to write out. The Earth–Moon distance is about 384 000 000 m, and a human hair is about 0.00006 m wide. It is easy to miscount the zeros and hard to compare numbers like these at a glance. Scientific notation fixes this:

384\,000\,000=3.84\times10^{8}0.00006=6\times10^{-5}

Each place value is a power of 10. Moving one place to the left multiplies by 10; one place to the right multiplies by 10^{-1} (divides by 10).

What Is Scientific Notation?

A number is in scientific notation when it has the form a\times10^{n}, where
  • the coefficient a satisfies 1\le a\lt10: exactly one nonzero digit before its decimal point, and
  • the exponent n is an integer (positive, negative, or zero).
✓ Correct4.2\times10^{5}9\times10^{0}
✗ Not correct52\times10^{3}(52\ge10)0.5\times10^{-2}(0.5\lt1)
3

Standard Notation → Scientific Notation

1Put the decimal point right after the first nonzero digit.
2Count how many places it moved. That count is the exponent.
3Number 10 or larger: the point moves left, and n is positive.

47\,000\rightarrow4.7 is 4 places left, so 47\,000=4.7\times10^{4}.

Example 1

Big numbers

Write each number in scientific notation.

(a) 6\,000
3 places left:
\boldsymbol{6\times10^{3}}
(b) 52\,000
4 places left:
\boldsymbol{5.2\times10^{4}}
(c) 730\,000
5 places left:
\boldsymbol{7.3\times10^{5}}
(d) 8\,400\,000
6 places left:
\boldsymbol{8.4\times10^{6}}
!
Count hops, not zeros. 52\,000 has 3 zeros, but the point hops 4 places (over the 2 as well). Check by going back: 5.2\times10^{3}=5\,200, not 52\,000.

Numbers smaller than 1

For a number smaller than 1 the decimal point moves right instead, which makes the exponent negative: 0.0004\rightarrow4 is 4 places right, so 0.0004=4\times10^{-4}.

Look at the size: 10 or more → positive exponent; less than 1 → negative exponent; from 1 up to 10 → exponent 0.

Scientific Notation → Standard Notation

Reverse the process: move the decimal point of the coefficient |n| places, right if n is positive and left if n is negative, filling in zeros as placeholders. For example, 3.4\times10^{-5} moves 5 places left to give 0.000034.

On a calculator, EE means “× 10 to the”. It is 2ndEE (above x⁻¹). Check the example: 3.42ndEE(−)5ENTER. A calculator showing 3.4E-5 or 3.4 ×10−5 means 3.4\times10^{-5}, never 3.4^{-5}.

Real-world sizes, both ways

Science writes its biggest and smallest measurements this way. Same moves as before: a positive exponent for big numbers, a negative one for numbers less than 1.

Comparing and Ordering

To compare two numbers in scientific notation, compare the exponents first: the smaller exponent gives the smaller number. Only when the exponents are equal do you compare the coefficients.

Think of each power of ten as a shelf. The exponent picks the shelf; the coefficient only says where on that shelf the number sits.

Example 5

Compare and order

Compare and order, justifying with the exponents.

(a) Order from smallest to largest: a bacterium (2\times10^{-6} m), a human hair (7\times10^{-5} m), and a grain of sand (5\times10^{-4} m).
Compare exponents first: -6\lt-5\lt-4, so 2\times10^{-6}\lt7\times10^{-5}\lt5\times10^{-4}: bacterium, then human hair, then grain of sand.
(b) City A has a population of 3.2\times10^{6} people and City B has 8\times10^{5} people. Which city has the greater population? Justify your answer using the exponents.
City A. Its exponent is 6 against City B’s 5, and a larger exponent wins no matter how big the coefficient is: 3.2\times10^{6}=3\,200\,000 while 8\times10^{5}=800\,000.
Math History3rd century BCE · Syracuse

How many grains of sand fill the universe?

Some people said the grains of sand were infinite. Archimedes disagreed. In The Sand Reckoner, a letter to King Gelon, he counted the sand that would fill the whole universe, as people pictured it then.

Greek number words stopped at a myriad (10\,000), so he invented bigger ones. His answer, in today’s notation: fewer than 10^{63} grains, a 1 followed by 63 zeros.

Each of his imagined grains was about 0.00002 m across. Write that in scientific notation. Is it bigger than a red blood cell, 7\times10^{-6} m?
\boldsymbol{2\times10^{-5}} m. Since -5\gt-6, yes: bigger than a blood cell, but smaller than a hair.
✓

Key ideas

Scientific notation is a\times10^{n}: a coefficient with 1\le a\lt10 (one nonzero digit before the point) times a power of ten. 52\times10^{3} and 0.5\times10^{-2} don’t count.
To scientific notation: put the point after the first nonzero digit and count the hops. 10 or more → positive exponent; less than 1 → negative: 0.0004=4\times10^{-4}.
To standard notation: hop the point |n| places, right for positive n, left for negative, filling zeros: 3.4\times10^{-5}=0.000034.
Compare exponents first. The bigger exponent wins, whatever the coefficients: 3.2\times10^{6}\gt8\times10^{5}. Same exponent? Then compare the coefficients.
