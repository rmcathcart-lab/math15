1

The Product Law

Multiplying powers with the same base: keep the base and add the exponents, a^{m}\cdot a^{n}=a^{m+n}. Multiply any coefficients separately. The reason: an exponent counts factors, and multiplying just puts all the factors in one row.

Example 1

The product law

Evaluate or simplify.

(a) Evaluate 6^{2}\times6^{3}.
6^{2+3}=6^{5}=\mathbf{7\,776}
(b) Simplify 5t^{4}\times3t^{2}.
(5\times3)t^{4+2}=\boldsymbol{15t^{6}}
2

Quotient Law and Power of a Power

Quotient law
a^{m}\div a^{n}=a^{m-n}
Dividing powers with the same base: subtract the exponents. (Matching factors cancel.)
Power of a power
(a^{m})^{n}=a^{mn}
A power raised to another power: multiply the exponents. (n copies of m factors.)

Replay the counting with the Example 2 check chips in the explainer above.

Example 2

Quotient law and power of a power

Simplify, using the quotient law, the power of a power law, or both.

(a) k^{11}\div k^{5}
k^{11-5}=\boldsymbol{k^{6}}
(b) (t^{3})^{4}
t^{3\times4}=\boldsymbol{t^{12}}
(c) \dfrac{(g^{2})^{5}}{g^{4}}
\dfrac{g^{10}}{g^{4}}=g^{10-4}=\boldsymbol{g^{6}}
3

Power of a Product and Power of a Quotient

Raise each factor to the outside power: (ab)^{n}=a^{n}b^{n}, and \left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^{n}=\dfrac{a^{n}}{b^{n}}: numerator and denominator both.

Example 3

Every factor gets the power

Simplify each expression.

(a) (4h)^{2}
4^{2}h^{2}=\boldsymbol{16h^{2}}
(b) \left(\dfrac{r}{5}\right)^{3}
\dfrac{r^{3}}{5^{3}}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{r^{3}}{125}}

Combining Exponent Laws

1Brackets first. Clear any bracket with an outside exponent.
2Matching bases. Product or quotient law.
3Coefficients last. Multiply or divide the numbers.
!
The outside power hits the coefficient too. (2t^3)^2=4t^6, not 2t^6.
Example 4

Brackets first, then matching bases

Simplify each expression.

(a) (2t^{3})^{2}\times5t^{4}
(2t^{3})^{2}=4t^{6}
4t^{6}\times5t^{4}=(4\times5)t^{6+4}=\boldsymbol{20t^{10}}
(b) \dfrac{(2w^{3})^{2}}{4w^{2}}
(2w^{3})^{2}=4w^{6}
\dfrac{4w^{6}}{4w^{2}}=w^{6-2}=\boldsymbol{w^{4}}

Zero Exponents

Any nonzero base to the exponent 0 equals 1: a^{0}=1. Check what the 0 is attached to: 8k^{0} raises only k, but (8k)^{0} raises the whole product.

5^3=125÷55^2=25÷55^1=5÷55^0=\mathbf{1}÷55^{-1}=\tfrac15
Example 5

What is the 0 attached to?

Evaluate each pair, and explain why they differ.

(a) 8k^{0} and (8k)^{0}
8k^{0}=8\times1=\mathbf{8}: the exponent is on k only.
(8k)^{0}=\mathbf{1}: the brackets make 8k the base.
(b) -9^{0} and (-9)^{0}
-9^{0}=-(9^{0})=\mathbf{-1}: the minus is not part of the base.
(-9)^{0}=\mathbf{1}: here -9 is the base.
6

Negative Exponents

A negative exponent means “reciprocal, then make the exponent positive”: a^{-n}=\dfrac{1}{a^{n}}. It never makes the value negative, and it never moves a coefficient outside the power.

Example 6

Reciprocal, then positive

Evaluate, or rewrite with a positive exponent.

(a) Evaluate 4^{-2}.
\dfrac{1}{4^{2}}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{1}{16}}
(b) Rewrite 9t^{-3}.
9\times\dfrac{1}{t^{3}}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{9}{t^{3}}}
(c) Evaluate \dfrac{1}{5^{-2}}.
=5^{2}=\mathbf{25}

Combining Zero, Negative, and Positive Exponents

Same checklist as before (brackets, then matching bases), with zero and negative exponents cleaned up last so the final answer has positive exponents only.

Example 7

Positive exponents in the final answer

Simplify. Write each answer with a positive exponent.

(a) \dfrac{15w^{-2}}{3w^{4}}
Coefficients: 15\div3=5.
w^{-2-4}=w^{-6}, so 5w^{-6}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{5}{w^{6}}}
(b) (2t^{-3})^{2}\times t^{5}
(2t^{-3})^{2}=4t^{-6}
4t^{-6}\times t^{5}=4t^{-1}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{4}{t}}
8

Scientific Notation

Scientific notation is a\times10^{n} with 1\le a<10 and n an integer. To multiply, multiply the leading numbers and add the exponents of 10, then move a back into range.

!
Fix the leading number last. Each hop of the decimal point left makes the exponent of 10 one bigger: 24.8\times10^{-4}=2.48\times10^{-3}.
Example 8

Multiplying and dividing in scientific notation

Answer each question, giving results in proper scientific notation where asked.

(a) Evaluate (6.2\times10^{3})\times(4.0\times10^{-7}), and write the answer in proper scientific notation.
6.2\times4.0=24.8, 10^{3+(-7)}=10^{-4}
24.8\times10^{-4} is not proper, so =\boldsymbol{2.48\times10^{-3}}
(b) A grain of pollen has a diameter of about 2.5\times10^{-5} m. About how many grains, laid end to end, would span a length of 0.5 m?
\dfrac{0.5}{2.5\times10^{-5}}=\dfrac{5\times10^{-1}}{2.5\times10^{-5}}=2\times10^{4}
About 20 000 grains.
9

Rational Exponents and Radical Form

A fraction exponent is a root and a power together: a^{m/n}=\sqrt[n]{a^{m}}. The denominator is the index, the numerator is the power. A negative rational exponent still means reciprocal.

!
A negative base under a cube root is fine. (-2)^3=-8, so \sqrt[3]{-8}=-2. (An odd number of negatives multiplies to a negative.)
Example 9

Radical form, and evaluating root first

Answer each question.

(a) Express t^{3/8} in radical form.
Denominator 8 is the index, numerator 3 the power:
\boldsymbol{\sqrt[8]{t^{3}}} (or \left(\sqrt[8]{t}\right)^{3})
(b) If p=16 and q=-8, evaluate p^{3/2}-q^{-2/3}.
16^{3/2}=\left(\sqrt{16}\right)^{3}=4^{3}=64
(-8)^{-2/3}=\dfrac{1}{\left(\sqrt[3]{-8}\right)^{2}}=\dfrac{1}{(-2)^{2}}=\dfrac14
64-\dfrac14=\mathbf{63.75}

Mixed Review: Putting It All Together

Each question below combines more than one skill from the unit: exactly the kind of question that shows up on the unit test. Try each one by hand first, then check it with the Challenge chips in the Section 7 stepper and the Section 8 scientific-notation explainer.

Challenge 1

Every kind of exponent in one expression

Simplify \dfrac{(2t^{3})^{-2}\times8t^{5}}{4t^{-3}}, expressing your answer with a positive exponent.
(2t^{3})^{-2}=2^{-2}t^{-6}=\dfrac{t^{-6}}{4}. Numerator: \dfrac{t^{-6}}{4}\times8t^{5}=2t^{-1}.
Divide: \dfrac{2t^{-1}}{4t^{-3}}=\dfrac12\,t^{-1-(-3)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{t^{2}}{2}}
Challenge 2

Rational exponents and scientific notation

Simplify s^{5/6}\times s^{1/6}. Then, if s=4\,000\,000, express the numeric value of the result in scientific notation.
s^{\frac56+\frac16}=s^{1}=s. So the value is 4\,000\,000=\boldsymbol{4\times10^{6}}.
Challenge 3

A square garden bed

The area of a square garden bed is (3\times10^{2})^{2}\text{ cm}^{2}.

(a) Express the area in proper scientific notation.
(3\times10^{2})^{2}=3^{2}\times10^{2\times2}
=\boldsymbol{9\times10^{4}}\text{ cm}^{2} (proper: 1\le9<10)
(b) The bed is divided into square tiles, each with an area of 9\times10^{2}\text{ cm}^{2}. How many tiles cover the bed?
\dfrac{9\times10^{4}}{9\times10^{2}}=1\times10^{2}
=\mathbf{100} tiles

Key ideas

An exponent counts factors. Multiply: add exponents. Divide: subtract. Power of a power: multiply. A bracket’s power goes to every factor.
Zero and negative exponents are the pattern carried on. a^0=1, a^{-n}=\dfrac{1}{a^n}. Check what the exponent is attached to.
Scientific notation: a\times10^n with 1\le a<10. Numbers with numbers, powers of 10 with powers of 10, then fix a.
Power over root: a^{m/n}=\sqrt[n]{a^m}. Take the root first. Final answers: positive exponents, fractions in lowest terms.
