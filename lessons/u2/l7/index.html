Combining Zero, Negative and Positive Exponents

No new rule today. Every exponent law you know now works together in one expression: brackets, products, quotients, zeros and negatives all at once. One habit matters the whole way through: finish every answer with positive exponents only.

I can…
  • name the seven exponent laws and pick the one each step needs
  • simplify with the three-step checklist: brackets first, combine matching bases, zero and negative exponents last
  • raise a bracket to a negative power by multiplying every exponent inside, signs included
  • flip a fraction that is raised to a negative power
  • write every final answer with positive exponents only
AN3 Demonstrate an understanding of powers with integral and rational exponents.
1

The Complete Set of Exponent Laws

Here are all seven rules in one place. Nothing new is added this lesson: the laws are just used together.

Producta^m\times a^n=a^{m+n}multiplying: add
Quotienta^m\div a^n=a^{m-n}dividing: subtract
Power of a Power(a^m)^n=a^{mn}bracket: multiply
Power of a Product(ab)^n=a^nb^nto every factor
Power of a Quotient\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^n=\dfrac{a^n}{b^n}top and bottom
Zero Exponenta^0=1a\neq0
Negative Exponenta^{-n}=\dfrac{1}{a^n}a\neq0: flip, not negative
One habit matters throughout: finish every answer with positive exponents only. An answer like x^{-4} is not finished yet: write it as \dfrac{1}{x^4}.

Adding and subtracting negative exponents

With the same base, the exponents do all the work. Negative exponents are just numbers on the number line: adding a negative hops left.

Check the warm-up trap. Use the negative key (−), not subtract: 2^(−)3ENTER gives 0.125, and 2ndF◂▸D turns it into 1┘8, which means \tfrac18. Small and positive.
Example 1

Three powers of x, one answer

Simplify 4x^{3}\cdot x^{-7}\cdot x^{0}. Write the answer with positive exponents.

Same base, so add the exponents: x^{3+(-7)+0}=x^{-4}.
4x^{-4}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{4}{x^{4}}}  (the coefficient 4 stays in the numerator)
Think Why is the answer not \dfrac{1}{4x^4}?
The exponent -4 touches only the x. The 4 has no negative exponent, so it stays on top. Only (4x)^{-4} would send the 4 to the bottom too.
2

A Three-Step Checklist

1
Brackets first. A power outside a bracket goes to the coefficient and every variable inside.
2
Combine matching bases. Multiplying: add exponents. Dividing: subtract. Coefficients and each variable separately.
3
Zero and negative exponents last. Replace any leftover a^0 with 1 (a zero exponent can be cleared as soon as you see it), then flip any negative exponents: a^{-n}=\dfrac{1}{a^n}.
Example 2

Dividing with a negative exponent

Simplify 12a^{-2}\div 4a^{5}. Write the answer with positive exponents.

Coefficients: 12\div4=3.   Variables: a^{-2-5}=a^{-7}.
3a^{-7}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3}{a^{7}}}
3

Brackets with Negative Outer Powers

A negative exponent outside a bracket still multiplies through: (5x^{3})^{-2}=5^{-2}x^{-6}, and 5^{-2}=\dfrac{1}{25}. Multiply the outer exponent by each inner exponent, signs included. Save the flip to positive exponents for the very last step.

!
The coefficient gets the power too. In (5x^3)^{-2} the 5 is really 5^1, so it becomes 5^{-2}=\tfrac{1}{25}, not -10 and not 5.

After the brackets: numbers together, each letter on its own

Once the brackets are gone, sort the pieces: the numbers reduce like a fraction, and each letter gets its own exponent sum (top adds, bottom subtracts).

Example 3

Brackets with negative and positive powers

Simplify. Write every final answer with positive exponents.

(a) (3x^{2})^{-2}\times x^{5}
(3x^{2})^{-2}=3^{-2}x^{-4}=\dfrac{x^{-4}}{9}
\dfrac{x^{-4}\times x^{5}}{9}=\dfrac{x^{1}}{9}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x}{9}}
(b) \dfrac{(2a^{3}b)^{2}}{4a^{7}b^{-1}}
Top: (2a^{3}b)^{2}=4a^{6}b^{2}
\dfrac{4a^{6}b^{2}}{4a^{7}b^{-1}}=a^{6-7}b^{2-(-1)}
=a^{-1}b^{3}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{b^{3}}{a}}
(c) (x^{-2}y^{3})^{-3}
Multiply each exponent by -3:
x^{(-2)(-3)}y^{(3)(-3)}=x^{6}y^{-9}
=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x^{6}}{y^{9}}}
Think In (c), the outer exponent is negative. Why does x end up with a positive exponent?
Negative times negative is positive: (-2)(-3)=6. Two flips cancel out, so x^6 stays on top.

Fractions Raised to a Power

Simplify inside the bracket first, before applying the outer power: it keeps the numbers small. A negative outer power on a fraction simply flips it:

\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^{-n}=\left(\dfrac{b}{a}\right)^{n}   so   \left(\dfrac{2}{x}\right)^{-2}=\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)^{2}=\dfrac{x^{2}}{4}
Example 4

Inside first, then the outer power

Simplify. Write the final answer with positive exponents.

(a) \left(\dfrac{2a^{4}}{a^{-2}}\right)^{-2}
Inside first: \dfrac{a^{4}}{a^{-2}}=a^{4-(-2)}=a^{6}, so the bracket is (2a^{6})^{-2}.
2^{-2}a^{-12}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{1}{4a^{12}}}
(b) \dfrac{(3x^{-1}y^{2})^{2}}{9x^{-4}y^{5}}
Top: (3x^{-1}y^{2})^{2}=9x^{-2}y^{4}
\dfrac{9x^{-2}y^{4}}{9x^{-4}y^{5}}=x^{-2-(-4)}y^{4-5}
=x^{2}y^{-1}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x^{2}}{y}}
5

Putting All Three Steps Together

Longer expressions are the same checklist, run once. A zero exponent hiding inside a bracket can be cleared right away (y^0=1, so it disappears). Only after every base is combined do you decide which exponents still need flipping.

Example 5

The whole checklist in one expression

Simplify \dfrac{(2x^{3}y^{0})^{-2}\times 6x^{5}}{3x^{-4}}. Write the answer with positive exponents.

Step 1: y^{0}=1, so the bracket is (2x^{3})^{-2}=2^{-2}x^{-6}=\dfrac{x^{-6}}{4}.
Step 2, numerator: \dfrac{x^{-6}}{4}\times6x^{5}=\dfrac{6x^{-1}}{4}=\dfrac{3x^{-1}}{2}.
Divide: \dfrac{3x^{-1}}{2}\div3x^{-4}=\dfrac{1}{2}\,x^{-1-(-4)}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{x^{3}}{2}}

Not sure? Check it with a number

If two expressions are equal, they give the same value for every x. Put x=2 into the question and into your answer: the values must match.

Key ideas

No new rule. The seven laws work together. Zero and negative exponents are just the pattern continued: a^0=1 and a^{-n}=\dfrac{1}{a^n}.
The checklist: 1. brackets first, 2. combine matching bases (add when multiplying, subtract when dividing), 3. zero and negative exponents last.
Negative outer power: multiply it into every exponent inside, the coefficient too, signs included: (5x^3)^{-2}=5^{-2}x^{-6}=\dfrac{1}{25x^6}.
Finish with positive exponents. Flip only what the exponent touches: 4x^{-4}=\dfrac{4}{x^4}. A fraction to a negative power flips: \left(\dfrac{2}{x}\right)^{-2}=\dfrac{x^2}{4}.
