Review: Integer Operations and Order of Operations

The Grade 9 rules for negative numbers, then BEDMAS. These are the building blocks for everything ahead. The rules are fixed, so everyone who follows them gets the same answer.

I can…
  • add and subtract integers, using a number line or the same-sign / different-sign rules
  • use the sign rules to multiply and divide integers
  • follow BEDMAS to evaluate an expression one step at a time
  • tell (-3)^2 and -3^2 apart
  • write and evaluate an integer expression for a real situation
Grade 9 review Integer operations and the order of operations: the toolkit for the rest of Unit 1.
1

Adding and Subtracting Integers

On a number line, adding a positive moves right. Adding a negative (or subtracting a positive) moves left.

-4+6=2: start at -4, move 6 right.

The two rules (no number line needed)

Same signs: add the numbers and keep the common sign.
-5+(-2)=-7
Different signs: subtract the smaller size (distance from zero) from the larger, and keep the sign of the number farther from zero.
-9+4=-5 (the 9 is bigger, so the answer is negative)
Subtracting is adding the opposite: a-b=a+(-b). So -6-(-10)=-6+10=4.
Example 1

Adding and subtracting

Evaluate each expression.

(a) -6+9
Different signs: 9-6=3, sign of the 9.
=\boldsymbol{3}
(b) -7-5
=-7+(-5)
Same signs: add, keep the -.
=\boldsymbol{-12}
(c) 8+(-3)
Different signs: 8-3=5, sign of the 8.
=\boldsymbol{5}
(d) -10-(-4)
=-10+4
10-4=6, keep the -.
=\boldsymbol{-6}
!
Two signs side by side? Rewrite first. -(-4) becomes +4, and -5 is the same as +(-5). Then use the rules.
2

Multiplying and Dividing Integers

Same signs give a positive answer.
(4)(3)=12 and (-4)(-3)=12
Different signs give a negative answer.
(-4)(3)=-12

Division follows the same rule: 12\div(-3)=-4 and -12\div(-3)=4. Think of each negative sign as flipping the answer once. Two flips bring you back to positive.

Why does a negative times a negative give a positive?

Watch a pattern. Each row multiplies by one less, and the answers change by the same amount every time. Keep the pattern going past zero.

Example 2

Multiplying and dividing

Evaluate each expression.

(a) (-4)(6)
Different signs → negative.
4\times6=24
=\boldsymbol{-24}
(b) (-9)(-3)
Same signs → positive.
9\times3=27
=\boldsymbol{27}
(c) 20\div(-5)
Different signs → negative.
20\div5=4
=\boldsymbol{-4}
(d) (-36)\div(-4)
Same signs → positive.
36\div4=9
=\boldsymbol{9}
3

Order of Operations with Integers

With more than one operation, we can’t just work left to right. Follow BEDMAS:

Brackets
Exponents
Division and Multiplicationtogether, left to right
Addition and Subtractiontogether, left to right

So -4+2\times(-5)=-4+(-10)=-14: multiply first, even though the addition sign is read first.

!
D and M are a team (so are A and S). Do whichever comes first from the left: in 12\div2\times3, divide first: 6\times3=18.
Example 3

One step at a time

Evaluate each expression, showing each step.

(a) 8-2\times(-5)
Multiply first (M before S):
=8-(-10)
=8+10
=\boldsymbol{18}
(b) (-3+7)\times(-2)-1
Brackets first:
=(4)\times(-2)-1
=-8-1
=\boldsymbol{-9}

Exponents with Negative Integers

(-3)^2 and -3^2 are not the same.

Brackets put the negative inside the power.
(-3)^2=(-3)\times(-3)=9
No brackets: the exponent grabs only the 3. The negative is applied afterwards, so the answer stays negative.
-3^2=-(3\times3)=-9
Example 4

Where is the negative?

Evaluate each expression.

(a) -4^2
No brackets: square the 4, then apply the -.
=-(4\times4)=\boldsymbol{-16}
(b) (-4)^2
Brackets: the -4 is squared.
=(-4)(-4)=\boldsymbol{16}
(c) 10-3\times(-2)^2
Exponent, then multiply:
=10-3\times4
=10-12=\boldsymbol{-2}
Think In 4(c), why is it wrong to start with 10-3=7?
That’s going left to right. BEDMAS says the exponent comes first, then the multiplication. The subtraction is last. (Left to right gives 7\times4=28, the wrong answer.)

Putting It All Together

Temperature, elevation and bank balances all use negative numbers naturally. Going up is +, going down is -. Write the expression first, then evaluate it.

Example 5

Use everything

Use everything above. Write the expression first, then evaluate it.

(a) A submarine is at a depth of -45 m. It rises 20 m, then descends 12 m. Write an integer expression for its final depth, then evaluate it.
Rising is +, descending is -:
-45+20-12
=-25-12
=\boldsymbol{-37}
The submarine ends at a depth of -37 m (37 m below the surface).
(b) Evaluate, showing every step in order:
-2\times(5-8)^2+(-6)\div3

Key ideas

Adding and subtracting. Same signs: add, keep the sign. Different signs: subtract, keep the sign of the one farther from zero. Subtracting is adding the opposite: -6-(-10)=-6+10.
Multiplying and dividing. Same signs give a positive, different signs give a negative. Each negative sign flips the answer once.
BEDMAS. Brackets, Exponents, then D and M left to right, then A and S left to right. Popular ≠ correct: the rules decide.
Watch the brackets. (-3)^2=9, but -3^2=-9: without brackets, only the 3 is squared.
