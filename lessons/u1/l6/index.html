Simplifying Square Roots: Entire and Mixed Radicals

\sqrt{50} and 5\sqrt{2} are the same number, written two ways. Today we pull perfect squares out from under the root sign, push numbers back in, and use that to put radicals in order without a calculator.

I can…
  • tell an entire radical like \sqrt{50} from a mixed radical like 5\sqrt{2}
  • convert an entire radical to a mixed radical using the largest perfect square factor
  • use prime factorization when the perfect square is hard to spot
  • convert a mixed radical back to an entire radical
  • order radicals from least to greatest without a calculator
AN2 Demonstrate an understanding of irrational numbers by representing, identifying and simplifying irrational numbers, and by ordering irrational numbers.
Today: simplifying square roots, and ordering them.
1

Entire Radicals and Mixed Radicals

An entire radical has the whole number sitting alone under the root sign.
\sqrt{50}\qquad\sqrt{18}\qquad\sqrt{7}
A mixed radical has a coefficient outside the root, multiplying what is left inside.
5\sqrt{2}\qquad3\sqrt{2}\qquad4\sqrt{6}
Every mixed radical can be rewritten as an entire radical. An entire radical can be rewritten as a mixed radical exactly when its radicand (the number under the root) has a perfect-square factor other than 1.

Converting an Entire Radical to a Mixed Radical

Split the radicand using \sqrt{ab}=\sqrt{a}\times\sqrt{b}. Take the largest perfect-square factor and work out its root outside.
Perfect squares (from the Lesson 5 table; tap to fill)
!
Use the largest perfect square. Pulling out a smaller one is not wrong, but then you must simplify a second time.
Example 1

Entire → mixed

Convert each entire radical to a mixed radical in simplest form. Show the factoring step.

(a) \sqrt{18}
\sqrt{18}=\sqrt{9\times2}
=\sqrt{9}\times\sqrt{2}
=3\sqrt{2}
(b) \sqrt{20}
\sqrt{20}=\sqrt{4\times5}
=\sqrt{4}\times\sqrt{5}
=2\sqrt{5}
(c) \sqrt{45}
\sqrt{45}=\sqrt{9\times5}
=\sqrt{9}\times\sqrt{5}
=3\sqrt{5}
Check (a) on the calculator. 2ndx²18ENTER and 3×2ndx²2ENTER both give 4.242640687.
3

When the Perfect Square Isn’t Obvious

For a large radicand, use the prime factorization from Lesson 3. Write the radicand as a product of primes, then move one copy of every pair of matching primes outside the root. Any prime without a partner stays inside.

For 180=2\times2\times3\times3\times5: the pair of 2s gives a 2 outside, the pair of 3s gives a 3 outside, and the 5 is left in.
\sqrt{180}=2\times3\times\sqrt{5}=6\sqrt{5}
Example 2

Pairs of primes

Use prime factorization to convert each entire radical to a mixed radical in simplest form.

(a) \sqrt{126}
126=2\times3\times3\times7
One pair of 3s comes out; 2 and 7 stay in.
\sqrt{126}=3\sqrt{2\times7}=3\sqrt{14}
Check: 3^2\times14=126 ✓
(b) \sqrt{150}
150=2\times3\times5\times5
The pair of 5s comes out; 2 and 3 stay in.
\sqrt{150}=5\sqrt{2\times3}=5\sqrt{6}
Check: 5^2\times6=150 ✓
(c) \sqrt{192}
192=2\times2\times2\times2\times2\times2\times3
Three pairs of 2s give 2\times2\times2=8 outside.
\sqrt{192}=8\sqrt{3}
Check: 8^2\times3=192 ✓
4

Converting a Mixed Radical to an Entire Radical

Reverse the process: square the coefficient, then multiply it by the radicand already under the root.
!
Square the coefficient. 3\sqrt{2}=\sqrt{9\times2}=\sqrt{18}, not \sqrt{6}. The 3 goes in as 3^2=9.
Example 3

Mixed → entire

Convert each mixed radical to an entire radical.

(a) 4\sqrt{3}
4^2=16
4\sqrt{3}=\sqrt{16\times3}
=\sqrt{48}
(b) 2\sqrt{13}
2^2=4
2\sqrt{13}=\sqrt{4\times13}
=\sqrt{52}
(c) 3\sqrt{7}
3^2=9
3\sqrt{7}=\sqrt{9\times7}
=\sqrt{63}
(d) 9\sqrt{2}
9^2=81
9\sqrt{2}=\sqrt{81\times2}
=\sqrt{162}

Comparing and Ordering Radicals

A mixed radical hides its size behind two numbers, so it is hard to compare at a glance. Convert every radical to entire form, then compare the radicands: the bigger the number under the root, the bigger the root.
!
Don’t rank by the number in front. 2\sqrt{14} has a smaller coefficient than 3\sqrt{5}, but 2\sqrt{14}=\sqrt{56} is bigger than 3\sqrt{5}=\sqrt{45}.
Example 4

Ordering without a calculator

Without a calculator, arrange the following in order from least to greatest:   6\sqrt{2},\quad3\sqrt{5},\quad\sqrt{70},\quad2\sqrt{14}

Write each one as an entire radical: 6\sqrt{2}=\sqrt{36\times2}=\sqrt{72},  3\sqrt{5}=\sqrt{9\times5}=\sqrt{45},  \sqrt{70},  2\sqrt{14}=\sqrt{4\times14}=\sqrt{56}.
Order the radicands: 45<56<70<72.
3\sqrt{5}<2\sqrt{14}<\sqrt{70}<6\sqrt{2}
6

Putting It All Together

Finished? A mixed radical is only finished when the number left inside has no perfect-square factor left.
Big radicand? It may need prime factorization to find all the pairs.
Example 5

Is it finished?

(a) Sanjay simplified \sqrt{96} this way: \sqrt{96}=\sqrt{4\times24}=2\sqrt{24}. Explain why this is not fully simplified, then finish simplifying it correctly.
The number left inside, 24, still has the perfect-square factor 4 (24=4\times6), so the job isn’t finished.
2\sqrt{24}=2\sqrt{4\times6}=2\times2\sqrt{6}= 4\sqrt{6}
The largest perfect square, 16, does it in one step: \sqrt{96}=\sqrt{16\times6}=4\sqrt{6}.
(b) Use prime factorization to simplify \sqrt{168}.
168=2\times2\times2\times3\times7
One pair of 2s comes out as a 2; the spare 2, the 3 and the 7 stay in.
\sqrt{168}=2\sqrt{2\times3\times7}=2\sqrt{42}
Check: 2^2\times42=168, and 42 has no perfect-square factor ✓

Tilted Squares

Find the area of A and of B. How long is each side?
A: area 4, side 2.  B: area 9, side 3. (Side × side = area, so side = \sqrt{\text{area}}.)

With a partner: can you draw a tilted square with area 5? With area 10?
Area 5: 1 over, 2 up (box 9, minus 4 corners of 1). Area 10: 1 over, 3 up (box 16, minus 4 corners of 1.5).
2
Find the area of tilted square C. Hint: dashed box minus 4 corners.
Box 2\times2=4, minus 4 half-squares: 4-2=2. So C’s side is \sqrt{2}\approx1.41.
3
How many copies of C fit inside D? So D’s area = ? D’s side = how many C-sides?
4 copies, so D’s area is 4\times2=8. D’s side is 2 C-sides: \sqrt{8}=2\sqrt{2}.
!
The big one. A tilted square that goes 5 over, 5 up has area 50, so its side is \sqrt{50} units. Which is the longest? Guess first.
A \sqrt{50}
B 5\sqrt{2}
C 7
D a tie!
D: A and B tie (both ≈ 7.07). The side is 5 C-sides, so it is 5\sqrt{2}, and it is also \sqrt{50}. And 7=\sqrt{49} is just barely shorter.
✓

Key ideas

Entire \sqrt{50} and mixed 5\sqrt{2} are the same number. 5\sqrt{2} means five \sqrt{2}’s.
Entire → mixed: pull out the largest perfect square: \sqrt{48}=\sqrt{16}\times\sqrt{3}=4\sqrt{3}. Big radicand? Pair up the primes.
Mixed → entire: square the coefficient and bring it in: 2\sqrt{5}=\sqrt{4\times5}=\sqrt{20}.
Order radicals by writing them all as entire radicals: bigger radicand, bigger root. Finished means nothing inside has a perfect-square factor.
