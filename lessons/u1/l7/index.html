1

The Index of a Radical

In \sqrt[n]{a} the small number n is the index: it says which root is being taken. A square root simply hides its index of 2.

An entire radical has a single number sitting alone under the root sign.
\sqrt[3]{24}
A mixed radical has a coefficient multiplying it from outside.
2\sqrt[3]{3}

The Lesson 6 vocabulary carries straight over: only the index is new.

Example 1

Entire or mixed?

State whether each radical is an entire radical or a mixed radical.

(a) \sqrt[3]{20}
One number sits alone under the root sign: entire
(b) 4\sqrt[3]{3}
Coefficient 4 multiplies from outside: mixed
(c) \sqrt[3]{9}
Nothing outside the root sign: entire
(d) -5\sqrt[3]{2}
Coefficient -5 sits outside: mixed
2

Converting an Entire Cube Radical to a Mixed Radical

Lesson 6 pulled out the largest perfect square. With index n you pull out the largest perfect nth power, then split the root with

\sqrt[n]{ab}=\sqrt[n]{a}\times\sqrt[n]{b}
\sqrt[3]{88}=\sqrt[3]{8\times11}=\sqrt[3]{8}\times\sqrt[3]{11}=2\sqrt[3]{11}, and 8 was chosen because no larger perfect cube divides 88.

Keep these handy

Perfect cubes, fourth powers and fifth powers. Tap a row to highlight it.

!
Use the largest perfect cube. \sqrt[3]{192}=\sqrt[3]{8\times24}=2\sqrt[3]{24} is true, but not simplest form: 24=8\times3 still hides a perfect cube. Start with 64 instead: \sqrt[3]{192}=\sqrt[3]{64\times3}=4\sqrt[3]{3}.

Another way: prime factors in groups of three

In Lesson 6 a pair of equal prime factors came out of a square root as one number. For a cube root, it takes a group of three equal prime factors to bring one out. Leftovers stay inside. Handy when the perfect cube is hard to spot.

Example 2

Entire → mixed

Convert each radical to a mixed radical in simplest form. Show the factoring step.

(a) \sqrt[3]{40}
\sqrt[3]{40}=\sqrt[3]{8\times5}
=\sqrt[3]{8}\times\sqrt[3]{5}
=\boldsymbol{2\sqrt[3]{5}}
8 is the largest cube dividing 40.
(b) \sqrt[3]{162}
\sqrt[3]{162}=\sqrt[3]{27\times6}
=\sqrt[3]{27}\times\sqrt[3]{6}
=\boldsymbol{3\sqrt[3]{6}}
(c) \sqrt[3]{500}
\sqrt[3]{500}=\sqrt[3]{125\times4}
=\sqrt[3]{125}\times\sqrt[3]{4}
=\boldsymbol{5\sqrt[3]{4}}
3

Converting a Mixed Cube Radical to an Entire Radical

To reverse the process, raise the outside coefficient to the power of the index, then multiply it into the radicand.
3\sqrt[3]{7}=\sqrt[3]{3^3}\times\sqrt[3]{7}=\sqrt[3]{27\times7}=\sqrt[3]{189}

Because the index 3 is odd, a negative coefficient may be cubed and absorbed as well, unlike a square root, where a negative coefficient must stay outside.

!
Cube it, don’t triple it. 2\sqrt[3]{11}=\sqrt[3]{2^3\times11}=\sqrt[3]{88}. Writing \sqrt[3]{6\times11} uses 2\times3 instead of 2^3=2\times2\times2.
Example 3

Mixed → entire

Convert each mixed radical to an entire radical. Show the step where you raise the coefficient.

(a) 2\sqrt[3]{9}
2\sqrt[3]{9}=\sqrt[3]{2^3}\times\sqrt[3]{9}
=\sqrt[3]{8\times9}
=\boldsymbol{\sqrt[3]{72}}
(b) 4\sqrt[3]{2}
4\sqrt[3]{2}=\sqrt[3]{4^3}\times\sqrt[3]{2}
=\sqrt[3]{64\times2}
=\boldsymbol{\sqrt[3]{128}}
(c) -2\sqrt[3]{6}
-2\sqrt[3]{6}=\sqrt[3]{(-2)^3}\times\sqrt[3]{6}
=\sqrt[3]{-8\times6}
=\boldsymbol{\sqrt[3]{-48}}
Index 3 is odd, so the − goes inside.

Odd and Even Indices: the Trap

An odd index accepts a negative radicand, since an odd power of a negative number stays negative.
\sqrt[3]{-8}=-2, and \sqrt[3]{-24}=\sqrt[3]{-8\times3}=-2\sqrt[3]{3}
An even index does not: no real number raised to an even power is negative.
\sqrt{-4} and \sqrt[4]{-16} are not real numbers.

Check the index before you touch a negative radicand.

5

Any Index at All

Nothing about the method changes as n grows. Only the list you hunt through changes:

\sqrt[4]{32}=\sqrt[4]{16\times2}=2\sqrt[4]{2} 16 is a perfect fourth power

\sqrt[5]{96}=\sqrt[5]{32\times3}=2\sqrt[5]{3} 32 is a perfect fifth power

Going the other way, raise the coefficient to the power of n, not always 3.

Same number, different index

The index is the group size: a fourth root needs four equal prime factors to bring one out, a fifth root needs five. Bigger groups means less comes out.

Example 4

Both directions

Convert each radical to the requested form, in simplest form.

(a) \sqrt[3]{320} (mixed)
\sqrt[3]{320}=\sqrt[3]{64\times5}
=\sqrt[3]{64}\times\sqrt[3]{5}
=\boldsymbol{4\sqrt[3]{5}}
(b) 3\sqrt[3]{13} (entire)
3\sqrt[3]{13}=\sqrt[3]{3^3}\times\sqrt[3]{13}
=\sqrt[3]{27\times13}
=\boldsymbol{\sqrt[3]{351}}
(c) \sqrt[4]{243} (mixed)
\sqrt[4]{243}=\sqrt[4]{81\times3}
=\sqrt[4]{81}\times\sqrt[4]{3}
=\boldsymbol{3\sqrt[4]{3}}
(d) -3\sqrt[3]{10} (entire)
-3\sqrt[3]{10}=\sqrt[3]{(-3)^3}\times\sqrt[3]{10}
=\sqrt[3]{-27\times10}
=\boldsymbol{\sqrt[3]{-270}}

The Giant Die

A giant novelty die is just a normal die, scaled up. How much bigger is it really? Tap a die.

1× as tall1 normal die

“Holds” = how many normal dice would fill it up (same volume).

1
A die 2× as tall holds how many normal dice?
8 dice: 2 wide × 2 deep × 2 high, 2^3=8.
2
A die 3× as tall holds how many?
27 dice: 3 wide × 3 deep × 3 high, 3^3=27.
3
A die holds 64 normal dice. How many times as tall is it?
4× as tall, because 4\times4\times4=64. Going backwards from “holds” to “tall” is the cube root: \sqrt[3]{64}=4.
!
The big one. A toy store sells a giant die that holds exactly 16 normal dice. How many times as tall as a normal die is it? Guess first.
A 8
B 4
C about 2.5
D 2
C: about 2.5 times as tall. We need a number that cubes to 16: that’s \sqrt[3]{16}. 2^3=8 is too small and 3^3=27 is too big, so it’s between 2 and 3: \sqrt[3]{16}\approx2.52.
A doubles the 8-dice die: that holds 4^3=64. B is square-root thinking (4\times4=16): right for the area of one face, not the volume.
Today: \sqrt[3]{16} has a small 3 on it, the index, and we’ll learn to write cube roots like it in simplest form.

Talk it over: is the giant die more than 2× as tall? Less than 3×? How do you know?

✓

Key ideas

The index is the small number in \sqrt[n]{a}. A square root hides its index of 2. Entire: one number alone under the root. Mixed: a coefficient outside.
Entire → mixed: pull out the largest perfect nth power. \sqrt[3]{88}=\sqrt[3]{8\times11}=2\sqrt[3]{11}
Mixed → entire: raise the coefficient to the power n and multiply it in. 3\sqrt[3]{7}=\sqrt[3]{27\times7}=\sqrt[3]{189}
Odd vs. even: an odd index takes a negative radicand (\sqrt[3]{-8}=-2). An even index can’t: \sqrt[4]{-16} is not real.
