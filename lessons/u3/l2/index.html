Multiplying a Monomial by a Polynomial

Split a rectangle into pieces and its area splits into the pieces’ areas. That one picture explains how to multiply one term by a whole bracket, from 7(x+3) to -2x(3x^{2}+x-6). Today: multiplying monomials, the distributive property, and keeping every sign.

I can…
  • multiply two monomials: multiply the coefficients and add the exponents
  • use the distributive property to multiply a monomial by a binomial or a trinomial
  • draw an area model where each piece is one product
  • expand first, then collect like terms
  • distribute a negative monomial, changing the sign of every term it reaches
AN4 Demonstrate an understanding of the multiplication of polynomial expressions (limited to monomials, binomials and trinomials), concretely, pictorially and symbolically.
1

Multiplying Two Monomials

Distributing is just repeated monomial-by-monomial multiplication, so start there. Multiply the coefficients and add the exponents (the Product Law from Unit 2):

2x^{2}\times4x^{3}=(2\times4)(x^{2}\times x^{3})=8x^{5}

!
Don’t add the coefficients, and don’t multiply the exponents. Treat signs as normal: negative times negative is positive.
Example 1

Multiplying monomials

Multiply each pair of monomials.

(a) 2x^{3}\times5x^{2}
(2\times5)x^{3+2}
=\boldsymbol{10x^{5}}
(b) -3x\times4x^{4}
(-3\times4)x^{1+4}
=\boldsymbol{-12x^{5}}
(c) 6x^{2}\times x
(6\times1)x^{2+1}
=\boldsymbol{6x^{3}}
(d) -2x^{3}\times(-5x^{4})
(-2)(-5)x^{3+4}
=\boldsymbol{10x^{7}} (negative × negative)

Multiplying a Monomial by a Binomial

The distributive property: multiply the outside term by each term inside, then add. That is exactly how the warm-up worked: 8 × 2 000, take away 8 × 3.

Distributive property
a(b+c)=ab+ac
Example
3x(x+4)=3x^{2}+12x
Signs travel with their term
-5(x-2)=-5x+10

Signs travel with their term

Draw an arrow from the outside term to each term inside. Each arrow is one product, and the sign in front of a term goes along with it: -5(x-2)=(-5)(x)+(-5)(-2)=-5x+10, not -5x-10.

Example 2

Monomial times binomial

Use the distributive property to expand each product.

(a) 4(x+7)
4\times x+4\times7
=\boldsymbol{4x+28}
(b) -3(x-5)
(-3)(x)+(-3)(-5)
=\boldsymbol{-3x+15}
(c) 2x(x+6)
2x\times x+2x\times6
=\boldsymbol{2x^{2}+12x}
(d) -5x(x-3)
(-5x)(x)+(-5x)(-3)
=\boldsymbol{-5x^{2}+15x}
3

Multiplying a Monomial by a Trinomial

Nothing changes with three terms: distribute to all of them, one at a time. 2x(x^{2}+3x-4)=2x^{3}+6x^{2}-8x.

Count your products. Your answer should have exactly as many products as there were terms in the bracket, so a trinomial always gives three terms. Arrows (or a grid) from the monomial to each term help you avoid skipping one.
Example 3

Monomial times trinomial

Use the distributive property to expand each product.

(a) 3(x^{2}+2x-1)
=\boldsymbol{3x^{2}+6x-3}  Three terms in, three out.
(b) -4(2x^{2}-x+5)
=\boldsymbol{-8x^{2}+4x-20}  Every sign flips.
(c) 5x(x^{2}-3x+2)
=\boldsymbol{5x^{3}-15x^{2}+10x}  Add 1 to each exponent.
(d) -2x(3x^{2}+x-6)
=\boldsymbol{-6x^{3}-2x^{2}+12x}

Simplifying After Distributing

When extra terms sit outside the brackets, distribute first and then collect like terms:

2x(x+4)+3x=2x^{2}+8x+3x=2x^{2}+11x

Only terms with the identical variable part combine, so 8x and 3x join but 2x^{2} stands alone.

Example 4

Expand and simplify

Expand and simplify.

(a) 3x(x+2)+4x
3x^{2}+6x+4x
=\boldsymbol{3x^{2}+10x}
(b) 5(x-3)-2x
5x-15-2x
=\boldsymbol{3x-15}
(c) 4x(x-1)+3(x+2)
4x^{2}-4x+3x+6
=\boldsymbol{4x^{2}-x+6}
(d) 2x(3x+1)-5x
6x^{2}+2x-5x
=\boldsymbol{6x^{2}-3x}

Combining Distributions with a Negative Monomial

The hardest expressions have two sets of brackets with at least one negative multiplier. Work one bracket at a time: write the first expansion out fully before you look at the second, then add the two results and collect like terms.

!
A negative monomial changes the sign of every term it reaches. This is the single most common place marks are lost: -3(x-5) is -3x+15, so the -5 becomes +15.
Example 5

Two brackets, a negative monomial

Expand and simplify.

(a) -4x(2x-3)-3(x-5)
First bracket: -4x(2x-3)=-8x^{2}+12x
Second: -3(x-5)=-3x+15
-8x^{2}+12x-3x+15=\boldsymbol{-8x^{2}+9x+15}
(b) -x(x+8)-2x(3x-1)
First: -x(x+8)=-x^{2}-8x
Second: -2x(3x-1)=-6x^{2}+2x
-x^{2}-8x-6x^{2}+2x=\boldsymbol{-7x^{2}-6x}
Think In (b), why is the second bracket -6x^{2}+2x and not -6x^{2}-2x?
The -2x multiplies the -1 too: (-2x)(-1)=+2x. Negative × negative is positive.

Beat the Calculator

Big multiplications get easy if you split the rectangle into friendly pieces. No calculators!

1
Add the two pieces: 7\times103=\ ?
721: 7\times100=700 and 7\times3=21, so 700+21=721.
2
Take the cut-off piece away from 6\times100: 6\times98=\ ?
588: 6\times100=600, the cut-off piece is 6\times2=12, so 600-12=588.
3
Cover the 100 and call it x. What is each piece’s area now?
7x and 21. The big piece is 7 by x; the small piece did not change at all.
!
The big one. Beat a calculator: work out 8 × 1 997 in your head. (Hint: 1 997 is almost a very friendly number.) Guess first.
A 16 024
B 15 976
C 15 997
B: 15 976. Think 1\,997=2\,000-3: 8\times2\,000=16\,000, cut off 8\times3=24, so 16\,000-24=15\,976.
A adds the 24 (a lost sign). C takes away just 3 (the 8 never multiplied the 3).

Key ideas

Monomial × monomial: multiply the coefficients, add the exponents. -3x\times4x^{4}=-12x^{5}.
Distributive property: a(b+c)=ab+ac. The outside term multiplies each term inside; a trinomial gives three products.
Signs travel with their term. A negative monomial changes the sign of every term it reaches: -5(x-2)=-5x+10.
Distribute first, then collect like terms. With two brackets, do one bracket at a time.
