Like Terms, and Adding/Subtracting Polynomials

A polynomial is built from terms, like a fruit basket is built from fruit. Today we name every piece of a polynomial, sort terms into families of like terms, and use that one idea to collect, add and subtract polynomials.

I can…
  • list the terms of a polynomial, with their signs, and state each coefficient and the constant term
  • find the degree of a term and of a polynomial, and name it a monomial, binomial, trinomial or polynomial
  • spot like terms: the same variable(s) with the same exponent(s)
  • collect like terms by adding their coefficients
  • add polynomials, and subtract them by adding the opposite (flip every sign in the second bracket)
AN4 Demonstrate an understanding of the multiplication of polynomial expressions (limited to monomials, binomials and trinomials), concretely, pictorially and symbolically.
1

Polynomial Vocabulary

A term is a part of an expression separated from the others by + or -. Each term keeps the sign in front of it, so 3x^{2}-7x+5 has the terms 3x^{2}, -7x and 5.

Coefficient — the number multiplying the variable: -7 in -7x. An unwritten coefficient is 1 (or -1 for -x).
A term with no variable is a constant: the 5.
Degree of a term — the sum of its exponents.
4x^{3} has degree 3 · 6xy has degree 2 · 9 has degree 0
Degree of a polynomial — its largest term degree.

Naming a polynomial by its number of terms

Monomial1 term
5y
Binomial2 terms
y-6
Trinomial3 terms
y^2+y-6
Polynomial4 or more terms
x^3-2x^2+x-5

Mono = one, bi = two, tri = three, poly = many: like a monorail, a bicycle and a tricycle.

Example 1

Every piece of a polynomial

Consider the polynomial 4x^{3}-5x^{2}+2x-9.

(a) List each term.
4x^{3},\ \ -5x^{2},\ \ 2x,\ \ -9
Each term carries the sign in front of it.
(b) State the coefficient of each variable term.
4,\ \ -5,\ \ 2 (-9 is the constant term).
(c) Identify the constant term.
-9: the only term with no variable.
(d) State the degree of each term, and the degree of the whole polynomial.
Degrees 3, 2, 1, 0 in order.
The polynomial’s degree is the largest: 3.
(e) Is this a monomial, binomial, trinomial, or simply a “polynomial”? Explain.
It has 4 terms. Monomial, binomial and trinomial name 1, 2 and 3 terms, so with four terms it is simply called a polynomial.
2

Identifying Like Terms

Like terms — exactly the same variable(s) raised to exactly the same exponent(s). Only the coefficients may differ.
2x and 6x · 3x^{2} and -8x^{2} · 3xy and -7xy
Unlike terms — a different variable or a different exponent.
4x and 4x^{2} (different exponents) · 5x and 5y (different variables)
!
The coefficient never decides a match. 4x and 4x^{2} share a 4 but are not like terms; 7x and -x look different but are. Look only at the letters and their exponents.
Example 2

Sorting terms into like-term groups

Sort these terms into groups of like terms (some groups may end up with only one term):

-x7x5x^{2}94y2x^{2}-3y-6x
x-terms:
-x,\ \ 7x,\ \ x
(exponent 1 on x)
x^{2}-terms:
5x^{2},\ \ 2x^{2}
Not like the x-terms: the exponents differ.
y-terms:
4y,\ \ -3y
Constant terms:
9,\ \ -6
No variable at all.
3

Collecting (Combining) Like Terms

To collect like terms, add or subtract their coefficients and keep the variable part unchanged: 4x+7x=(4+7)x=11x. Unlike terms cannot be combined: leave them alone.

With longer expressions, group the like terms first, then combine each group:

3x^{2}+5x-2x^{2}+x=(3x^{2}-2x^{2})+(5x+x)=x^{2}+6x

!
x^{2}+6x is already simplified. It is not 7x^{2} or 7x^{3}: an x^{2}-term and an x-term are unlike.
!
Keep each term’s sign when you group: in 5y^{2}-3y+2y^{2}+8y the y-group is (-3y+8y).
Example 3

Collecting like terms

Simplify each expression by collecting like terms.

(a) 6x+4-2x+9
(6x-2x)+(4+9)
=\boldsymbol{4x+13}
(b) 5y^{2}-3y+2y^{2}+8y-1
(5y^{2}+2y^{2})+(-3y+8y)-1
=\boldsymbol{7y^{2}+5y-1}
(c) 7a-3b+2a+5b-4
(7a+2a)+(-3b+5b)-4
=\boldsymbol{9a+2b-4} (a and b stay separate)
(d) 9x^{2}-4x+3-5x^{2}+6x-7
(9x^{2}-5x^{2})+(-4x+6x)+(3-7)
=\boldsymbol{4x^{2}+2x-4}
Think Can 9a+2b-4 be simplified any further? Why or why not?
No. 9a, 2b and -4 are all in different families (different letters, or no letter), so no two of them are like terms.

Adding Polynomials

Horizontal method — adding never changes a sign, so drop the brackets and collect like terms.
(3x^{2}+5x-2)+(x^{2}-4x+6)
=3x^{2}+5x-2+x^{2}-4x+6=4x^{2}+x+4
Vertical method — line the polynomials up so like terms sit in the same column, then add straight down each column.

See it with algebra tiles

Each tile’s area is the term it stands for. The shape tells you which power of x; the shading tells you the sign. A zero pair (one shaded and one unshaded tile of the same shape) is worth 0, so it can be taken away.

Try it: the tile mat

Add tiles with the buttons, tap a tile to take it away. Shaded tiles sit above the line and unshaded tiles below, so zero pairs line up.

The vertical method

Stack the polynomials so like terms are in the same column. Each column is one family of like terms.

Example 4

Adding polynomials

Add each pair of polynomials.

(a) (2x^{2}+3x-5)+(4x^{2}-x+7)
Drop the brackets: 2x^{2}+3x-5+4x^{2}-x+7
=(2x^{2}+4x^{2})+(3x-x)+(-5+7)
=\boldsymbol{6x^{2}+2x+2}
(b) (5a-3b+2)+(-2a+6b-9)
5a-3b+2-2a+6b-9
=(5a-2a)+(-3b+6b)+(2-9)
=\boldsymbol{3a+3b-7}
(c) Add using the vertical method:  \begin{array}{r} 4x^{2}-2x+6 \\ +\ (3x^{2}+5x-1) \\ \hline \end{array}
Add straight down each column: 4x^{2}+3x^{2}=7x^{2}; -2x+5x=3x; 6-1=5.
=\boldsymbol{7x^{2}+3x+5}
5

Subtracting Polynomials

Subtraction means subtracting every term of the second polynomial, so rewrite it as adding the opposite: change the sign of each term in the second bracket, then add.

Step 1 — flip every sign in the second bracket.
-(2x^{2}-6x+7)=-2x^{2}+6x-7
Step 2 — collect like terms.
5x^{2}+3x-4-2x^{2}+6x-7=3x^{2}+9x-11
!
The most common error is flipping only the first term’s sign: 5x^{2}+3x-4-2x^{2}-6x+7=3x^{2}-3x+3 is wrong, because all three terms in the bracket must change sign.

Subtracting with tiles: flip every tile

The opposite of a polynomial has every sign changed. In tiles, every tile flips: shaded ↔ unshaded. Then add as usual.

Subtracting with the vertical method

Line up the columns, change every sign in the bottom row, then add down each column.

Example 5

Subtracting polynomials

Subtract, distributing the negative sign to every term of the second polynomial.

(a) (6x^{2}+4x-3)-(2x^{2}+7x-5)
Flip all three signs: -2x^{2}-7x+5
6x^{2}+4x-3-2x^{2}-7x+5
=\boldsymbol{4x^{2}-3x+2}
(b) (4y^{2}-5y+2)-(-3y^{2}+2y-6)
Flip all three signs: +3y^{2}-2y+6
4y^{2}-5y+2+3y^{2}-2y+6
=\boldsymbol{7y^{2}-7y+8}
(c) Subtract using the vertical method:  \begin{array}{r} 7x^{2}-3x+5 \\ -\ (3x^{2}+2x-8) \\ \hline \end{array}
Change every sign in the bottom row, then add: -3x^{2}-2x+8.
7x^{2}-3x^{2}=4x^{2}; -3x-2x=-5x; 5+8=13.
=\boldsymbol{4x^{2}-5x+13}
Think Why can you just drop the brackets when adding, but not when subtracting?
A + in front of a bracket keeps every sign the same. A - in front of a bracket takes away every term inside, so every sign changes.
Extra

The Fruit Puzzle

Each fruit is worth a number. Same fruit, same number. Look closely!

1
What is one apple worth?
Apple =10: three apples make 30.
2
What is one orange worth?
Orange =6. Row 2: 10+bunch+bunch=26, so a bunch of 4 bananas is 8. Row 3: 8+12=20, so two oranges are 12.
3
In the jumbled last line, how many apples? How many oranges? Does the order matter?
3 apples and 2 oranges. Order doesn’t matter: slide the same fruit together before you add.
!
The big one. What is the whole last line worth? It’s trickier than it looks! Guess first.
A 38
B 48
C 50
B: 48. The trap: the last bunch has only 3 bananas. A bunch of 4 is 8, so one banana is 2 and this bunch is 6.
30+12+6=48.
C missed the missing banana (used 8). A missed one of the scattered apples. Both are “counting like things” mistakes: exactly today’s lesson.

Compare with a neighbour: same answer? Then write your own fruit puzzle to stump them.

✓

Key ideas

Terms are split by + and -, and keep their signs. The coefficient is the number in front; a term with no variable is the constant. 1, 2, 3 terms: monomial, binomial, trinomial; 4 or more: polynomial.
A term’s degree is the sum of its exponents (6xy: 2). A polynomial’s degree is its largest term degree.
Like terms have the same variables with the same exponents. To collect them, add the coefficients and keep the variable part: 4x+7x=11x.
Adding: drop the brackets and collect. Subtracting: add the opposite: flip every sign in the second bracket, then collect.
