Like Terms, and Adding/Subtracting Polynomials

Like terms have identical variables with identical exponents. They combine by adding their coefficients.
8x^2 and -2x^2 are like terms · 8x^2 and 8x are not
To add, drop the brackets and collect. To subtract, distribute the negative to every term first (add the opposite).
-(3x^2-5x+4)=-3x^2+5x-4
x^2x1-1 (unshaded)a shaded and an unshaded tile of the same kind make a zero pair

Sorting like terms into buckets

Without tiles: deal with any brackets first, then sort the terms into buckets by variable and exponent, and add each bucket.

!
The minus must reach every term. -(3x^2-5x+4) is -3x^2+5x-4, not -3x^2-5x+4.
Example 1

Collect, and subtract

Simplify each expression.

(a) Collect like terms: 8x^{2}-3x+5-2x^{2}+9x-11
(8x^{2}-2x^{2})+(-3x+9x)+(5-11)
=\boldsymbol{6x^{2}+6x-6}
(b) Subtract: (7x^{2}+2x-6)-(3x^{2}-5x+4)
Flip all three signs: -3x^{2}+5x-4
7x^{2}+2x-6-3x^{2}+5x-4=\boldsymbol{4x^{2}+7x-10}

Multiplying a Monomial by a Polynomial

Distribute the monomial to every term inside the brackets: multiply the coefficients, add the exponents on matching bases. A negative monomial flips the sign of every term it touches.

Example 2

Distribute to every term

Expand and simplify.

(a) -4x\left(3x^{2}-2x+5\right)
(-4x)(3x^{2})+(-4x)(-2x)+(-4x)(5)
=\boldsymbol{-12x^{3}+8x^{2}-20x}
(b) 3x(x+4)-2x(x-6)
3x^{2}+12x-2x^{2}+12x
=\boldsymbol{x^{2}+24x}

Use FOIL: First, Outer, Inner, Last. Then collect the like terms, which are almost always the Outer and Inner products. FOIL is just the area model: four boxes, one for each product.

Special Products

Two binomial products follow shortcut patterns: the same two terms once added and once subtracted, or the same binomial squared.

With a number in front, as in 3(x-1)^{2}, square first, then multiply: 3(x^{2}-2x+1)=3x^{2}-6x+3.

Difference of squares
(a+b)(a-b)=a^{2}-b^{2}
the middle terms cancel: two terms left
Perfect square (sum)
(a+b)^{2}=a^{2}+2ab+b^{2}
middle term = 2 × first × last
Perfect square (difference)
(a-b)^{2}=a^{2}-2ab+b^{2}
the middle term is negative; b^2 is still positive

Why the middle term is 2ab

A binomial squared is the area of a square: a big square, a small square, and two rectangles. Lose the rectangles and you lose the middle term.

!
A classic slip. (a+b)^2\neq a^2+b^2: the two rectangles are the middle term.
Example 4

Use the pattern

Use the appropriate pattern to expand.

(a) Difference of squares: (4x+3)(4x-3)
a=4x, b=3:
(4x)^{2}-3^{2}=\boldsymbol{16x^{2}-9} (no middle term)
(b) Perfect square: (5x-2)^{2}
(5x)^{2}-2(5x)(2)+2^{2}
=\boldsymbol{25x^{2}-20x+4}

Multiplying a Binomial by a Trinomial

Distribute each term of the binomial across all three terms of the trinomial: six products in total. Then collect like terms. On a grid, like terms line up on the diagonals.

Example 5

Six products, then collect

Expand and simplify.

(a) (2x+3)\left(x^{2}-4x+5\right)
2x^{3}-8x^{2}+10x+3x^{2}-12x+15
x^{2}: -8x^{2}+3x^{2}=-5x^{2}; x: 10x-12x=-2x
=\boldsymbol{2x^{3}-5x^{2}-2x+15}
(b) (x-2)\left(3x^{2}+x-6\right)
3x^{3}+x^{2}-6x-6x^{2}-2x+12
x^{2}: x^{2}-6x^{2}=-5x^{2}; x: -6x-2x=-8x
=\boldsymbol{3x^{3}-5x^{2}-8x+12}

Applications: Area and Volume

Rectangle: area = length \times width.
square units: \text{m}^2, \text{cm}^2
Rectangular prism: volume = length \times width \times height.
cubic units: \text{cm}^3

Set up the product first, then expand and simplify. The simplified polynomial is the area (or the volume).

Area left = whole − cut-out; keep the cut-out in brackets: a (2x+5) square sheet with an x by x square cut out leaves (2x+5)^{2}-x^{2}=(4x^{2}+20x+25)-x^{2}=3x^{2}+20x+25.
Equal areas: expand both, set them equal, the x^{2} terms cancel, solve:
(x+3)^{2}=(x+1)(x+6) gives x^{2}+6x+9=x^{2}+7x+6, so x=3.
Example 6

Area and volume expressions

Write a simplified expression for each measurement.

(a) A rectangular garden has length (x+7) m and width (x+2) m. Find its area.
A=(x+7)(x+2)=x^{2}+2x+7x+14
=\boldsymbol{x^{2}+9x+14}\text{ m}^{2}
(b) A box has a square base of side length x cm and height (x+4) cm. Find its volume.
V=x\cdot x\cdot(x+4)=x^{2}(x+4)
=\boldsymbol{x^{3}+4x^{2}}\text{ cm}^{3}

Mixed Review: Putting It All Together

Each challenge below combines more than one skill from the unit: exactly the kind of question a unit test asks. Check an expansion by substituting the same number into both forms. First, three classic slips to watch for: spot each one, then run that check.

Challenges 1–3

More than one skill at once

1. Simplify 4x(x+3)-(x-5)(x+2).
4x(x+3)=4x^{2}+12x
(x-5)(x+2)=x^{2}-3x-10
Subtract all of it: 4x^{2}+12x-x^{2}+3x+10
=\boldsymbol{3x^{2}+15x+10}
2. Expand and simplify (2x-3)^{2}+x(x+6).
(2x-3)^{2}=4x^{2}-12x+9
x(x+6)=x^{2}+6x
4x^{2}-12x+9+x^{2}+6x=\boldsymbol{5x^{2}-6x+9}
3. A rectangular prism has a square base of side length (x+2) cm and height (x-1) cm. Write a simplified expression for its volume.
V=(x+2)^{2}(x-1)=(x^{2}+4x+4)(x-1)
=x^{3}-x^{2}+4x^{2}-4x+4x-4
V=\boldsymbol{x^{3}+3x^{2}-4}\text{ cm}^{3} (the x terms cancel)

Key ideas

Only like terms combine: same variables, same exponents. Subtracting a polynomial = adding its opposite: flip every sign.
Every product is each term × each term: distribute (monomial), 2 × 2 = 4 (FOIL), 2 × 3 = 6. Coefficients multiply, exponents add, signs multiply.
Patterns: (a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2 and (a\pm b)^2=a^2\pm2ab+b^2. Never just a^2+b^2.
Applications: the expression is the length, area or volume. A=lw, V=lwh. Check any expansion by substituting a number.
