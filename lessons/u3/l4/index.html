Special Products: Difference of Squares & Perfect Squares

Some binomial products show up so often that they are worth memorizing. Then you can skip FOIL and write the answer. Today a square loses a corner and turns into a rectangle, we see why (a+b)^2 is never just a^2+b^2, and we learn to spot which pattern fits.

I can…
  • expand a difference of squares: (a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2 (no middle term)
  • expand a perfect square: (a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2 and (a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2
  • explain with a picture why (a+b)^2\neq a^2+b^2
  • decide which pattern a product fits, or that it fits neither and needs FOIL

Recall: Multiplying Two Binomials

FOIL multiplies the First terms, the Outer terms, the Inner terms and the Last terms, then combines like terms. The two special products below are just FOIL results worth memorizing. Warm up with one full expansion first.

Example 1

One full FOIL

Expand (x+3)(x+5) by filling in each step of FOIL.

First: x\cdot x=
\boldsymbol{x^2}
Outer: x\cdot5=
\boldsymbol{5x}
Inner: 3\cdot x=
\boldsymbol{3x}
Last: 3\cdot5=
\boldsymbol{15}
Now add the four results and combine like terms.
x^2+5x+3x+15=\boldsymbol{x^2+8x+15}
2

The Difference of Squares Pattern

When the same two terms appear once added and once subtracted, the Outer and Inner terms are opposites and cancel:

(x+4)(x-4)=x^2-4x+4x-16=x^2-16

Difference of squares  (a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2
Square the first term, square the second, subtract. There is no middle term at all.
Example 2

Difference of squares

Use the difference of squares pattern to expand each product.

(a) (x+2)(x-2)
a=x,\ b=2
x^2-2^2
=\boldsymbol{x^2-4}
(b) (x-6)(x+6)
Order doesn’t matter:
x^2-6^2
=\boldsymbol{x^2-36}
(c) (2x+1)(2x-1)
a=2x,\ b=1
(2x)^2-1^2
=\boldsymbol{4x^2-1}
(d) (7-x)(7+x)
a=7,\ b=x
7^2-x^2
=\boldsymbol{49-x^2}
!
Square the whole term. In (c) the first term is 2x, so its square is (2x)^2=4x^2, not 2x^2.

Back to the warm-up: mental math

Two numbers the same distance from a friendly number make a difference of squares. That’s how 49\times51 turns into 50^2-1^2, with no long multiplication.

3

The Perfect Square Trinomial Pattern (Sum)

Squaring a binomial means multiplying it by itself, so now the Outer and Inner terms are identical and add:

(x+3)^2=(x+3)(x+3)=x^2+3x+3x+9=x^2+6x+9

Perfect square trinomial  (a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2
Square the first, double the product, square the last. Three terms, never two.
!
Careful: (a+b)^2\neq a^2+b^2. Check with x=1: (1+3)^2=16, but 1^2+3^2=10. The missing 6 is the two middle rectangles.
Example 3

Squaring a sum

Use the perfect square trinomial pattern to expand each square.

(a) (x+2)^2
x^2+2(x)(2)+2^2
=\boldsymbol{x^2+4x+4}
(b) (x+7)^2
x^2+2(x)(7)+49
=\boldsymbol{x^2+14x+49}
(c) (3x+2)^2
(3x)^2+2(3x)(2)+4
=\boldsymbol{9x^2+12x+4}
(d) (2x+5)^2
(2x)^2+2(2x)(5)+25
=\boldsymbol{4x^2+20x+25}
Think How can you check (b) without FOIL? Try x=1.
(1+7)^2=64 and 1+14+49=64 ✓. The wrong answer x^2+49 would give only 50.
4

The Perfect Square Trinomial Pattern (Difference)

Squaring a difference works the same way, but both middle terms are now negative:

(x-4)^2=x^2-4x-4x+16=x^2-8x+16

Perfect square trinomial  (a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2
The first and last terms stay positive (a square always is). Only the middle term is subtracted.
Example 4

Squaring a difference

Use the perfect square trinomial pattern to expand each square.

(a) (x-4)^2
x^2-2(x)(4)+16
=\boldsymbol{x^2-8x+16}
(b) (x-9)^2
x^2-2(x)(9)+81
=\boldsymbol{x^2-18x+81}
(c) (4x-1)^2
(4x)^2-2(4x)(1)+1
=\boldsymbol{16x^2-8x+1}
(d) (3x-4)^2
(3x)^2-2(3x)(4)+16
=\boldsymbol{9x^2-24x+16}
!
The last term is always plus. In (a), (-4)(-4)=+16. Only the middle term takes the minus sign.

Identifying Which Pattern Applies

Before you expand, ask which of three cases you have.

Difference of squares
The same two terms, once with + and once with −.
(x+5)(x-5)=x^2-25
Perfect square trinomial
The same binomial squared (times itself).
(x+5)^2=x^2+10x+25
Neither
No shortcut exists: just FOIL it.
(x+5)(x-2)=x^2+3x-10

The Square That Lost a Corner

A, 2 499. A 50\times50 square loses a 1\times1 corner and slides into a 51 by 49 rectangle: 49\times51=2\,500-1=2\,499. It is exactly one less than 50\times50.
1
How many small squares are left after the corner is cut out?
40: 7\times7=49, minus the 3\times3=9 cut out.
2
How long and how wide is the new rectangle? Same number of squares?
10 by 4: 7+3=10 long, 7-3=4 tall. 10\times4=40, the same squares.
3
A 10 by 10 square loses a 2 by 2 corner. What rectangle do you get?
12 by 8: 10+2 and 10-2. Check: 12\times8=96=100-4.

Race your neighbour, no calculators: who can work out 98 × 102 first? 100^2-2^2=10\,000-4=\boldsymbol{9\,996}

Key ideas

Difference of squares: (a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2. The O and I terms cancel, so there is no middle term.
Perfect square trinomial: (a\pm b)^2=a^2\pm2ab+b^2. Square the first, double the product, square the last: always three terms.
(a+b)^2\neq a^2+b^2. The two middle rectangles are real: (1+3)^2=16, not 10.
Check first: same two terms with + and −? Difference of squares. Same binomial squared? Perfect square. Neither? Just FOIL.
