Multiplying Two Binomials

In Lesson 2 one term was multiplied by every term in a bracket. Today both factors are binomials, like (x+2)(x+5). The big idea: each term times each term. That makes four products every time, and FOIL is a checklist so you never miss one.

I can…
  • multiply two binomials by distributing twice: (x+2)(x+5)=x(x+5)+2(x+5)
  • use FOIL (First, Outer, Inner, Last) to find all four products
  • carry a negative sign into every product it is part of
  • collect the like terms (usually the O and I terms) to get one simplified answer
  • expand two products, then add or subtract them

1

Two Binomials by the Distributive Property

In Lesson 2 a monomial was distributed over every term in the brackets: a(b+c)=ab+ac. The same rule works when the “multiplier” is a whole binomial. Distribute the first binomial, then distribute each of its terms:

(x+2)(x+5)=x(x+5)+2(x+5)=x^2+5x+2x+10=x^2+7x+10

In general (a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd, then collect like terms. Each term times each term: two terms times two terms makes four products.

Distribute twice: Step 1, then Step 2

Step 1: keep the second binomial in one piece and distribute the first binomial. Step 2: distribute each term. The four arrows land on the four cells of the area model.

Example 1

Distribute twice

Use the distributive property. Show both distributing steps, then collect like terms.

(a) (x+3)(x+4)
Step 1x(x+4)+3(x+4)
Step 2x^2+4x+3x+12
Collect\boldsymbol{x^2+7x+12}
(b) (x+6)(x+2)
Step 1x(x+2)+6(x+2)
Step 2x^2+2x+6x+12
Collect\boldsymbol{x^2+8x+12}
Think Both answers end in 12. Where does the last number always come from?
From the two numbers multiplied together: 3\times4=12 and 6\times2=12. It is the small corner piece of the rectangle.
2

The FOIL Method

The four products ac,\ ad,\ bc,\ bd always come from the same four pairs, so FOIL is a checklist for them. It is not a new rule: it just makes sure no product is missed. Always finish by collecting the like terms (usually the O and I terms).

Firstac
Outerad
Innerbc
Lastbd

Why you can’t skip the O and I

A common shortcut is to multiply only the first terms and the last terms. Test it with a number and count the squares.

!
Four products, not two. (x+3)(x+2)\neq x^2+6. The Outer and Inner products are real pieces of the rectangle. FOIL keeps you from skipping them.
Example 2

FOIL

Use FOIL to determine each product.

(a) (2x+1)(x+5)
F 2x^2  O 10x
I x  L 5
2x^2+10x+x+5
=\boldsymbol{2x^2+11x+5}
(b) (3x+2)(x+4)
F 3x^2  O 12x
I 2x  L 8
3x^2+12x+2x+8
=\boldsymbol{3x^2+14x+8}
(c) (2x+5)(3x+1)
F 6x^2  O 2x
I 15x  L 5
6x^2+2x+15x+5
=\boldsymbol{6x^2+17x+5}
3

Binomials with Negative Terms

A subtracted term is a negative term, so rewrite x-4 as x+(-4) and carry the sign into every product it appears in. Check the sign of each of the four FOIL products separately.

(+)(+)=+positive × positive
(+)(-)=-positive × negative
(-)(-)=+negative × negative
!
Two negatives make a positive. In (x-2)(x-7) the Last product is (-2)(-7)=+14, not -14.
Example 3

Watch your signs

Use FOIL to determine each product. Watch your signs carefully.

(a) (x-4)(x+6)
F x^2  O 6x
I -4x  L -24
x^2+6x-4x-24
=\boldsymbol{x^2+2x-24}
(b) (x-2)(x-7)

=\boldsymbol{x^2-9x+14}
(c) (2x-3)(x-5)

=\boldsymbol{2x^2-13x+15}
(d) (x+3y)(x-2y)

Multiplying and Then Simplifying

Collecting the Outer and Inner terms is what turns four terms into a trinomial ax^2+bx+c. Both are x-terms, so collecting them is just adding the numbers in front, signs and all.

Example 4

Collect the middle terms

Expand and simplify. Pay close attention to collecting the middle terms.

(a) (3x-2)(2x-5)
F 6x^2  O -15x  I -4x  L +10
6x^2-15x-4x+10
=\boldsymbol{6x^2-19x+10}
(b) (4x+1)(2x-3)
F 8x^2  O -12x  I +2x  L -3
8x^2-12x+2x-3
=\boldsymbol{8x^2-10x-3}

Two products in one expression

If an expression contains two binomial products, expand both completely first. Then collect like terms. A subtraction sign in front of a product flips the sign of all of its terms. This is how the calendar trick works!

!
Keep the second product in brackets until you use the minus. -(x^2+6x+8)=-x^2-6x-8. Flipping only the first sign is the most common mistake here.
Example 5

Expand both, then collect

Expand and simplify.

(a) (x+4)(x+2)+(x+1)(x+5)
First product: x^2+6x+8
Second product: x^2+6x+5
Add: \boldsymbol{2x^2+12x+13}
(b) (x+7)(x+3)-(x+2)(x+4)
First: x^2+10x+21   Second: x^2+6x+8
Subtract all three terms of the second:
x^2+10x+21-x^2-6x-8=\boldsymbol{4x+13}
Think In (b), where did the x^2 go?
x^2-x^2=0: a zero pair. Both products start with x^2, so subtracting them cancels it. The same thing happens in the calendar trick.
Extra

The Calendar Trick

Box any 2 × 2 block of dates. Multiply along each diagonal, then subtract: top-right × bottom-left minus top-left × bottom-right. Tap a date to move the block.

!
The big one. Do the trick with the corners of a 3 × 3 block. What happens? Guess first.
A 7
B 14
C 28
D it changes
C, 28, every time. Corners 8, 10, 22, 24: 10\times22-8\times24=220-192=28. A bigger block, and the answer is four times as big. Today’s algebra explains it.
1
Do the trick on the block that starts at 18.
19\times25=475, 18\times26=468, and 475-468=\boldsymbol{7}.
2
Now pick any 2 × 2 block you like. What do you get?
7, every time: all 20 of the full 2 × 2 blocks in October.
3
Call the top-left date n. The date beside it is n+1. What are the two dates below?
n+7 and n+8: one week later is always 7 more.

Open the calendar on your phone: any month, any year. Can anyone break the trick? Only with a block that spills into the next month, where the dates start over.

✓

Key ideas

Each term times each term. Two binomials make four products: (a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd. The area model shows all four pieces.
FOIL is a checklist for those four: First, Outer, Inner, Last. (x+3)(x+2)\neq x^2+6: never skip O and I.
Signs: rewrite x-4 as x+(-4). Check each product’s sign; negative × negative is positive.
Collect last. The O and I terms usually combine into one middle term. Two products? Expand both first; a minus flips every term.
