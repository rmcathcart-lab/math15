Multiplying a Binomial by a Trinomial

FOIL works when each bracket has two terms. Today one bracket has three. The rule doesn’t change: every term times every term. That makes six partial products. We organize them in a grid or a list, then collect the like terms.

I can…
  • explain why a binomial times a trinomial makes 2\times3=6 partial products
  • multiply a binomial by a trinomial with a grid and with a list (the distributive property)
  • collect the like terms to write the answer as ax^3+bx^2+cx+d
  • find the sign of each partial product on its own when there are negative terms
  • expand a product first, then add or subtract another polynomial
AN4 Demonstrate an understanding of the multiplication of polynomial expressions (limited to monomials, binomials and trinomials), concretely, pictorially and symbolically.
Today: a binomial times a trinomial.
1

Recall: Two Binomials and the Special Products

FOIL handles two-term brackets. Lesson 4 added two patterns. All of these tools need exactly two terms in each bracket.

FOIL(a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd
Difference of squares(a+b)(a-b)=a^{2}-b^{2}
Perfect squares(a\pm b)^{2}=a^{2}\pm 2ab+b^{2}
Example 1

Two binomials: a quick warm-up

Expand and simplify, using the method named in each part.

(a) (x+2)(x+6)  use FOIL
F: x^2   O: 6x   I: 2x   L: 12
x^2+6x+2x+12=\mathbf{x^2+8x+12}
(b) (x+7)(x-7)  use a special product
Difference of squares, a=x, b=7:
\mathbf{x^2-49}  (no middle term)

Multiplying a Binomial by a Trinomial

A trinomial has three terms, so FOIL no longer fits. But the distributive property still does:

(a+b)(c+d+e)=a(c+d+e)+b(c+d+e)=ac+ad+ae+bc+bd+be
That’s six partial products: 2\times3=6.

The warm-up already did this with numbers. Let’s replace each 10 with x.

Two terms × three terms

Each of the 2 terms in the binomial must multiply each of the 3 terms in the trinomial.

!
FOIL only works for 2 × 2. On a trinomial, FOIL never touches the middle term, so two partial products go missing.

Method 1: the grid

The binomial’s terms label the rows. The trinomial’s terms label the columns. Every cell is row × column. Fill all six cells, then add them up.

Method 2: a systematic list

The first term of the binomial multiplies all three terms of the trinomial. Then the second term does the same. Same six products as the grid, written as a list.

Example 2

One product, two ways

Expand (x+2)\left(x^{2}+4x+5\right) two ways.

(i) Fill in all six cells, then add them up.
×x^24x5
xx^34x^25x
22x^28x10
Sum of the six cells:
x^3+4x^2+5x+2x^2+8x+10
=\mathbf{x^3+6x^2+13x+10}
(ii) Use the distributive property as a list of all six products.
x\cdot x^2= x^3
2\cdot x^2= 2x^2
x\cdot 4x= 4x^2
2\cdot 4x= 8x
x\cdot 5= 5x
2\cdot 5= 10
Add them and collect like terms:
x^2 terms: 4x^2+2x^2=6x^2;  x terms: 5x+8x=13x
=\mathbf{x^3+6x^2+13x+10}, the same answer as the grid.
Think Check the answer the warm-up way: put x=10 into both sides. What numbers do you get?
Left side: (10+2)(100+40+5)=12\times145=1\,740. Right side: 1\,000+600+130+10=1\,740. They match ✓.
3

Collecting Like Terms After Expanding

In this lesson’s products, the six partial products are one x^3 term, two x^2 terms, two x terms and one constant. So expect to combine two pairs of like terms.

x^31 term
x^22 terms: combine
x2 terms: combine
\text{number}1 term

Like terms have the same variable and the same exponent. The tidy answer looks like ax^3+bx^2+cx+d.

↘
In the grid, like terms sit on a diagonal. Write both brackets with the highest power first, and the two x^2 cells (and the two x cells) line up corner to corner.
Example 3

Six partial products, then collect

Expand and simplify. Write all six partial products first, then combine like terms.

(a) (2x+1)\left(x^{2}+3x+4\right)
2x^3+6x^2+8x+x^2+3x+4
x^2: 6x^2+x^2=7x^2;  x: 8x+3x=11x
=\mathbf{2x^3+7x^2+11x+4}
(b) (3x+4)\left(x^{2}+2x+1\right)
3x^3+6x^2+3x+4x^2+8x+4
x^2: 6x^2+4x^2=10x^2;  x: 3x+8x=11x
=\mathbf{3x^3+10x^2+11x+4}

Products Involving Negative Terms

The process doesn’t change. Only the bookkeeping gets harder. Find the sign of each of the six partial products on its own:

(+)(+)=+
(+)(-)=-
(-)(+)=-
(-)(-)=+
!
Don’t copy a sign out of the brackets. In (x-3)(x^2+2x-5), the product (-3)(-5) is +15, even though both brackets show a minus sign.
Example 4

Watch your signs

Expand and simplify. Watch your signs in every partial product.

(a) (x-3)\left(x^{2}+2x-5\right)
x^3+2x^2-5x-3x^2-6x+15
x^2: 2x^2-3x^2=-x^2;  x: -5x-6x=-11x
=\mathbf{x^3-x^2-11x+15}
(b) (2x-1)\left(x^{2}-3x+4\right)
2x^3-6x^2+8x-x^2+3x-4
x^2: -6x^2-x^2=-7x^2;  x: 8x+3x=11x
=\mathbf{2x^3-7x^2+11x-4}
5

Combining with Another Polynomial

Sometimes the product is only part of a longer expression. Work in this order:

1Expand and simplify the product completely.
2Deal with the sign in front of the other bracket.
3Collect like terms.
!
A subtraction sign flips every term. -\left(x^2+5x-6\right)=-x^2-5x+6. The last term becomes +6. A plus sign in front changes nothing.
Example 5

A product plus or minus a polynomial

Expand and simplify.

(a) (x+4)\left(x^{2}-2x+3\right)-\left(x^{2}+5x-6\right)
Product: x^3-2x^2+3x+4x^2-8x+12=x^3+2x^2-5x+12
Subtract every term: -x^2-5x+6
=\mathbf{x^3+x^2-10x+18}
(b) (2x-3)\left(x^{2}+x-4\right)+\left(3x^{2}-2x+5\right)
Product: 2x^3+2x^2-8x-3x^2-3x+12=2x^3-x^2-11x+12
Add: +3x^2-2x+5
=\mathbf{2x^3+2x^2-13x+17}

✓

Key ideas

Every term × every term. A binomial times a trinomial makes 2\times3=6 partial products. FOIL only covers 2\times2.
Grid or list: the same six products. (x+2)(x^2+4x+5)=x^3+6x^2+13x+10.
Collect two pairs: the two x^2 terms and the two x terms. The answer looks like ax^3+bx^2+cx+d.
Signs: find the sign of each partial product on its own. Expand a product first; a minus sign in front of a bracket flips every term.
