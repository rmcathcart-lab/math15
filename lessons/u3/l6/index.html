Applying Polynomial Products to Area, Volume, and Equations

Every multiplication skill from this unit, put to work on real shapes. When the sides of a garden or a crate are expressions, the product is the area or the volume. Put in a number for x and you get real square metres or cubic centimetres.

I can…
  • write the area of a rectangle with binomial sides as a simplified trinomial, then evaluate it
  • write the volume of a box with three binomial edges: multiply two edges first, then the third
  • set two equal areas equal, expand both sides, and solve the equation that is left when the x^2 terms cancel
  • work through a multi-step problem, changing a dimension before I multiply
  • check an answer by multiplying the real side lengths
AN4 Demonstrate an understanding of the multiplication of polynomial expressions (limited to monomials, binomials and trinomials), concretely, pictorially and symbolically.
Today: products that measure areas and volumes.

2

Area Applications

Area of a rectangle: A=\text{length}\times\text{width}

When the length and width are binomials, multiply them with FOIL and collect like terms. The simplified trinomial is the area. To find a number, substitute the given value of x into that trinomial.

Example 1

A vegetable garden

A rectangular vegetable garden has length (x+6) m and width (x+2) m.

(a) Write an expression for the area of the garden as a simplified trinomial.
A=(x+6)(x+2)=x^2+2x+6x+12
A=\mathbf{x^2+8x+12} (in m²)
(b) Determine the area of the garden when x=3 m.
A=3^2+8(3)+12=9+24+12=\mathbf{45} m²
Check with the sides: 9\times5=45 ✓

An area with a piece cut out

Area left = whole − cut-out

When a piece is cut out of a shape, subtract its area from the whole area, and keep the cut-out in brackets so every term of it is subtracted. A lawn (x+7) m by (x+5) m has a square bed of side (x+1) m cut out:

(x+7)(x+5)-(x+1)^2=(x^2+12x+35)-(x^2+2x+1)=10x+34

At x=3 that is 64 m².

3

Volume Applications

Volume of a rectangular prism: V=\text{length}\times\text{width}\times\text{height}

With three binomial edges, multiply two of them first to get a trinomial (the area of the base). Then multiply that trinomial by the third binomial. That’s exactly the Lesson 5 skill.

Example 2

A storage crate

A rectangular storage crate has length (x+2) cm, width (x+1) cm, and height (x+3) cm.

(a) Write an expression for the volume of the crate as a simplified polynomial. Hint: multiply the length and width first, then multiply that trinomial by the height.
Length × width: (x+2)(x+1)=x^2+3x+2
Now × height: (x^2+3x+2)(x+3)
=x^3+3x^2+2x+3x^2+9x+6
V=\mathbf{x^3+6x^2+11x+6} (in cm³)
(b) Determine the volume of the crate when x=2 cm.
V=8+6(4)+11(2)+6=8+24+22+6=\mathbf{60} cm³
Check: 4\times3\times5=60 ✓

Writing and Solving Equations from Equal Areas

If two regions have the same area, set the two area expressions equal and expand both sides. The x^2 terms cancel, and a simple linear equation is left to solve.

1Write area A = area B.
2Expand both sides (FOIL).
3The x^2 terms cancel. Solve for x.
4Check with the real sizes.
Example 3

Two plots, the same area

Plot A measures (x+2) m by (x+9) m. Plot B measures (x+1) m by (x+11) m. The two plots have exactly the same area.

(a) Write an equation that sets the area of Plot A equal to the area of Plot B.
\mathbf{(x+2)(x+9)=(x+1)(x+11)}
(b) Expand both sides and simplify to solve for x.
x^2+11x+18=x^2+12x+11
Subtract x^2 from both sides: 11x+18=12x+11
18-11=12x-11x, so \mathbf{x=7}
(c) State the actual dimensions and area of each plot.
Plot A: 9 m by 16 m; Plot B: 8 m by 18 m.
Both areas =\mathbf{144} m² ✓
Think Why do the x^2 terms always cancel in a question like this?
Each side is x\cdot x plus smaller terms, so both sides start with exactly one x^2. Subtracting x^2 from both sides removes it, and only x terms and numbers are left.
5

A Multi-Step Application

This one puts area, volume and evaluating together. Watch for phrases like “2 cm longer and 2 cm wider”: they change a dimension before you multiply.

!
Change the sides first, then multiply. A lid 2 cm longer than (x+4) is (x+4+2)=(x+6). Adding 2 to the area instead would be wrong.
Example 4

A fish tank with a glass lid

A rectangular fish tank has a base with length (x+4) cm and width (x+1) cm, and a height of (x+2) cm. A glass lid is 2 cm longer and 2 cm wider than the base, so it overhangs slightly.

(a) Write and simplify an expression for the area of the base of the tank.
A=(x+4)(x+1)
=x^2+x+4x+4
=\mathbf{x^2+5x+4} cm²
(b) Write and simplify an expression for the volume of the tank. Hint: use part (a).
V=(x^2+5x+4)(x+2)
=x^3+2x^2+5x^2+10x+4x+8
V=\mathbf{x^3+7x^2+14x+8} cm³
(c) Write and simplify an expression for the area of the lid.
Lid: (x+4+2) by (x+1+2), so (x+6)(x+3)
=\mathbf{x^2+9x+18} cm²
(d) If x=6, calculate the volume of the tank and the area of the lid.
V=216+252+84+8=\mathbf{560} cm³
Lid =36+54+18=\mathbf{108} cm²
Check: 10\times7\times8=560, 12\times9=108 ✓

The Open-Top Box

Cut a square from each corner of a 12 cm by 12 cm sheet. Fold up the sides. Tap a cut size to fold it. Tap a ? to reveal it.

1
Cut 1 cm squares. Why is the box 10 cm long, not 11 cm?
A square is cut from both ends of each side, so the length loses 1 cm twice: 12-2\times1=10.
2
Cut 3 cm squares. How long, wide and tall is the box? Its volume?
6 by 6 by 3 (12-2\times3=6). Volume 6\times6\times3=108 cm³.
3
What if you cut 6 cm squares?
12-2\times6=0: nothing is left to fold. No box, volume 0.
!
The big one. A small cut makes a box that is wide but flat. A big cut makes one that is tall but skinny. Which cut makes the box hold the most? Guess first, then fill in the table.
A 1 cm
B 2 cm
C 3 cm
D 4 cm
B, 2 cm: 8\times8\times2=128 cm³.
The table: 100, 128, 108, 64, 20, 0. The volume climbs, peaks, then falls to 0.
The 4\times4\times4 cube looks the fullest, but it holds only 64, half of the winner.
+
Try it: cut and fold a real sheet. Could a cut like 2.5 cm beat the winner?
No. 2.5 cm gives 7\times7\times2.5=122.5 and 1.5 cm gives 9\times9\times1.5=121.5. 128 cm³ is the most.
✓

Key ideas

Area: multiply length × width with FOIL, collect like terms. (x+6)(x+2)=x^2+8x+12. Substitute x to get a number.
Volume: multiply two edges first (the base), then multiply by the third edge: a binomial × trinomial.
Equal areas: set the areas equal, expand both sides, cancel the x^2 terms, solve for x.
Multi-step: change a side before multiplying (“2 cm longer”). Check every answer with the real side lengths.
