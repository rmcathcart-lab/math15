Determining Collinearity Using Slope

Do three points lie on one straight line? Your eyes can be fooled, but slope can’t. Three points are collinear exactly when the two slopes through the shared middle point agree.

I can…
  • explain what collinear means: one straight line passes through all the points
  • use the slope test: find m_{AB} and m_{BC} (sharing the point B) and compare them
  • follow the three steps to decide whether three points are collinear, even with negative coordinates
  • find a missing coordinate when I’m told the points are collinear
  • decide whether places on a map lie along one straight road
RF3 Demonstrate an understanding of slope with respect to: rise and run; line segments and lines; rate of change; parallel lines; perpendicular lines.
Today: slope as a test. Equal slopes through a shared point mean one straight line.
Warm-up

Line Up the Shot

A laser beam goes in a straight line. It can never bend. Launcher → target → bullseye.

1
Name the point where the launcher sits.
(2,\ 4): 2 across, 4 up.
2
Shot 2, launcher to target: how many squares up? across?
Up 1, across 4, so the slope is \tfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}=\tfrac{1}{4}.
3
Shot 3 goes down 2, across 4 to its target, then down 1, across 8. Can one straight beam do that?
No. \tfrac{-2}{4}=-\tfrac{1}{2} is much steeper than \tfrac{-1}{8}. The beam would have to bend at the target, and lasers can’t.
!
The big one. To score, the beam must hit the exact centre of the target and the bullseye. Which shots score? Guess first.
A shot 1 only
B shot 2 only
C shots 1 and 2
D none
B, shot 2 only. Shot 2: \tfrac{5-4}{6-2}=\tfrac{1}{4} and \tfrac{7-5}{14-6}=\tfrac{2}{8}=\tfrac{1}{4}. Same slope: it scores.
Shot 1: \tfrac{6-4}{5-2}=\tfrac{2}{3} but \tfrac{9-6}{10-5}=\tfrac{3}{5}. Different! The beam through the target reaches x=10 at y=9\tfrac{1}{3}: it misses by \tfrac{1}{3} of a square.
Most people pick C: by eye, shot 1 looks perfect. Your eyes can be fooled; slopes can’t.

Hands up for your guess. Then convince a neighbour who disagrees.

1

What “Collinear” Means

Three or more points are collinear if one single straight line passes through all of them at once. Lay a ruler on the page, and its edge touches every point. If even one point sits off to the side of the line through the other two, the points are not collinear.
2

The Slope Test

A picture only helps once the points are graphed. Given coordinates alone, use slope: m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}.

Key idea. For points A, B, C with B shared by AB and BC: if m_{AB}=m_{BC}, the two segments point in exactly the same direction. Since they also share the point B, they are two pieces of the same straight line, so A, B, C are collinear. If m_{AB}\neq m_{BC}, they bend at B and are not collinear. Any two segments that share a point work the same way, for example m_{AB} and m_{AC}.
Example 1

Compare the two slopes

Determine whether A(1,2), B(3,6), and C(6,12) are collinear by comparing m_{AB} and m_{BC}.

m_{AB}=\dfrac{y_B-y_A}{x_B-x_A}=
m_{AB}=\dfrac{6-2}{3-1}=\dfrac{4}{2}=\boldsymbol{2}
m_{BC}=\dfrac{y_C-y_B}{x_C-x_B}=
m_{BC}=\dfrac{12-6}{6-3}=\dfrac{6}{3}=\boldsymbol{2}
Conclusion:
m_{AB}=m_{BC}=2, so A, B, C are collinear.
3

The Three-Step Process

1Find the slope between the first two points.
2Find the slope between the second two points (sharing the middle point).
3Compare. Equal slopes: collinear. Unequal slopes: not collinear.

Try it on the warm-up shots. Call the launcher, the target and the bullseye three points: a shot scores only if they are collinear.

Example 2

Three steps, negative coordinates

Determine whether D(-3,-4), E(1,0), and F(5,4) are collinear. Show both slopes, then conclude.

Step 1: m_{DE}=
\dfrac{0-(-4)}{1-(-3)}=\dfrac{4}{4}=\boldsymbol{1}
Step 2: m_{EF}=
\dfrac{4-0}{5-1}=\dfrac{4}{4}=\boldsymbol{1}
Step 3: compare. Collinear?
m_{DE}=m_{EF}=1, and both segments share the point E.
Yes: D, E, F are collinear.
Example 3

Same three steps

Determine whether G(-2,5), H(0,1), and I(3,-2) are collinear. Use the same three steps.

Step 1: m_{GH}=
\dfrac{1-5}{0-(-2)}=\dfrac{-4}{2}=\boldsymbol{-2}
Step 2: m_{HI}=
\dfrac{-2-1}{3-0}=\dfrac{-3}{3}=\boldsymbol{-1}
Step 3: compare. Collinear?
-2\neq-1, so the path bends at H.
No: G, H, I are not collinear.
Negatives on the calculator. Brackets around the top and the bottom, and (−) for each negative number. Step 1: (1−5)÷(0−(−)2)ENTER gives -2.

Check Examples 2 and 3

The three steps on the grid. Then turn the bent piece of Example 3 around the shared point until the slopes agree: where would I have to be?

4

An Unknown Coordinate, and a Real-World Check

If we are told three points are collinear, the two slopes must already be equal. Set the known slope equal to the slope expression containing the unknown, then solve the equation. The same test decides whether real places on a map lie along one straight road.

!
Solving a slope equation like \dfrac{k-4}{2}=3: undo the division first (multiply both sides by 2) to get k-4=6. Then undo the subtraction (add 4): k=10.
Example 4

Find the missing coordinate

The points J(2,3), K(5,9), and L(8,k) are collinear. Find k.

m_{JK}=\dfrac{9-3}{5-2}=
m_{JK}=\dfrac{6}{3}=\boldsymbol{2}
m_{KL}=\dfrac{k-9}{8-5}=m_{JK}\ \Longrightarrow\ k=
\dfrac{k-9}{3}=2\ \Rightarrow\ k-9=6
k=\boldsymbol{15}, so L(8,15).
Check: m_{JL}=\dfrac{15-3}{8-2}=\dfrac{12}{6}=2 ✓
Example 5

One straight road?

On a town map the Fire Station is at F(2,3), the School at S(6,9), and the Library at L(12,18), each unit 1 km. Do all three lie along one straight road?

m_{FS}=
\dfrac{9-3}{6-2}=\dfrac{6}{4}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3}{2}}
m_{SL}=
\dfrac{18-9}{12-6}=\dfrac{9}{6}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3}{2}}
Conclusion:
m_{FS}=m_{SL}=\tfrac{3}{2}, so all three do lie along one straight road.
✓

Key ideas

Collinear means one straight line passes through all the points. Your eyes can be fooled; slope can’t.
The slope test. Find m_{AB} and m_{BC}. They share the point B, so equal slopes mean one straight line.
Three steps. Step 1: first two points. Step 2: second two points. Step 3: compare. Equal: collinear. Not equal: the path bends.
Told they’re collinear? Set the two slopes equal and solve for the unknown. The same test checks a straight road on a map.
