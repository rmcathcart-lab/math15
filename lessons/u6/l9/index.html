Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

Two segments on the same grid. Do they point the same way forever, or meet at a perfect square corner? Your eyes can be fooled, but the slopes can’t. Find both slopes and compare: equal slopes mean parallel; slopes that are flipped with the sign changed mean perpendicular.

I can…
  • calculate the slope of each segment with the slope formula m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}
  • show two segments are parallel because their slopes are equal
  • find the negative reciprocal of a slope: flip it and change the sign
  • show two segments are perpendicular because their slopes multiply to -1
  • decide whether two slopes are parallel, perpendicular, or neither
RF3 Demonstrate an understanding of slope with respect to: rise and run; line segments and lines; rate of change; parallel lines; perpendicular lines.

Up 2, across 6.

1

Slope, and the Two Relationships

For P(x_1,\ y_1) and Q(x_2,\ y_2), the slope is m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}=\dfrac{\color{#7b4fc4}\text{rise}}{\color{#c27a0e}\text{run}}.
Parallel
slopes are equal
m_1=m_2
Perpendicular
slopes are negative reciprocals
m_1\times m_2=-1
Special case. A horizontal segment has slope 0 and a vertical segment has an undefined slope. Those two are perpendicular to each other, but the product rule can’t be used on them (you can’t multiply by “undefined”).
2

Parallel Lines Have Equal Slopes

Parallel segments point in the same direction, so they must have the same steepness. No matter how far you extend them, they never meet.

Worked demonstration. Segment AB (blue) and segment CD (red) sit on the same grid:

m_{AB}=\dfrac{1-(-2)}{-1-(-6)}=\dfrac{3}{5}m_{CD}=\dfrac{-1-(-4)}{5-0}=\dfrac{3}{5}

The slopes are identical, so AB\parallel CD.

Rule: AB\parallel CD if and only if m_{AB}=m_{CD}. To test a pair, find each slope separately, then compare the two values.
Example 1

Are they parallel? Read the graph

The graph shows segments PQ and RS.

(a) Calculate the slope of segment PQ.
P(-5,\ -3), Q(-1,\ 3): m_{PQ}=\dfrac{3-(-3)}{-1-(-5)}=\dfrac{6}{4}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3}{2}}
(b) Calculate the slope of segment RS.
R(0,\ -5), S(4,\ 1): m_{RS}=\dfrac{1-(-5)}{4-0}=\dfrac{6}{4}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{3}{2}}
(c) Are PQ and RS parallel? Explain how you know.
Yes. Both segments have slope \tfrac{3}{2}, and segments with equal slopes are parallel, so PQ\parallel RS.
Example 2

Are they parallel? From the coordinates

Segment TU has endpoints T(-8,\ 5) and U(4,\ -1); segment VW has endpoints V(-3,\ -2) and W(9,\ -8).

(a) Calculate the slope of TU.
m_{TU}=\dfrac{-1-5}{4-(-8)}=\dfrac{-6}{12}
=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{1}{2}}
(b) Calculate the slope of VW.
m_{VW}=\dfrac{-8-(-2)}{9-(-3)}=\dfrac{-6}{12}
=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{1}{2}}
(c) Are TU and VW parallel? Explain.
Yes, parallel. The two slopes are equal (-\tfrac12=-\tfrac12), and equal slopes mean the segments never meet.
!
Brackets around a negative coordinate. 4-(-8)=4+8=12. Write the bracket every time you subtract a negative.
The whole slope in one go. Bracket the top and the bottom, and use (−) for a negative: ((−)1−5)÷(4−(−)8)= gives -0.5. Then 2ndF◂▸D shows it as a fraction: −1┘2, which means -\tfrac12.

Make them longer: do they ever meet?

Equal slopes keep the gap between two segments the same forever. If the slopes are even a little different, the segments are not parallel: make them long enough and they cross.

Math History1557

Why “=” is two parallel lines

In 1557 the Welsh doctor Robert Recorde wrote a math book, The Whetstone of Witte. He was tired of writing “is equalle to” over and over, so he drew “Gemowe [twin] lines of one lengthe”.

His reason: “bicause noe .2. thynges, can be moare equalle.” Two twin lines are parallel, so their slopes are equal: today’s lesson, hiding in every equation you write.

A tilted “=” has its bottom line from (0,\ 0) to (6,\ 2). The top line starts at (0,\ 3). Where must it end to be parallel and the same length? What is each slope?
Slide the bottom line 3 up: it ends at \boldsymbol{(6,\ 5)}. Both slopes are \frac{2-0}{6-0}=\frac{5-3}{6-0}=\boldsymbol{\frac{1}{3}}.
3

Perpendicular Lines Have Negative Reciprocal Slopes

Perpendicular segments meet at a right angle (90^\circ). To get the negative reciprocal of a slope, flip it and change the sign: the perpendicular of \tfrac{2}{3} is -\tfrac{3}{2}, and the perpendicular of -5 (that is, -\tfrac{5}{1}) is \tfrac{1}{5}.

Worked demonstration. EF and FG meet at F, and the small square marks the right angle there:

m_{EF}=\dfrac{0-(-3)}{2-(-2)}=\dfrac{3}{4}m_{FG}=\dfrac{-4-0}{5-2}=-\dfrac{4}{3}
m_{EF}\times m_{FG}=\dfrac{3}{4}\times\left(-\dfrac{4}{3}\right)=-1

Notice -\tfrac{4}{3} is \tfrac{3}{4} flipped, with the sign changed.

Rule: two segments are perpendicular if and only if m_1\times m_2=-1. To test a pair, find both slopes and multiply.

Flip it and change the sign

From one slope you can get both partners: a parallel slope (the same number) and a perpendicular slope (flip it, change the sign). Tap a card to check.

m=\dfrac{3}{4}
∥ \dfrac34   ⊥ -\dfrac43
m=-3
∥ -3   ⊥ \dfrac13
m=-\dfrac{2}{5}
∥ -\dfrac25   ⊥ \dfrac52
m=6
∥ 6   ⊥ -\dfrac16

Multiply the slopes: is it a square corner?

A “+” can look perfect and still be a few degrees off. The product of the slopes settles it: exactly -1 means a right angle; anything else means it is not.

Example 3

A square corner at N?

The graph shows segments MN and NO, which share the point N.

(a) Calculate the slope of segment MN.
M(-4,\ -3), N(2,\ 1): m_{MN}=\dfrac{1-(-3)}{2-(-4)}=\dfrac{4}{6}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{2}{3}}
(b) Calculate the slope of segment NO.
N(2,\ 1), O(4,\ -2): m_{NO}=\dfrac{-2-1}{4-2}=\dfrac{-3}{2}=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{3}{2}}
(c) Multiply the two slopes together. Are MN and NO perpendicular?
\dfrac{2}{3}\times\left(-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)=-\dfrac{6}{6}=\boldsymbol{-1}
Yes, perpendicular. The product is -1 (the slopes are negative reciprocals), which matches the right-angle mark at N.
Multiply fractions with the fraction key. 2ab/c3×(−)3ab/c2= gives -1: perpendicular.
Example 4

Perpendicular? From the coordinates

Segment HI has endpoints H(-7,\ 2) and I(1,\ -2); segment JK has endpoints J(-3,\ -6) and K(1,\ 2).

(a) Calculate the slope of HI.
m_{HI}=\dfrac{-2-2}{1-(-7)}=\dfrac{-4}{8}
=\boldsymbol{-\dfrac{1}{2}}
(b) Calculate the slope of JK.
m_{JK}=\dfrac{2-(-6)}{1-(-3)}=\dfrac{8}{4}
=\boldsymbol{2}
(c) Multiply the slopes. Are HI and JK perpendicular?
-\dfrac{1}{2}\times 2=\boldsymbol{-1}
Yes, perpendicular.

Parallel, perpendicular, or neither?

Two slopes, two questions. Are they equal? Then parallel. If not, is their product -1? Then perpendicular. If neither is true: neither. Watch the trap: flipping the fraction without changing the sign gives neither.

Example 5

Parallel, perpendicular, or neither?

For each pair of slopes, state whether the segments are parallel, perpendicular, or neither.

(a) m_1=\dfrac{2}{5},\ m_2=\dfrac{2}{5}
The slopes are equal.
Parallel
(b) m_1=\dfrac{3}{4},\ m_2=-\dfrac{4}{3}
\dfrac{3}{4}\times\left(-\dfrac{4}{3}\right)=-1
Perpendicular
(c) m_1=\dfrac{5}{6},\ m_2=\dfrac{6}{5}
Flipped, but the sign did not change: \dfrac{5}{6}\times\dfrac{6}{5}=+1, not -1.
Neither

Extra: a missing number in a slope

Sometimes one slope has a letter in it. Use the rule as an equation. Parallel: set the two slopes equal. Perpendicular: set the letter’s slope equal to the negative reciprocal (flip it and change the sign). Then solve.

✓

Key ideas

Find both slopes first. m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}, same order top and bottom, brackets around negatives: 4-(-8)=12.
Parallel segments have equal slopes: m_1=m_2. They point the same way and never meet.
Perpendicular segments have negative reciprocal slopes: flip it and change the sign. Check: m_1\times m_2=-1.
Neither if the slopes are not equal and the product is not -1. Special case: horizontal (0) \perp vertical (undefined).
