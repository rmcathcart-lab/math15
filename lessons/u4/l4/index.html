Factoring Trinomials x^2+bx+c, Part 2: c Negative

Same hunt as Lesson 3: two numbers that multiply to c and add to b. The new twist: when c is negative, one number is positive and the other is negative, and the bigger one decides the sign of the sum.

I can…
  • explain why a negative c means the two numbers have opposite signs
  • decide which number is negative: the bigger one takes the sign of b
  • factor x^2+bx+c when c is negative, using a table of factor pairs when there are many
  • show a trinomial is prime by checking every factor pair
  • take out a common factor first, and check my answer by expanding

1

When c Is Negative: Opposite Signs

To factor x^2+bx+c=(x+p)(x+q) we still need two integers with product c and sum b. A negative product forces p and q to have opposite signs.

The integer with the larger absolute value (the bigger number, ignoring signs) takes the same sign as b. It controls the sum.
x^2+3x-18=(x+6)(x-3)bigger number positive, like b=+3
x^2-3x-18=(x-6)(x+3)bigger number negative, like b=-3

Same pair, 6 and 3. Only the signs swap.

Why the bigger number wins

Adding a positive and a negative number is a tug of war. Each +1 and −1 pair cancels to 0. Whatever is left over gives the answer, so the sum has the sign of the bigger number.

Example 1

Same two numbers, signs swapped

Factor each trinomial. Both parts use the same two numbers, just with the signs swapped.

(a) x^2+9x-22
Product -22, sum +9. Pair: 11 and 2, opposite signs, the larger one positive.
11+(-2)=9 ✓
=\boldsymbol{(x+11)(x-2)}
Check: x^2-2x+11x-22=x^2+9x-22 ✓
(b) x^2-9x-22
Same pair, signs swapped: -11 and +2.
-11+2=-9 ✓
=\boldsymbol{(x-11)(x+2)}
Check: x^2+2x-11x-22=x^2-9x-22 ✓

Is the minus on the right number? Check by expanding

The most common slip with a negative c is putting the minus sign on the wrong number. The product still works, but the middle term comes out with the wrong sign. Expanding catches it every time.

A Systematic Approach for Trickier Cases

When c has many factor pairs, guessing is slow and easy to get wrong. Instead, list every factor pair of c in order, starting from 1. Check the sum each pair gives in both sign arrangements until one matches b.

3

Prime Trinomials

Sometimes no pair works. If you list every factor pair of c and none of them, in either sign arrangement, adds to b, the trinomial cannot be factored over the integers.

A trinomial that cannot be factored over the integers is called prime. Check every pair before you say “prime”: stopping too early is the most common mistake.
Example 4

Factor it, or show it’s prime

Decide whether each can be factored over the integers. If it can, factor it. If not, list the factor pairs of c you checked and explain why none work.

(a) x^2+4x-15
Factor pairs of 15: 1,\ 15 and 3,\ 5.
With opposite signs their sums are 14 or -14, and 2 or -2. Neither is +4.
Prime: it cannot be factored over the integers.
(b) x^2+4x-12
Factor pairs of 12: 1,\ 12 (sums \pm11); 2,\ 6 (sums \pm4) ✓; 3,\ 4 (sums \pm1).
Take 6 and -2: product -12, sum 4.
=\boldsymbol{(x+6)(x-2)}
Check: x^2-2x+6x-12=x^2+4x-12 ✓
4

Removing a Common Factor First

Always look for a greatest common factor among all the terms before factoring the trinomial. Removing it leaves smaller numbers to work with.

Factoring is not finished until nothing more can be pulled out. Keep the common factor in front of your answer.
Example 5

Common factor first

Factor each expression completely.

(a) 2x^2+4x-30
GCF 2: 2(x^2+2x-15)
Product -15, sum 2: 5 and -3
=\boldsymbol{2(x+5)(x-3)}
Check: 2(x^2-3x+5x-15)
=2x^2+4x-30 ✓
(b) 3x^2-6x-45
GCF 3: 3(x^2-2x-15)
-5 and 3

Check: 3(x^2+3x-5x-15)

(c) 2x^3+2x^2-24x

Key ideas

Negative c: the two numbers have opposite signs. The bigger one takes the sign of b: x^2+3x-18=(x+6)(x-3), x^2-3x-18=(x-6)(x+3).
Many pairs? Make a table: every factor pair of c from 1 up, with the sum both ways. x^2+5x-36: the 4,\ 9 row gives (x+9)(x-4).
Prime: if no pair adds to b, the trinomial can’t be factored. x^2+4x-15 is prime. Check every pair first.
Common factor first, and keep it in front: 2x^3+2x^2-24x=2x(x+4)(x-3). Always check by expanding.
