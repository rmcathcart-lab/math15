Factoring ax^2+bx+c (where a\neq1)

So far every trinomial started with a plain x^2. Today the x^2 has a number in front, like 2x^2+7x+3. The new method is called decomposition: split the middle term, group in pairs, and factor out the common binomial. Same six steps every time.

I can…
  • explain why the sum-product method only works when a=1
  • find two integers with product ac and sum b
  • factor ax^2+bx+c by decomposition: split, group, and factor out the common binomial
  • use the signs of ac and b to choose the signs of the two integers
  • remove a GCF first, then decompose, and check by expanding (FOIL)
AN5 Demonstrate an understanding of common factors and trinomial factoring, concretely, pictorially and symbolically.

Why the Sum-Product Method Is Not Enough

Expanding (x+p)(x+q) always gives

(x+p)(x+q)=\textcolor{#c93a33}{1}x^2+(p+q)x+pq

The leading coefficient is always 1, because x\cdot x=x^2. So a trinomial like 2x^2+7x+3 cannot come from two binomials that both start with a plain x. When a\neq1 we need a method that accounts for the leading coefficient: decomposition.

2

The Decomposition Method, Step by Step

A trinomial is like the warm-up’s wrapped bar. We can see the corner pieces ax^2 and c, but the middle term bx is two pieces stuck together. Their product is a\times c (milk × milk), and their sum is b.

To factor ax^2+bx+c by decomposition, split the middle term using two integers whose product is a\times c and whose sum is b.
1MultiplyWork out a\times c.
2Find the pairTwo integers: product ac, sum b.
3SplitWrite bx as two terms. Now there are four terms.
4GroupTwo pairs. Factor the GCF out of each pair.
5Factor outBoth pairs share a binomial. Take it out.
6CheckExpand with FOIL. You should get the trinomial back.

Worked model: factor 2x^2+7x+3. Here a=2, b=7, c=3, so ac=6. The two integers with product 6 and sum 7 are 6 and 1. Split, group, and factor:

2x^2+7x+3=\underbrace{2x^2+6x}_{\text{GCF}=2x}+\underbrace{x+3}_{\text{GCF}=1}=2x(x+3)+1(x+3)=(x+3)(2x+1)

Check: (x+3)(2x+1)=2x^2+x+6x+3=2x^2+7x+3 ✓

Step 6 is the safety net. Factoring is expanding run backwards, so expanding your answer should give back the trinomial you started with. FOIL makes sure you multiply all four pairs of terms.

Example 1

Every step, then check

Factor using every step: find ac, find the two integers, split, group, factor out the binomial, then check with FOIL.

(a) 3x^2+10x+3
ac=3\times3=9. Integers with product 9 and sum 10: 9 and 1.
3x^2+9x+x+3
=3x(x+3)+1(x+3)
=\boldsymbol{(x+3)(3x+1)}
Check: 3x^2+x+9x+3=3x^2+10x+3 ✓
(b) 2x^2+9x+4
ac=2\times4=8. Product 8 and sum 9: 8 and 1.
2x^2+8x+x+4
=2x(x+4)+1(x+4)
=\boldsymbol{(x+4)(2x+1)}
Check: 2x^2+x+8x+4=2x^2+9x+4 ✓
3

Practising Decomposition

Once you know the six steps, the method is the same every time. Only the numbers change. Two things that often worry students: Which order do I write the split in? (Either works.) What if I pick the wrong pair? (The brackets won’t match, and that tells you.)

!
The GCF of a pair is not always ax. In 4x^2+10x the GCF is 2x, not 4x: 4x^2+10x=2x(2x+5). Take out the greatest factor the two terms share.
Example 2

a, b and c all positive

Factor each trinomial using decomposition.

(a) 2x^2+11x+5
ac=10; integers 10 and 1.
2x^2+10x+x+5
=2x(x+5)+1(x+5)
=\boldsymbol{(x+5)(2x+1)}
Check: 2x^2+x+10x+5 ✓
(b) 3x^2+13x+4
ac=12; integers 12 and 1.
3x^2+12x+x+4
=3x(x+4)+1(x+4)
=\boldsymbol{(x+4)(3x+1)}
Check: 3x^2+x+12x+4 ✓
(c) 4x^2+12x+5
ac=20; integers 10 and 2.
4x^2+10x+2x+5
=2x(2x+5)+1(2x+5)
=\boldsymbol{(2x+5)(2x+1)}
Check: 4x^2+2x+10x+5 ✓
Think In (c), 6+6 also adds to 12. Why can’t we split 12x as 6x+6x?
The pair must also multiply to ac=20, and 6\times6=36. Try it anyway: 4x^2+6x+6x+5=2x(2x+3)+1(6x+5). The brackets don’t match, so we’re stuck.
4

Decomposition with Negative b and/or c

Nothing changes except the signs of the two integers you find in Step 2.

ac positive: the two integers have the same sign. Both are negative when b is negative (both positive when b is positive).
ac negative: the two integers have opposite signs. The one with the bigger size (larger absolute value) takes the sign of b.
Example 3

ac positive, b negative

Factor each trinomial. Here ac is positive, so both integers have the same sign.

(a) 2x^2-9x+4
ac=8, sum -9: -8 and -1.
2x^2-8x-x+4=2x(x-4)-1(x-4)
=\boldsymbol{(x-4)(2x-1)}
Check: 2x^2-x-8x+4=2x^2-9x+4 ✓
(b) 3x^2-11x+6
ac=18, sum -11: -9 and -2.
3x^2-9x-2x+6=3x(x-3)-2(x-3)
=\boldsymbol{(x-3)(3x-2)}
Check: 3x^2-2x-9x+6=3x^2-11x+6 ✓
!
Taking out a negative flips the signs inside. -x+4=-1(x-4), not -1(x+4). The second bracket must match the first one exactly.

Here is the whole method with negative signs. Watch the second pair: when its first term is negative, take out a negative GCF so the brackets match.

Example 4

ac negative

Factor each trinomial. Here ac is negative, so the two integers have opposite signs.

(a) 2x^2+7x-4
ac=-8, sum 7: 8 and -1.
2x^2+8x-x-4
=2x(x+4)-1(x+4)
=\boldsymbol{(x+4)(2x-1)}
Check: 2x^2-x+8x-4 ✓
(b) 3x^2-4x-4
ac=-12, sum -4: -6 and 2.
3x^2-6x+2x-4
=3x(x-2)+2(x-2)
=\boldsymbol{(x-2)(3x+2)}
Check: 3x^2+2x-6x-4 ✓
(c) 4x^2-4x-3
ac=-12, sum -4: -6 and 2.
4x^2-6x+2x-3
=2x(2x-3)+1(2x-3)
=\boldsymbol{(2x-3)(2x+1)}
Check: 4x^2+2x-6x-3 ✓
5

Removing a GCF First, Then Decomposing

Always check for a GCF before decomposing. It shrinks a, b and c, so Steps 1 and 2 get much easier.

For example: 4x^2+10x-6=2(2x^2+5x-3). Then ac=-6 with sum 5 gives 6 and -1, so

2x^2+5x-3=2x^2+6x-x-3=2x(x+3)-1(x+3)=(x+3)(2x-1)

and 4x^2+10x-6=\boldsymbol{2(x+3)(2x-1)}. Don’t forget to write the GCF in front of the final answer!

✓
A quick check without expanding: put x=1 into both forms. 4(1)^2+10(1)-6=8 and 2(1+3)(2-1)=2(4)(1)=8. Same number ✓ (If they were different, the factoring would be wrong.)
Example 5

GCF first, then decompose

Factor each expression completely. Remove the GCF first, then decompose what remains.

(a) 6x^2+3x-30
GCF 3: 3(2x^2+x-10)
ac=-20, sum 1: 5 and -4
2x^2+5x-4x-10=x(2x+5)-2(2x+5)
=(2x+5)(x-2)
So 6x^2+3x-30=\boldsymbol{3(2x+5)(x-2)}
Check: 3(2x^2-4x+5x-10)=6x^2+3x-30 ✓
(b) 4x^3-2x^2-30x
GCF 2x: 2x(2x^2-x-15)
ac=-30, sum -1: -6 and 5
2x^2-6x+5x-15=2x(x-3)+5(x-3)
=(x-3)(2x+5)
So 4x^3-2x^2-30x=\boldsymbol{2x(x-3)(2x+5)}
Check: 2x(2x^2+5x-6x-15)=4x^3-2x^2-30x ✓
Think What goes wrong if you skip the GCF in (a)?
Then ac=6(-30)=-180, a much longer search. And you may end up with an answer like (2x+5)(3x-6), which is not finished: 3x-6=3(x-2) still has a common factor. Taking the GCF out first avoids both problems.
✓

Key ideas

Sum-product needs a=1. (x+p)(x+q) always starts with 1x^2. When a\neq1, use decomposition.
The magic pair: two integers with product a\times c and sum b. Like the chocolate bar: dark × dark = milk × milk.
Split, group, factor out: write bx as two terms, take the GCF out of each pair, then take out the common binomial. Signs: ac>0 same signs, ac<0 opposite signs.
Every time: GCF out first, and check by expanding with FOIL. Factoring is expanding run backwards.
