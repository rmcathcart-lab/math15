The Cartesian Plane and Plotting Points

Every spot on a grid has an address made of two numbers. Today: the parts of the Cartesian plane, how to plot a point from its ordered pair (over first, then up or down), how to read the coordinates of a point, and how the signs tell you the quadrant without plotting at all.

The Cartesian Plane

A Cartesian coordinate system is built from two perpendicular number lines called axes: the horizontal x-axis and the vertical y-axis. Together they form the Cartesian plane.
The axes cross at the origin, O, which is always located at (0,\ 0).
The axes divide the plane into four regions called quadrants, numbered I, II, III, IV, starting in the top-right region and moving counter-clockwise.

2

Ordered Pairs

Every point on the plane is located by an ordered pair (x,\ y).
The first number is the x-coordinate: how far to move left or right from the origin (positive is right, negative is left).
The second number is the y-coordinate: how far to move up or down (positive is up, negative is down).
(\textcolor{#c27a0e}{x},\ \textcolor{#7b4fc4}{y})
1st: over (left or right)
2nd: up or down

Worked demonstration: to plot P(2,\ 3), start at the origin, move 2 units right, then 3 units up.

“Ordered” matters: over first, then up or down

Swapping the two numbers gives a different point, and dropping a negative sign sends you the wrong way. The treasure map had a trap for each slip.

!
Over first, then up or down. (1,\ 4) and (4,\ 1) are different points. That is why it is called an ordered pair. Read the sign too: -3 means left (or down), never right.
3

Plotting Points and Reading Their Coordinates

Reading coordinates is the reverse of plotting. The distance from the y-axis gives the x-coordinate, and the distance from the x-axis gives the y-coordinate. Example 2 below practises plotting; Example 3 reverses it.

!
A point on an axis has a 0. On the y-axis there is no move left or right, so x=0, like (0,\ 2). On the x-axis there is no move up or down, so y=0, like (-4,\ 0).

Math History1637

A fly on the ceiling

The word “Cartesian” comes from René Descartes, a French thinker. In 1637 he published a short book, La Géométrie, that joined algebra to geometry. Pierre de Fermat had the same idea at about the same time.

Legend says Descartes was lying in bed watching a fly on the ceiling. He saw that two numbers, its distances from two walls, could pin down exactly where it was. Nobody knows if it really happened.

A fly is 6 tiles from the left wall and 2 tiles from the bottom wall. Write its position as an ordered pair. Is (2,\ 6) the same spot?
\mathbf{(6,\ 2)}: 6 across, 2 up. (2,\ 6) is 2 across and 6 up, a different spot. The order matters.

Which Quadrant Does a Point Lie In?

The signs of the coordinates give the quadrant without any plotting. Right of the y-axis means x is positive; above the x-axis means y is positive.

I(+,\ +)
II(-,\ +)
III(-,\ -)
IV(+,\ -)
If x=0, the point lies on the y-axis. If y=0, it lies on the x-axis. (0,\ 0) is the origin. A point on an axis lies in no quadrant.

Read the signs, find the quadrant

Look at each coordinate on its own: is it positive, negative or zero? The two signs point to one quadrant, or to an axis.

Plotting Several Points and Recognizing a Pattern

Several related points plotted on one grid often reveal a pattern, such as lining up in a straight row. That is the first step toward a relation, the topic of the next lessons. Two quick patterns to watch for: points with the same y-coordinate lie in a horizontal row, and points with the same x-coordinate lie in a vertical column.

Example 5

Five points in a row

Consider the points (-4,\ -4), (-2,\ -2), (0,\ 0), (2,\ 2), and (4,\ 4).

(a) Plot all five points on the grid.
Five points on one straight line through the origin.
(b) What do you notice about the way these five points are arranged?
They all lie on one straight line through the origin, rising to the right. In every pair the two coordinates are equal, so each point is the same distance right as it is up (or the same distance left as it is down).
(c) Other than the origin, which two quadrants contain these points?
Quadrant I: (2,\ 2) and (4,\ 4), both (+,\ +). Quadrant III: (-2,\ -2) and (-4,\ -4), both (-,\ -). The origin is in no quadrant.
✓

Key ideas

The plane: the horizontal x-axis and the vertical y-axis cross at the origin O(0,\ 0). They cut the plane into four quadrants, I to IV, counter-clockwise from the top right.
Plotting: (x,\ y) means move over by x (right +, left -), then up or down by y. Order matters: (1,\ 4)\ne(4,\ 1).
Reading: the reverse. Go straight up or down to the x-axis for x, then across to the y-axis for y. A point on an axis has a 0.
Signs → quadrant: I (+,+), II (-,+), III (-,-), IV (+,-). A 0 means on an axis, in no quadrant. Points can line up in a pattern.
