1

Writing an Equation from Slope and y-Intercept

In slope–intercept form y=mx+b, the slope is m and the y-intercept is b. When both are handed to you, writing the equation is a single step: substitute and simplify. Nothing to calculate. The y-intercept may be given as a value (b=-4) or as the point (0,b), such as (0,-4); either way only the number -4 goes into the equation.
Example 1

Substitute and simplify

Write the equation of each line.

(a) slope 5 and y-intercept 2
m=5, b=2, so y=mx+b gives
\boldsymbol{y=5x+2}
(b) slope -2 and y-intercept -7
m=-2, b=-7, so
y=-2x+(-7)
\boldsymbol{y=-2x-7}
!
Plus a negative is minus. y=-2x+(-7) tidies to y=-2x-7. Keep the sign with its number.

A point on the y-axis is b in disguise

If a point has x=0, it sits on the y-axis, so it is the y-intercept. Check which coordinate is the zero!

Example 2

Watch how the information is given

Write the equation of each line. Watch for the y-intercept given as a point.

(a) slope \dfrac{1}{3}, passing through (0,-4)
The point (0,-4) has x=0, so it is the y-intercept: b=-4.
\boldsymbol{y=\tfrac{1}{3}x-4}
(b) y-intercept 9 and slope -\dfrac{3}{4}
m=-\tfrac{3}{4}, b=9 (the slope is given second, but it is still m).
\boldsymbol{y=-\tfrac{3}{4}x+9}
!
Order doesn’t matter in the sentence; it matters in the equation. The slope always goes in front of x, and the y-intercept always goes on its own.
2

Writing an Equation from a Graph

Both ingredients can be read straight off a graph: the y-intercept is where the line crosses the vertical axis, and the slope is the rise over run counted between two lattice points (points where the line passes exactly through a grid corner, so both coordinates are integers), just as in Unit 6.
Example 3

Two lines on one grid

Each line passes through points with integer coordinates. State the slope and y-intercept, then write the equation.

Line p
Slope: 2 y-intercept: -4
Equation: y=2x-4
Lattice points (0,-4) and (2,0): m=\dfrac{0-(-4)}{2-0}=\dfrac{4}{2}=2, and p cuts the y-axis at -4. So \boldsymbol{y=2x-4}.
Line q
Slope: -\tfrac{1}{2} y-intercept: 3
Equation: y=-\tfrac{1}{2}x+3
Lattice points (0,3) and (2,2): m=\dfrac{2-3}{2-0}=-\dfrac{1}{2}, and q cuts the y-axis at 3. So \boldsymbol{y=-\tfrac{1}{2}x+3}.

It takes both numbers

Remember Red and Blue from the warm-up? Same b, different lines. A line is pinned down only when you know both m and b.

3

Writing an Equation from a Table of Values

A table hides the same two numbers. If the x-values go up by 1 each row, the slope is the constant change in y from row to row. The y-intercept is the y-value paired with x=0.
Example 4

Two tables

Determine the equation of the line represented by each table.

(a)
Slope: 3 y-intercept: 4
Equation: y=3x+4
x goes up by 1 and y goes up by 3 each row, so m=3; at x=0, y=4, so b=4.
\boldsymbol{y=3x+4}
Check: x=3\Rightarrow 3(3)+4=13 ✓
(b)
Slope: -2 y-intercept: 10
Equation: y=-2x+10
y drops by 2 for every 1 that x gains, so m=-2; at x=0, y=10, so b=10.
\boldsymbol{y=-2x+10}
Check: x=3\Rightarrow -2(3)+10=4 ✓
!
Going down means a negative slope. In (b) the y-values shrink by 2 each time, so the change is -2, not 2.
4

A Real-World Application

A story that names a starting amount and a constant rate of change is already in y=mx+b form: the starting value (before any time passes) is b, and the rate per time period is m.
Start words → b
starting balance, sign-up fee, flat fee, already contains, at first, initial
Rate words → m
per week, each hour, every month, for each, per kilometre
Example 5

Jaden’s savings

Jaden opens a savings account with a starting balance of $60. He deposits $12 every week and makes no withdrawals. Let B be the balance in dollars after w weeks.

(a) Identify the starting value and the constant rate of change, then write an equation for B in terms of w.
Starting value (at w=0): b=\$60.
Rate of change: m=\$12 per week.
\boldsymbol{B=12w+60}
(b) Use your equation to determine Jaden’s balance after 9 weeks.
B=12(9)+60
\phantom{B}=108+60
\phantom{B}=\boldsymbol{168}
After 9 weeks the balance is $168.
!
New letters, same form. B=12w+60 is y=mx+b with B in place of y and w in place of x. The rate still goes in front of the variable.

Where are m and b hiding?

Four ways the two numbers can arrive. Find them, then substitute into y=mx+b.

✓

Key ideas

Given m and b? Substitute into y=\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{m}x+\textcolor{#12805a}{b} and tidy: +(-7) is -7, 1x is x, +0 disappears. A point (0,b) is the y-intercept.
From a graph. b is where the line crosses the y-axis. Count rise over run between two lattice points for m.
From a table. With x going up by 1, the change in y is m. The y beside x=0 is b.
From a story. The starting amount is b; the rate per time period is m. Then substitute to answer questions: B=12(9)+60=168.
