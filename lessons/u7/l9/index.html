Slope in Context: Interpreting Rates of Change

In a real situation, every number in y=mx+b has a job. The slope is a rate of change with units: km per hour, dollars per lock, cm per minute. The y-intercept is the starting value. Today we read them, write them, use them, and compare two plans.

I can…
  • state the slope as a rate of change with units (units of y per unit of x) and say whether the quantity is increasing or decreasing
  • explain the y-intercept as the starting value (the value when x=0), with units
  • write an equation from a story (the rate is m, the start is b) and use it to find y, or to find x
  • find the rate from two data points, then solve for b
  • compare two situations and find the break-even point
RF3 Demonstrate an understanding of slope with respect to: rise and run; line segments and lines; rate of change; parallel lines; perpendicular lines.
RF4 Describe and represent linear relations, using: words; ordered pairs; tables of values; graphs; equations.
Warm-up

The Great Race

A made-up 60 m race. The rabbit is so fast, it gives the others a head start. The graph shows the first 2 minutes; the rest is in the fog.

Nobody changes speed. Guess first, then lift the fog.
1
How big is each racer’s head start?
Rabbit 0 m, turtle 30 m, snail 50 m: where each line starts on the d-axis.
2
How many metres does the rabbit run each minute?
10 m each minute (20 m in 2 min).
3
Whose line is steepest? Why?
The rabbit’s: it covers the most metres each minute. Steeper line = faster racer.
4
The turtle’s speed: ___ metres per ___.
5 metres per minute (10 m in 2 min). The snail: 2.5 m per minute.
!
The big one. Nobody changes speed. Who crosses the finish line first? Guess first.
A Rabbit
B Turtle
C Snail
D A tie
C: the snail! It needs only 10 more metres at 2.5 m per minute: 50+2.5t=60 gives t=4 min. Rabbit: 10t=60, t=6. Turtle: 30+5t=60, t=6.
+
Photo finish! Grab a ruler with your neighbour: who comes in second?
The rabbit and the turtle tie for second: both reach 60 m at exactly 6 minutes (10t=5t+30 gives t=6). A big head start wins early; a big speed wins later.
1

Slope as a Rate of Change

In y=mx+b the slope m tells how much y changes each time x increases by 1, so it is a rate of change. It must be stated with units: units of y per unit of x. Dollars against hours gives dollars per hour.
Positive slope
the quantity is increasing
Negative slope
the quantity is decreasing
Name all three
the quantity, the rate and the units, not just the number

Watch a story and its graph together: each step of 1 along the horizontal axis changes the quantity by the same amount. That amount is the slope.

!
Answer with a sentence that has units. “The distance increases by 70 km per hour”, not just “70”. A bare number doesn’t say what is changing, which way, or how fast.
Example 1

State the slope as a rate

State the slope, say what it represents with units, and say whether the quantity rises or falls.

(a) A courier’s distance is d=70t, where d is in kilometres and t is in hours.
m= 70  The distance is increasing by 70 km per hour.
m=70 (and b=0).
The distance grows by 70 km for every extra hour, so the slope is the courier’s speed: 70 kilometres per hour.
m>0, so the distance is increasing.
(b) A propane tank holds V=-6t+40, where V is in litres and t is in minutes since lighting.
m= −6  The propane is decreasing by 6 L per minute.
m=-6.
The tank loses 6 litres each minute: −6 litres per minute.
m<0, so the volume of propane is decreasing.

Say it as a sentence

A rate sentence has four blanks, and the equation with its units fills all of them: the sign of m gives the direction, its size gives the number, and the units of y and x finish it.

2

The y-Intercept as a Starting Value

The y-intercept b is the value of y when x=0: the amount before anything has happened. It is a starting amount, an initial value, or a flat fee charged no matter what. Unlike the slope it has no “per” in its units, only the ordinary unit of y.
Slope m: the rate. Units: units of y per unit of x (dollars per coffee).
y-intercept b: the start. Units: just the units of y (dollars).

To find the starting value, rewind the story to x=0, and watch where the graph meets the vertical axis.

Example 2

What does the y-intercept mean?

State the y-intercept, with units, and explain what it represents in the situation.

(a) A coffee card’s balance is B=-4n+60, where B is in dollars and n is the number of coffees purchased.
b=60, so the y-intercept is $60.
When n=0 no coffees have been bought yet, so $60 is the starting balance on the card.
(b) A tree’s height is H=20t+150, where H is in cm and t is the number of years since planting.
b=150, so the y-intercept is 150 cm.
At t=0 the tree was 150 cm tall: its height at the moment it was planted.
!
No “per” for b. The coffee card starts with $60, not “$60 per coffee”. Only the slope is a “per” number.
3

Writing and Using a Real-World Equation

1
Find the rate: that is m. It is usually signalled by “per” or “for every”.
2
Find the starting value: that is b, often a flat fee or base amount.
3
Write y=mx+b with letters that match the situation (C for cost, n for number…).
4
To answer a question, substitute x to get y, or substitute y and solve for x.
If no starting value is given but two data points are, find m from the two points first, then substitute one point and solve for b (the same method as Lesson 6).

Example 3

The locksmith

A locksmith charges a $35 service call fee, plus $20 for every lock changed.

(a) Write an equation for the total cost C in terms of the number of locks changed, n.
Rate: $20 per lock, so m=20.
Flat fee: $35, so b=35.
\boldsymbol{C=20n+35}
(b) Use your equation to determine the cost of changing 5 locks.
C=20(5)+35
=100+35
=\boldsymbol{\$135}
(c) A customer’s bill came to $175. How many locks were changed?
175=20n+35
140=20n
n=\boldsymbol{7} locks
Check: 20(7)+35=175 ✓

Use the equation forwards and backwards

Forwards, the equation turns a number of locks into a cost: substitute and calculate. Backwards, it answers “how many locks gave this bill?”: substitute the cost and undo the steps in reverse order.

Example 4

The burning candle: two data points

A candle burns at a constant rate. After 10 minutes it is 14 cm tall; after 25 minutes it is 8 cm tall.

(a) Determine the rate at which the candle is burning (the slope), including units.
Points (10,\ 14) and (25,\ 8):
m=\dfrac{8-14}{25-10}=\dfrac{-6}{15}=\boldsymbol{-0.4}
The candle burns down 0.4 cm per minute.
(b) Write an equation for the height H (in cm) in terms of the number of minutes, m, it has been burning.
H=-0.4m+b, and (10,\ 14) is on the line:
14=-0.4(10)+b
14=-4+b
b=18, so \boldsymbol{H=-0.4m+18}
(c) What was the height of the candle before it was lit?
Before lighting, m=0:
H=-0.4(0)+18=\boldsymbol{18} cm
(that is the y-intercept).
(d) How many minutes until the candle burns out completely (H=0)?
0=-0.4m+18
0.4m=18
m=\boldsymbol{45} minutes
!
Careful: here m means minutes. The letters match the story, so in H=-0.4m+18 the slope is -0.4 and m is the number of minutes.
!
Dividing by a decimal, no calculator. 0.4m=18: multiply both sides by 10 to get 4m=180, then m=45.
4

Comparing Two Linear Situations

The situation with the smaller y-intercept starts out cheaper…
…but the one with the smaller slope grows more slowly. So one option can win at first, and the other can win later.
Set the two equations equal and solve: that gives the break-even point, the x-value where both cost the same. On the graph it is where the two lines cross.
Example 5

Two rental companies

Two rental companies charge as below for a one-day rental, where d is kilometres driven:

Company A: C=0.20d+30Company B: C=0.10d+50
(a) State the slope and y-intercept of each equation, and explain what each means here.
Company A: m=0.20: it charges $0.20 for every kilometre driven; b=30: a $30 flat fee charged before any driving (d=0).
Company B: m=0.10: $0.10 per kilometre; b=50: a $50 flat fee.
B costs less per kilometre, but it starts $20 higher.
(b) On the grid, plot the y-intercept of each line, then use the slope to plot one more point for each company and sketch both lines.
Tap the grid to plot a point (drag to adjust, tap it again to remove it).
A: start at (0,\ 30), then up $20 for each 100 km, e.g. (400,\ 110).
B: start at (0,\ 50), then up $10 for each 100 km, e.g. (400,\ 90).
The two lines cross at (200,\ 70).
(c) Solve algebraically to determine the distance at which both companies charge the same amount.
0.20d+30=0.10d+50
0.10d+30=50
0.10d=20
d=\boldsymbol{200} km (both cost $70 there)
(d) For a 100 km trip, which company is cheaper? For a 300 km trip, which company is cheaper?
100 km: A =0.20(100)+30=\$50; B =0.10(100)+50=\$60, so Company A is cheaper.
300 km: A =0.20(300)+30=\$90; B =0.10(300)+50=\$80, so Company B is cheaper.
A wins below 200 km, B wins above it: exactly what the graph shows.
!
Decimals without a calculator. 0.20(100) is one fifth of 100, so 20. And 0.10d=20 means one tenth of d is 20: multiply both sides by 10 to get d=200.

Solving for the break-even point

Setting the two costs equal makes an equation with the variable on both sides. Keep it balanced: whatever you do to one side, do to the other.

Think Company C charges C=0.20d+50. Is Company C ever cheaper than Company A? How do their graphs compare?
Never. Both charge $0.20 per km (same slope), so the lines are parallel and never cross. C starts $20 higher and stays $20 higher at every distance: there is no break-even point.
✓

Key ideas

Slope = rate of change, with units: units of y per unit of x. Positive: increasing; negative: decreasing. d=70t: the distance increases by 70 km per hour.
y-intercept = starting value, the value when x=0. Units of y only, no “per”. B=-4n+60: the card starts with $60.
Write it and use it. Rate → m, start → b: C=20n+35. Substitute x to get y (5 locks: $135), or substitute y and solve (a $175 bill: 7 locks). Two data points? Find m first, then b.
Compare two plans. The smaller b wins at first; the smaller m wins later. Set them equal for the break-even point: 0.20d+30=0.10d+50 gives d=200 km.
