Writing the Equation of a Line Given Two Points

No slope given? Two points are enough. There is one extra step at the front: find the slope with the slope formula. After that it is Lesson 5 all over again: substitute one point, solve for b, write the equation. Today’s big idea: slope first, then b, then write it.

I can…
  • find the slope of the line through two points with m=\tfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}
  • substitute that slope and one of the points into y=mx+b and solve for b
  • explain why either point gives the same b, and check my equation with the other point
  • keep a fraction slope exact, in lowest terms
  • write an equation from two readings in a real situation, and explain what the slope and b mean

Warm-up

Candle Clues

A candle burns down at a steady rate. Someone snapped two photos of it: 20 cm tall after 2 hours, and 14 cm tall after 5 hours.

1
How many cm did the candle lose between Photo 1 and Photo 2?
20-14=6 cm.
2
How many hours passed? So how many cm does it burn each hour?
5-2=3 hours, so 6\div3=2 cm each hour.
3
How tall will it be after 6 hours?
14-2=12 cm (one more hour after Photo 2).
!
The big one. How tall was it when first lit? Guess first. Then: when is it all gone?
A 20 cm
B 22 cm
C 24 cm
D 26 cm
C, 24 cm. Rewind Photo 1 by 2 hours: 20+2+2=24 cm. From Photo 2: 14+5\times2=24 cm. Same answer!
All gone after 12 hours: 24\div2=12. (Not 7: 14\div2=7 is 7 more hours after Photo 2, so hour 5+7=12.)
A copied Photo 1 and forgot it had already burned 2 hours. B rewound only 1 hour. D added the whole 6 cm drop instead of 2 hours’ worth.
+
Partner check: one of you works back from Photo 1, the other from Photo 2. Same answer?
Both give 24 cm. Any point on the line leads back to the same start. Today you’ll see that either point gives the same b.

From Two Points to an Equation

Lesson 5’s method needs a slope to start, but many problems give only two points. The fix is one extra step at the front: use the slope formula from Unit 6 to turn the two points into a slope.

Stage 1Find the slopem=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}
→
Stage 2Find bSubstitute m and one point into y=mx+b; solve for b.
→
Stage 3Write itWrite y=mx+b, then check with the other point.
Walkthrough through (1,3) and (4,9): m=\tfrac{9-3}{4-1}=\tfrac{6}{3}=2, then 3=2(1)+b\Rightarrow b=1, so \boldsymbol{y=2x+1}. Check with the other point: 2(4)+1=9 ✓

Either point works

Either point may be used for the substitution: the value of b comes out the same, so there is no “right” one to pick. Both points are on the same line, and walking back from either one lands on the same y-intercept. Pick the point with the friendlier numbers.

Example 1

One stage at a time

Determine the equation of the line through (2,5) and (5,14), one stage at a time.

(a) Step 1: find the slope.
m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}=\dfrac{14-5}{5-2}
=\dfrac{9}{3}=\boldsymbol{3}
(b) Step 2: substitute m and one point into y=mx+b; solve for b.
Use (2,5): y=3x+b
5=3(2)+b\Rightarrow 5=6+b
b=\boldsymbol{-1}
(c) Write the final equation.
\boldsymbol{y=3x-1}
Check with the other point (5,14): 3(5)-1=14 ✓
2

Practising the Process

Run the same three stages every time, and keep asking: which stage am I in? Negative coordinates change only the arithmetic: substitute each coordinate with its sign, in brackets.

6-(-6)=6+6=12subtracting a negative is adding
2-(-4)=2+4=6brackets keep the sign safe
-8-4=-12going down: a negative rise

The rise is how far you travel from y_1 to y_2; the run is how far from x_1 to x_2. A number line shows both.

Which stage am I in? Finish with the other point

A good habit is to finish by substituting the point you did not use. Both given points must make the final equation true. If one doesn’t, go back and find the slip.

!
Two special cases. If the slope comes out 0, the line is horizontal: y=b. If the run is 0 (both x-values match), the slope is undefined and the line is vertical: x=k.
Example 2

A negative slope

Determine the equation, in the form y=mx+b, of the line through (-1,4) and (3,-8).
m=\dfrac{-8-4}{3-(-1)}=\dfrac{-12}{4}=-3
Use (-1,4): 4=-3(-1)+b\Rightarrow 4=3+b, so b=1.
\boldsymbol{y=-3x+1}
Check with (3,-8): -3(3)+1=-8 ✓
Example 3

Use one point, check with the other

Determine the equation of the line through (-4,-6) and (2,6).

(a) Use the point (-4,-6) to find the equation, in the form y=mx+b.
m=\dfrac{6-(-6)}{2-(-4)}=\dfrac{12}{6}=2
-6=2(-4)+b\Rightarrow -6=-8+b
b=2
\boldsymbol{y=2x+2}
(b) Check your equation by substituting the other point, (2,6), into it.
y=2(2)+2
=4+2=6 ✓
The point (2,6) satisfies the equation, so y=2x+2 is correct.
3

When the Slope Is a Fraction

Nothing in the method changes when \dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} does not divide evenly. Reduce the fraction and keep it exact in both the substitution and the final equation. Do not round it to a decimal.

!
A fraction times a number: divide by the bottom, multiply by the top. -\tfrac{1}{2}(-2): half of 2 is 1, and negative times negative is positive, so -\tfrac{1}{2}(-2)=+1.
Example 4

A fraction slope

Determine the equation, in the form y=mx+b, of the line through (-2,5) and (4,2).
m=\dfrac{2-5}{4-(-2)}=\dfrac{-3}{6}=-\tfrac{1}{2}
Use (-2,5): 5=-\tfrac{1}{2}(-2)+b\Rightarrow 5=1+b, so b=4.
\boldsymbol{y=-\tfrac{1}{2}x+4}
Check with (4,2): -\tfrac{1}{2}(4)+4=-2+4=2 ✓
4

A Real-World Application

Two points often arrive as two readings of the same quantity taken at different times. If the quantity changes at a constant rate, those two readings are enough.

Two readings → two points“10 cm on day 3” is the point (3,\ 10): time first.
Slope → rate of changeHow much the quantity changes per unit of time, e.g. cm per day.
b → starting valueThe value at time zero.
Example 5

A sunflower seedling

A sunflower seedling was 10 cm tall on day 3 and 25 cm tall on day 8 after planting. Assume constant growth; let h be the height (cm) after d days.

(a) Determine an equation for h in terms of d.
Points (3,10) and (8,25).
m=\dfrac{25-10}{8-3}=\dfrac{15}{5}=3
10=3(3)+b\Rightarrow b=1
\boldsymbol{h=3d+1}
(b) What do the slope and the h-intercept represent here?
Slope 3: the seedling grows 3 cm taller each day, its growth rate.
h-intercept 1: it was 1 cm tall on day 0, when it was planted.
(c) Predict the height on day 15.
h=3(15)+1
=45+1
On day 15 it is about 46 cm tall.
✓

Key ideas

Slope first. Two points give the slope: m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}. Same order on top and bottom, and brackets around negatives.
Then b, then write it. Substitute m and one point into y=mx+b, solve for b, and write the equation. Don’t stop at b.
Either point works. Both points give the same b. Use one, then check with the other: it must fit the equation too.
Fractions stay exact; stories have meaning. Reduce \tfrac{-3}{6}=-\tfrac{1}{2}, no decimals. In a story the slope is the rate (3 cm per day) and b is the start (1 cm on day 0).
