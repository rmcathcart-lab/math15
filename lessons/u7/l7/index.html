The General Form of a Linear Equation

A second way to write the same line: collect every term on one side, leaving zero. It is not a new kind of line. It is the same line in a different outfit, and you can always change it back to y=mx+b.

I can…
  • tell when an equation is in general form Ax+By+C=0: A, B, C are integers and A is positive
  • change y=mx+b into general form by moving every term to the left (and multiplying by -1 if A is negative)
  • clear fractions first by multiplying every term by the common denominator
  • change general form back into y=mx+b: isolate y, then divide every term by B, and read off the slope and y-intercept
  • find the x-intercept (put y=0) and the y-intercept (put x=0) straight from general form

1

Introducing General Form

A linear equation is in general form when every term has been collected on one side, leaving 0 on the other side.
General form → Ax+By+C=0
1
One side is 0.
2
A, B and C are integers (no fractions, no decimals).
3
A (the number in front of x) is positive.

It is not a new kind of line: y=2x-5 and 2x-y-5=0 describe the exact same line. Every point on the line makes the left side come out to exactly 0.

One line, many outfits

A line can be written with lots of different equations. Test two points to see which ones are the same line. Then the three rules pick out the one that is proper general form.

2

Slope–Intercept Form → General Form

Move every term to the left side so the right side becomes 0. A term that crosses the = sign changes its sign. If the result has A<0, multiply the whole equation by -1.

Walkthrough: y=3x-5\;\Rightarrow\;-3x+y+5=0\;\Rightarrow\;3x-y-5=0
i
Shortcut. Move the terms to the right side instead, so the x-term stays positive: y=3x-5 becomes 0=3x-y-5. Read it right to left: 3x-y-5=0. Same answer, no sign fix needed.
Example 1

Slope–intercept form to general form

Convert to general form Ax+By+C=0, with A, B, C integers and A positive.

(a) y=2x+5
Move every term to the left: -2x+y-5=0
A=-2 is negative, so multiply by -1:
\boldsymbol{2x-y+5=0}
(Shortcut: move terms right, 0=2x-y+5.)
(b) y=-4x+1
Move every term to the left:
4x+y-1=0
A=4 is already positive, so
\boldsymbol{4x+y-1=0}

When m or b is a fraction

General form has no fractions. So first multiply every term by the common denominator, so that all the numbers become integers. Then move the terms across.

Walkthrough: y=\dfrac{1}{2}x+4\;\xrightarrow{\times 2}\;2y=x+8\;\Rightarrow\;-x+2y-8=0\;\xrightarrow{\times(-1)}\;x-2y+8=0
!
Multiply every term, on both sides. y=\tfrac13x+2 times 3 is 3y=x+6, not 3y=x+2. The constant needs the 3 too.

Clear the fraction, then tidy up

The same machine, with one more station at the start. Check Example 2 here after you try it.

Example 2

Clear the fraction first

Convert each equation to general form, clearing any fractions first.

(a) y=\dfrac{1}{2}x+3
Clear the fraction, \times2:
2y=x+6
Move terms left: -x+2y-6=0
\times(-1): \boldsymbol{x-2y+6=0}
(b) y=-\dfrac{3}{4}x+2
Clear the fraction, \times4:
4y=-3x+8
Move terms left:
\boldsymbol{3x+4y-8=0}
3

General Form → Slope–Intercept Form

Start with Ax+By+C=0 and isolate y: move the Ax and C terms to the right side, then divide every term by B. The result is y=mx+b, so the slope and the y-intercept can be read off directly.

Walkthrough: 4x-y-1=0\;\Rightarrow\;-y=-4x+1\;\xrightarrow{\div(-1)}\;y=4x-1
Example 3

General form to slope–intercept form

Convert each equation from general form to slope–intercept form.

(a) 2x-y-7=0
Isolate the y term:
-y=-2x+7
\div(-1): \boldsymbol{y=2x-7}
slope 2, y-intercept -7
(b) 3x+y-9=0
Isolate y: move 3x and -9 across:
\boldsymbol{y=-3x+9}
slope -3, y-intercept 9

When the number in front of y is not 1 or -1

Dividing by B can leave a fraction for the slope. That is expected: just simplify it fully.

Walkthrough: 3x-4y+8=0\;\Rightarrow\;-4y=-3x-8\;\xrightarrow{\div(-4)}\;y=\dfrac{3}{4}x+2
!
Divide every term by B, signs included. Dividing by a negative number flips the sign of every term: -4y=-3x-8 divided by -4 is y=\tfrac34x+2.
Example 4

A fraction for the slope

Convert each equation to slope–intercept form. Simplify the slope fully.

(a) 5x-2y-6=0
-2y=-5x+6
\div(-2) on every term:
\boldsymbol{y=\tfrac{5}{2}x-3}
Check x=2: y=2; 5(2)-2(2)-6=0 ✓
(b) 4x+3y-12=0
3y=-4x+12
\div3 on every term:
\boldsymbol{y=-\tfrac{4}{3}x+4}
Check x=3: y=0; 4(3)+3(0)-12=0 ✓
4

Finding Intercepts Directly from General Form

The x-intercept is where the line crosses the x-axis. There, y=0.
The y-intercept is where the line crosses the y-axis. There, x=0.

Substitute straight into the general-form equation. No converting needed.

Walkthrough: for 2x+y-8=0: y=0 gives 2x-8=0, so x=4 and the x-intercept is (4,0). x=0 gives y-8=0, so the y-intercept is (0,8). The same idea works backwards: if a point is on the line, substitute it to find a missing coefficient.
i
Two intercepts are two points. Plot them and join them with a ruler, and you have graphed the line. This is often the fastest way to graph a line in general form.
Example 5

Both intercepts, no converting

Find the x-intercept and the y-intercept without converting to slope–intercept form.

(a) 3x+2y-12=0
y=0: 3x-12=0\Rightarrow x=4
x-intercept \boldsymbol{(4,0)}
x=0: 2y-12=0\Rightarrow y=6
y-intercept \boldsymbol{(0,6)}
(b) 4x-y-8=0
y=0: 4x-8=0\Rightarrow x=2
x-intercept \boldsymbol{(2,0)}
x=0: -y-8=0\Rightarrow y=-8
y-intercept \boldsymbol{(0,-8)}
✓

Key ideas

General form Ax+By+C=0. Every term on one side, 0 on the other. A, B, C are integers and A is positive. Same line, different outfit.
To general form. Fractions? Multiply every term by the common denominator. Move every term to the left (each one changes sign). A negative? Multiply by -1.
Back to y=mx+b. Move the x-term and the constant to the right, then divide every term by the number in front of y. Simplify the slope fully.
Intercepts. x-intercept: put y=0 and solve. y-intercept: put x=0 and solve. Write them as points, (x,0) and (0,y).
