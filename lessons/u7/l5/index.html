Writing the Equation of a Line Given Slope and a Point

Last lesson the y-intercept was handed to you. Today you get the slope and some other point. Every point on a line makes y=mx+b true, so put the point in and b is the only thing left to find. Today’s big idea: substitute, solve for b, then write the equation.

Warm-up

The Piggy Bank

Maya saves in a glass jar. She adds $5 every week and never takes any money out. You only get to peek in week 6: the jar holds $47.

1
How much is in the jar at week 7?
$52: one more week adds $5, 47+5=52.
2
How much was in it at week 5?
$42: rewinding one week takes the $5 back out, 47-5=42.
3
By week 6, how many times has Maya added $5?
6 times (weeks 1 to 6).
!
The big one. How much was in the jar at the start (week 0)? Guess first.
A $0
B $17
C $42
D $47
B, $17. Rewind 6 weeks, taking $5 out each time: 47\to42\to37\to32\to27\to22\to17. All at once: 47-6\times5=17.
C rewinds only one week. D forgets Maya was adding money at all. A assumes the jar must have started empty.
+
Ask your neighbour: in what week can Maya buy $97 headphones?
Week 16. She needs 97-47=50 more dollars, which is 50\div5=10 more weeks after week 6.
1

The Substitute-and-Solve Method

Sometimes we are given the slope and some other point, not the y-intercept. Every point on a line makes y=mx+b true. So when we substitute the point, b is the only unknown left.

The method
  1. 1
    Write y=mx+b with the given slope in place of m.
  2. 2
    Substitute the point: its x-coordinate for x, its y-coordinate for y.
  3. 3
    Solve for \textcolor{#12805a}{b}.
  4. 4
    Rewrite the equation using m and that b.
Walkthrough m=3 through (2,5): y=3x+b\ \Rightarrow\ 5=3(2)+b\ \Rightarrow\ 5=6+b\ \Rightarrow\ b=-1, so \boldsymbol{y=3x-1}
!
Two classic slips. Stopping at b=-1 instead of writing the final equation y=3x-1. And putting the coordinates into the wrong variables: in (2,5) the first number is x and the second is y.

Substitute and solve, step by step

Watch the slope and the point drop into y=mx+b. Then solve for b and write the equation.

Example 1

Slope and a point

Determine the equation of each line from its slope and the given point.

(a) slope 4, point (1,9)
y=4x+b
9=4(1)+b\ \Rightarrow\ 9=4+b
b=5
\boldsymbol{y=4x+5}
(b) slope 2, point (5,7)
y=2x+b
7=2(5)+b\ \Rightarrow\ 7=10+b
b=-3
\boldsymbol{y=2x-3}

Check it on the graph: walk back to the y-axis

Remember rewinding the piggy bank? Each step back along a line goes left 1 and changes y by the slope. Walk back to x=0 and you land on \textcolor{#12805a}{b}, the same b the algebra gave.

Two classic slips, and how a check catches them

The last step of every question: put the point back into your final equation. If it doesn’t come out right, something slipped.

2

Negative Slopes and Negative Coordinates

The method does not change when signs go negative: only the arithmetic does. Substitute each coordinate with its sign, in brackets: m=-3 through (2,9) gives 9=-3(2)+b.

-3(2)=-6different signs: negative
2(-4)=-8different signs: negative
-3(-2)=+6same signs: positive

Then b is the jump from mx to y. A number line makes the jump easy to see.

!
Undo with the opposite. In 9=-6+b, the -6 is subtracted from b, so add 6 to both sides: b=9+6=15. In -2=5+b, subtract 5: b=-2-5=-7.
Example 2

A negative slope, a negative coordinate

Determine the equation of each line.

(a) slope -3, point (2,9)
y=-3x+b
9=-3(2)+b\ \Rightarrow\ 9=-6+b
b=15
\boldsymbol{y=-3x+15}
(b) slope 5, point (1,-2)
y=5x+b
-2=5(1)+b\ \Rightarrow\ -2=5+b
b=-7
\boldsymbol{y=5x-7}
Example 3

Both coordinates negative

Determine the equation of each line. Both coordinates are negative.

(a) slope 2, point (-4,-5)
y=2x+b
-5=2(-4)+b\ \Rightarrow\ -5=-8+b
b=3
\boldsymbol{y=2x+3}
(b) slope -3, point (-2,-1)
y=-3x+b
-1=-3(-2)+b\ \Rightarrow\ -1=6+b
b=-7
\boldsymbol{y=-3x-7}
3

Fractional Slopes

A fraction slope is no harder. The point is chosen so that m\cdot x comes out whole: \tfrac{1}{2}(8)=4. To find a fraction of a number, divide by the bottom, then multiply by the top.

!
Keep the fraction. Write y=\tfrac{1}{2}x+3, not y=0.5x+3. Don’t round the slope to a decimal.
Example 4

A fraction for the slope

Determine the equation of each line. Leave the slope as a fraction.

(a) slope \dfrac{1}{2}, point (8,7)
y=\tfrac{1}{2}x+b
7=\tfrac{1}{2}(8)+b\ \Rightarrow\ 7=4+b
b=3
\boldsymbol{y=\tfrac{1}{2}x+3}
(b) slope -\dfrac{3}{4}, point (4,-1)
y=-\tfrac{3}{4}x+b
-1=-\tfrac{3}{4}(4)+b\ \Rightarrow\ -1=-3+b
b=2
\boldsymbol{y=-\tfrac{3}{4}x+2}
4

A Real-World Application

Many situations give a constant rate of change (that’s the slope) plus one data point that is not the starting value. The starting value is exactly the b we solve for.

Rate of change → slope m“$5 every week”, “20 m per minute”. Going down (descending, cooling, shrinking)? The slope is negative.
One data point → (x,\ y)“After 5 minutes it is at 350 m” is the point (5,\ 350): time first.
Starting value → bThe value at time 0. This is what we solve for.
Example 5

A hot air balloon

A hot air balloon descends at a constant rate. After 5 minutes of descent it is at a height of 350 m, and it continues descending at 20 m per minute. Let h be the height (in metres) after t minutes.

(a) Identify the slope and the known point, then write an equation for h in terms of t.
Descending, so m=-20; the point is (5,\ 350).
350=-20(5)+b
350=-100+b
b=450
\boldsymbol{h=-20t+450}
(b) What was the balloon’s height at t=0?
h=-20(0)+450=450
The balloon started at a height of 450 m.
(c) Predict the height after 12 minutes.
h=-20(12)+450
=-240+450
After 12 min it is at 210 m.
✓

Key ideas

Substitute, solve, rewrite. Put the slope into y=mx+b, put the point in (x with x, y with y), solve for b, then write the equation. Don’t stop at b.
Signs go in brackets. -3(2)=-6, 2(-4)=-8, -3(-2)=6. Then undo mx with the opposite to get b.
Fractions stay fractions. \tfrac{1}{2}(8)=4: divide by the bottom, multiply by the top. The answer is y=\tfrac{1}{2}x+3, not a decimal.
b is the start. Walk back to x=0 and you land on b. In a story it is the value at time 0: the balloon started at 450 m. Always check by putting the point back in.
