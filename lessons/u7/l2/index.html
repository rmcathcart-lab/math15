Slope–Intercept Form: y=mx+b

When a linear equation is written y=\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{m}x+\textcolor{#12805a}{b}, it tells you two things right away: m, how much y changes every step, and b, where the line starts on the y-axis.

  • read the slope m and the y-intercept b straight off y=mx+b, including y=x, y=-x, y=7x and y=-9

1

Slope–Intercept Form

A linear equation solved for y can be written y=\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{m}x+\textcolor{#12805a}{b} called slope–intercept form.
m is the slope: the constant change in y each time x increases by 1.
b is the y-intercept: the y-value where the line crosses the y-axis, the point (0,\ b).

So for y=\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{2}x+\textcolor{#12805a}{3}: m=2, so y increases by 2 each time x increases by 1. And b=3, so the line passes through (0,\ 3).

The warm-up again: each plan is a line

Call the cost y and the GB x. Then every plan is y=mx+b: the fee is b, where the line starts, and the cost per GB is m, how steeply it climbs.

Example 1

What m and b tell you

State m and b, then complete each sentence.

(a) y=4x+7
m= 4 b= 7
Every time x increases by 1, y increases by 4.
The line crosses the y-axis at (0,\ 7).
m=\boldsymbol{4}, b=\boldsymbol{7}. y increases by 4; the line crosses at \boldsymbol{(0,\ 7)}.
The coefficient of x is the slope; the constant term is the y-intercept.
(b) y=-5x+1
m= -5 b= 1
Every time x increases by 1, y decreases by 5.
The line crosses the y-axis at (0,\ 1).
m=\boldsymbol{-5}, b=\boldsymbol{1}. y decreases by 5; the line crosses at \boldsymbol{(0,\ 1)}.
A negative slope means y goes down as x goes up.
2

Reading m and b Off an Equation

If the equation is already y=mx+b, the coefficient of x is m and the constant term is b. Each one keeps its sign. Three special cases:

y=x-8 and y=-x+4
A bare x means m=1. A bare -x means m=-1.
y=7x
No constant term means b=0: the line goes through the origin.
y=-9
A number alone means m=0: a horizontal line.
!
The sign belongs to the number. In y=-3x+2, m=-3, not 3. In y=x\mathbin{\textcolor{#12805a}{-}}\textcolor{#12805a}{8}, b=-8, not 8.
Example 2

Read the slope and the y-intercept

State the slope and y-intercept of each linear equation.

(a) y=5x+6
Coefficient of x: \boldsymbol{m=5}
Constant term: \boldsymbol{b=6}
(b) y=-3x+2
The sign belongs to m: \boldsymbol{m=-3}
\boldsymbol{b=2}
(c) y=x-8
A bare x means \boldsymbol{m=1}
The term -8 gives \boldsymbol{b=-8}
(d) y=-x+4
“-x” means \boldsymbol{m=-1}
\boldsymbol{b=4}
(e) y=7x
\boldsymbol{m=7}
No constant term, so \boldsymbol{b=0}: through the origin.
(f) y=-9
No x-term, so \boldsymbol{m=0} (horizontal line)
\boldsymbol{b=-9}
3

Rearranging Into Slope–Intercept Form

You can’t read m and b until the equation is solved for y: 2x+y=7 is not yet in slope–intercept form. Get y alone with inverse operations, then write the x-term first:

2x+y=7\ \Rightarrow\ y=7-2x\ \Rightarrow\ y=\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{-2}x+\textcolor{#12805a}{7}, so m=-2 and b=7.
!
Each term keeps its own sign when it moves to the front. y=7-2x becomes y=-2x+7, not y=2x+7.
Example 3

Get y alone first

Rearrange each equation into slope–intercept form, then state m and b.

(a) x+y=9
x+y=9
y=9-x (subtract x)
\boldsymbol{y=-x+9}
\boldsymbol{m=-1}, \boldsymbol{b=9}
(b) 5x+y=2
5x+y=2
y=2-5x (subtract 5x)
\boldsymbol{y=-5x+2}
\boldsymbol{m=-5}, \boldsymbol{b=2}
(c) -3x+y=6
-3x+y=6
y=6+3x (add 3x)
\boldsymbol{y=3x+6}
\boldsymbol{m=3}, \boldsymbol{b=6}
4

When y Has a Coefficient

If y has a coefficient other than 1, move the x-term first, then divide every term (not just the first one) by that coefficient:

2y=6x+10\ \Rightarrow\ \dfrac{2y}{2}=\dfrac{6x}{2}+\dfrac{10}{2}\ \Rightarrow\ y=\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{3}x+\textcolor{#12805a}{5}, so m=3 and b=5.
Example 4

Divide every term

Rearrange each equation into slope–intercept form, then state m and b.

(a) 3y=9x+12
Divide every term by 3:
\dfrac{3y}{3}=\dfrac{9x}{3}+\dfrac{12}{3}
\boldsymbol{y=3x+4}
\boldsymbol{m=3}, \boldsymbol{b=4}
(b) 4x+2y=10
2y=-4x+10 (subtract 4x)
Divide every term by 2:
\boldsymbol{y=-2x+5}
\boldsymbol{m=-2}, \boldsymbol{b=5}
(c) 9x+3y=21
3y=-9x+21 (subtract 9x)
Divide every term by 3:
\boldsymbol{y=-3x+7}
\boldsymbol{m=-3}, \boldsymbol{b=7}

Checking Tables and Interpreting m and b

To test a table against y=mx+b, substitute each x-value and compare with the table’s y. One row that fails means the table does not match.

In a real situation: b is the start, m is the rate

In a real situation, b is usually the starting amount and m is the rate: the change for each additional unit.

Example 6

The landscaping company

A landscaping company charges \text{cost}=35+20h, where h is hours worked and cost is in dollars.

(a) Identify the starting cost (b) and explain what it represents.
\boldsymbol{b=35}. It is the flat call-out fee of $35, charged before any hours are worked (h=0).
(b) Identify the cost per hour (m) and explain what it represents.
\boldsymbol{m=20}. The cost rises by $20 for each extra hour of work.
(c) Determine the total cost of a 4-hour job.
\text{cost}=35+20(4)
=35+80
=\boldsymbol{\$115}
✓

Key ideas

Slope–intercept form y=\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{m}x+\textcolor{#12805a}{b}. m is the slope: the change in y each time x goes up by 1. b is the y-intercept: the line crosses at (0,\ b).
Read it off. Each number keeps its sign: y=-3x+2 has m=-3, b=2. Special cases: y=x (m=1), y=-x (m=-1), y=7x (b=0), y=-9 (m=0).
Solve for y first. 5x+y=2\Rightarrow y=-5x+2. If y has a coefficient, divide every term: 2y=6x+10\Rightarrow y=3x+5.
Tables and real life. One row that fails means the table doesn’t match. In \text{cost}=35+20h, b=35 is the start (the fee) and m=20 is the rate ($20 per hour).
