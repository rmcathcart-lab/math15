Factoring Trinomials x^2+bx+c, Part 1: c Positive

FOIL turns (x+2)(x+9) into x^2+11x+18. Today we run FOIL backwards: start with the trinomial and find the two brackets. The secret is two numbers that multiply to c and add to b, just like the diamond puzzles.

From FOIL Back to Factors

FOIL turns (x+2)(x+9) into x^2+9x+2x+18=x^2+11x+18. Factoring runs that in reverse: start from x^2+11x+18 and find your way back to (x+2)(x+9), without testing every pair. The trick is to notice exactly where the 11 and the 18 came from.

(x+2)(x+9)
expanding →
← factoring
x^2+11x+18

A picture: algebra tiles make a rectangle

An x^2-tile is a square, an x-tile is a long strip, and a unit tile is a small square. The tiles for a trinomial fit together into a rectangle. Its length and width are the two brackets.

Example 1

Expand, then look for the pattern

Expand each product. Compare the coefficient of x (that is b) and the constant (that is c) to the two numbers in the binomials.

(a) (x+2)(x+6)
x^2+6x+2x+12
=\boldsymbol{x^2+8x+12}
2+6=8, 2\times6=12
(b) (x+4)(x+5)
x^2+5x+4x+20
=\boldsymbol{x^2+9x+20}
4+5=9, 4\times5=20
(c) (x+1)(x+8)
x^2+8x+x+8
=\boldsymbol{x^2+9x+8}
1+8=9, 1\times8=8
(d) (x+3)(x+3)
x^2+3x+3x+9
=\boldsymbol{x^2+6x+9}
3+3=6, 3\times3=9
Think What do you notice about the connection between b and the two numbers? Between c and the two numbers?
Every time, b is the sum of the two numbers in the brackets and c is their product. So factoring means hunting for two numbers that multiply to c and add to b.
2

The Sum–Product Rule

In general (x+p)(x+q)=x^2+(p+q)x+pq. Comparing with x^2+bx+c gives b=p+q and c=pq.

To factor x^2+bx+c, find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b. Those numbers are p and q, and x^2+bx+c=(x+p)(x+q).
(x+p)(x+q)
expanding →
← factoring
x^2+(p+q)x+pq

Finding the two numbers: list the pairs

Guessing works for small numbers. A list never misses: write the pairs that multiply to the product, starting from 1, and add each pair until one gives the sum. It is a diamond puzzle with the work shown.

Factoring When b and c Are Both Positive

If c is positive, the two numbers have the same sign. If b is also positive, that sign must be positive: two positive numbers give a positive product and a positive sum. So for x^2+7x+12, look for two positive numbers with product 12 and sum 7.

1Diamond: c on top, b on the bottom.
2List the pairs that multiply to c.
3Pick the pair that adds to b.
4Write the brackets, then check by expanding.

Factoring When b Is Negative and c Is Positive

With c positive the two numbers still share a sign, but a negative b forces both to be negative. For x^2-8x+15 we need product 15 and sum -8. That is -3 and -5, so x^2-8x+15=(x-3)(x-5).

Rule of thumb: c positive \Rightarrow both numbers have the same sign, and that sign matches b.

both  positive   x^2+7x+12: 3, 4

!
Same numbers, different signs. x^2+7x+12=(x+3)(x+4) but x^2-7x+12=(x-3)(x-4). Only the sign of b changed, so only the signs in the brackets change.

Factoring with both numbers negative

Same steps as before. List the pairs that multiply to c, make them both negative, and find the pair that adds to b.

5

Checking Your Answer, and Common Factors

Check any factoring by expanding it back with FOIL. If you get the original trinomial, you are correct. Factoring is expanding run backwards, so the check runs it forwards.

A quick second check: put x=2 into both forms. They must give the same number. For x^2+13x+30=(x+3)(x+10): 2x²+13×2+30= and (2+3)×(2+10)= both give 60 ✓ (Use 2 or 3, not 0 or 1.)

Common factor first

If the terms share a common factor, remove it first. Factoring is not finished until nothing more can be factored.
!
Keep the common factor in your answer. 3x^2+15x+18=3(x+2)(x+3). Writing only (x+2)(x+3) loses the 3, and that is a different trinomial.

Key ideas

Backwards FOIL: (x+p)(x+q)=x^2+(p+q)x+pq. So b is the sum of the two numbers and c is their product.
The search: list the pairs that multiply to c, then pick the pair that adds to b. x^2+11x+18: 2 and 9, so (x+2)(x+9).
Signs: c positive means the same sign, and it matches b. x^2+7x+12=(x+3)(x+4); x^2-7x+12=(x-3)(x-4).
Common factor first, then the pair: 3x^2+27x+60=3(x+4)(x+5). Always check by expanding.
