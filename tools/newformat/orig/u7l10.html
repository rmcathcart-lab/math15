Unit Review: Equations of Lines

One reminder and one worked example for every topic in Unit 7, then mixed challenges for the test. One idea ties it all together: every line comes down to its slope m and its y-intercept b. Whatever you are given, find m, then find b.

I can…
  • graph a line from a table of values, or from its slope and y-intercept
  • read m and b from y=mx+b, rearranging first when the equation is not solved for y
  • write the equation of a line from m and b, from m and a point, or from two points
  • convert between slope–intercept form and general form, and find the intercepts
  • recognize horizontal (y=k) and vertical (x=k) lines
  • explain slope and y-intercept in a real situation, and use parallel and perpendicular slopes
RF7 Determine the equation of a linear relation, given: a graph; a point and the slope; two points; a point and the equation of a parallel or perpendicular line, to solve problems.
RF6 Relate linear relations expressed in: slope–intercept form (y=mx+b); general form (Ax+By+C=0); slope–point form (y-y_1=m(x-x_1)) to their graphs.
RF5 Determine the characteristics of the graphs of linear relations, including the: intercepts; slope; domain; range.

Graphing From a Table of Values

Substitute several x-values into the equation, solve for y, plot the ordered pairs, then draw one straight line through them across the whole grid.
Example 1

Table, then graph

For y=3x-2: (a) complete the table of values; (b) plot the points and draw the line across the grid.

(a) Complete the table.
x-2-1012
y-8-5-214
x=-2: y=3(-2)-2=-8; x=-1: -5; x=0: -2; x=1: 1; x=2: 4.
The y-values go up by 3 each time.
(b) Plot the points and draw the line.
Tap the grid to plot a point (drag to adjust, tap it again to remove it).
Plot (-2,\ -8), (-1,\ -5), (0,\ -2), (1,\ 1), (2,\ 4) and rule one line through them, right across the grid.
2

Slope–Intercept Form Basics

In y=\textcolor{#1f5bd6}{m}x+\textcolor{#12805a}{b}, m is the slope and b is the y-intercept. If the equation is not solved for y, isolate y first.
Example 2

State m and b

State m and b, rearranging first where needed.

(a) State the slope and y-intercept of y=-5x+6.
Already in y=mx+b form.
slope \boldsymbol{m=-5}
y-intercept \boldsymbol{b=6}, that is (0,\ 6)
(b) Rearrange 4x+y=9 into slope–intercept form, then state m and b.
4x+y=9
y=-4x+9 (subtract 4x)
\boldsymbol{m=-4}, \boldsymbol{b=9}
3

Graphing Using Slope–Intercept Form

Plot (0,\ b) first, then write m=\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} and step right by the run and up or down by the rise to get a second point.
Example 3

Graph y=-3x+4

Graph the line y=-3x+4.

(a) State the slope and y-intercept.
slope \boldsymbol{m=-3}=\dfrac{-3}{1} (down 3, right 1)
y-intercept \boldsymbol{b=4}, that is (0,\ 4)
(b) Plot the y-intercept, use the slope to plot a second point, then draw the line across the grid.
From (0,\ 4) go right 1 and down 3 to (1,\ 1), then rule the line right across the grid.
Tap the grid to plot a point.
4

Writing an Equation From Slope and y-Intercept

With m and b both known, substitute them straight into y=mx+b: one step, no solving.
Example 4

Straight into y=mx+b

Write the equation of each line.

(a) slope 6, y-intercept -3
m=6, b=-3 into y=mx+b:
\boldsymbol{y=6x-3}
(b) slope -\dfrac{1}{4}, y-intercept 8
m=-\tfrac{1}{4}, b=8:
\boldsymbol{y=-\tfrac{1}{4}x+8}
!
Keep the signs. b=-3 makes y=6x-3, not y=6x+3.
5

Writing an Equation From Slope and a Point

When the given point is not the y-intercept: put m into y=mx+b, substitute the point, solve for b, then rewrite the equation.
Example 5

Slope and a point

Determine the equation of each line.

(a) slope 4 through (3,\ 7)
y=4x+b
7=4(3)+b\ \Rightarrow\ 7=12+b
b=-5
\boldsymbol{y=4x-5}
(b) slope -2 through (-1,\ 6)
y=-2x+b
6=-2(-1)+b\ \Rightarrow\ 6=2+b
b=4
\boldsymbol{y=-2x+4}
!
Brackets around the x-value. -2(-1)=+2. Write the bracket every time, especially with negatives.
6

Writing an Equation From Two Points

Two points, no slope. Step 1: find m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}. Step 2: substitute m and either point into y=mx+b and solve for b.
Example 6

Two points

Determine the equation, in the form y=mx+b, of the line through (1,\ 4) and (4,\ 13).

Step 1 m=\dfrac{13-4}{4-1}=\dfrac{9}{3}=3
Step 2 y=3x+b; using (1,\ 4): 4=3(1)+b\ \Rightarrow\ 4=3+b\ \Rightarrow\ b=1
\boldsymbol{y=3x+1}
Check with the other point: 3(4)+1=13 ✓
7

General Form

General form is Ax+By+C=0 with integer A, B, C and A positive. Move terms across the equal sign (clearing fractions first) to convert. Find the intercepts by setting y=0 or x=0.
Example 7

Convert, and read the intercepts

Convert between forms and read intercepts directly.

(a) Convert y=5x-2 to general form.
y=5x-2
0=5x-y-2
\boldsymbol{5x-y-2=0}
A=5>0, all integers ✓
(b) Convert 3x-2y-8=0 to slope–intercept form.
-2y=-3x+8
divide by -2:
\boldsymbol{y=\tfrac{3}{2}x-4}
(c) Determine the x- and y-intercepts of 2x+3y-12=0 without converting.
y=0: 2x-12=0, x=6
x-intercept \boldsymbol{(6,\ 0)}
x=0: 3y-12=0, y=4
y-intercept \boldsymbol{(0,\ 4)}
!
Divide every term. In (b), dividing -2y=-3x+8 by -2 changes both signs on the right: \tfrac{-3}{-2}=\tfrac32 and \tfrac{8}{-2}=-4.
8

Horizontal Lines, Vertical Lines, and Any Form

Horizontal: y=k. Every point has the same y. Slope 0.
Vertical: x=k. Every point has the same x. Slope undefined.
For general form, the two-intercept method is usually the fastest graph: find both intercepts, plot them, join them.
Example 8

Which kind of line?

Describe each graph, then find the two points you would plot in part (c).

(a) Is y=3 horizontal or vertical? Give one point on it.
Horizontal: y is stuck at 3 for every x (slope 0).
For example \boldsymbol{(0,\ 3)}
(b) Is x=-4 horizontal or vertical? Give one point on it.
Vertical: x is stuck at -4 for every y (slope undefined).
For example \boldsymbol{(-4,\ 0)}
(c) Determine the x- and y-intercepts of 3x-y-3=0 for the two-intercept method.
y=0: 3x-3=0, x=1: \boldsymbol{(1,\ 0)}
x=0: -y-3=0, y=-3: \boldsymbol{(0,\ -3)}
Plot those two and join them.
9

Slope in Context

In a real-world linear model the slope is the constant rate of change, with units (“per”).
The y-intercept is the starting value: a base fee, a starting amount.
Example 9

A ride-share fare

A ride-share app charges a $4 base fee plus $2.50 per kilometre. Let F be the fare (dollars) for a trip of k kilometres.

(a) Write an equation for F in terms of k.
fare = rate × km + base fee
\boldsymbol{F=2.50k+4}
(b) State what the slope and the F-intercept represent here.
Slope 2.50: the fare grows by $2.50 for each extra kilometre.
F-intercept 4: the $4 base fee, charged before any distance is driven.
(c) Determine the fare for a 12 km trip.
F=2.50(12)+4
=30+4
A 12 km trip costs $34.
!
No calculator: 2.50(12) is 12 lots of $2.50: 12\times2=24 plus 12\times0.50=6, so 30.

Mixed Review: Putting It All Together

Each challenge below combines several skills from the unit, exactly like a test question.

Challenge 1

Filling the pool

A backyard pool already contains 300 L of water, and a hose adds water at 60 L per minute.

(a) Write an equation for the volume V (litres) after t minutes.
rate m=60, start b=300
\boldsymbol{V=60t+300}
(b) Determine the volume after 8 minutes.
V=60(8)+300
=480+300
After 8 min there is 780 L.
(c) How many minutes until the pool reaches 1\,500 L?
1\,500=60t+300
1\,200=60t
t=\boldsymbol{20} minutes
Challenge 2

The weather balloon

A weather balloon was at 450 m after 3 minutes and at 1\,050 m after 7 minutes, rising at a constant rate.

(a) Determine an equation for the altitude A (metres) in terms of the minutes t since launch.
Points (3,\ 450) and (7,\ 1\,050).
m=\dfrac{1\,050-450}{7-3}=\dfrac{600}{4}=150
450=150(3)+b\ \Rightarrow\ b=0
\boldsymbol{A=150t}
(b) State what the slope represents in this situation.
The balloon climbs 150 m every minute: its constant rate of ascent.
(c) Predict the altitude after 15 minutes.
A=150(15)
After 15 min it is at 2\,250 m.
Challenge 3

Parallel and perpendicular (back to Unit 6)

Segment AB joins A(-2,\ -3) and B(1,\ 3). This question connects back to Unit 6.

(a) Determine the slope of AB.
m=\dfrac{3-(-3)}{1-(-2)}=\dfrac{6}{3}
\boldsymbol{m=2}
(b) Line L_1 is parallel to AB through P(3,\ -1). Determine its equation in slope–intercept form.
Parallel, so m=2.
-1=2(3)+b\ \Rightarrow\ -1=6+b
b=-7
\boldsymbol{y=2x-7}
(c) Line L_2 is perpendicular to AB through Q(-4,\ 5). Determine its equation in general form.
Perpendicular, so m=-\tfrac{1}{2}.
5=-\tfrac{1}{2}(-4)+b\ \Rightarrow\ 5=2+b, b=3
y=-\tfrac{1}{2}x+3\ \Rightarrow\ 2y=-x+6
\boldsymbol{x+2y-6=0}
✓

Key ideas

Every line comes down to m and b. In y=mx+b: plot (0,\ b), then step the rise and run. A table goes up by the same amount each step.
Find m, then find b. Given m and b: write it. Given m and a point: substitute, solve for b. Given two points: slope formula first.
General form Ax+By+C=0: integers, A positive. Isolate y to read m and b; set x=0 or y=0 for the intercepts. y=k is flat, x=k is vertical.
In context, the slope is a rate with units and b is the start. Parallel lines: same slope. Perpendicular: flip it and change the sign.
