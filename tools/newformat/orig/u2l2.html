The Quotient Law & Power of a Power

Dividing powers subtracts exponents; raising a power to a power multiplies them. Both laws come from the same idea as last day: write out the factors and count.

I can…
  • use the Quotient Law a^{m}\div a^{n}=a^{m-n}: same base, subtract the exponents
  • divide terms with coefficients: divide the numbers, subtract the exponents
  • use the Power of a Power Law \left(a^{m}\right)^{n}=a^{mn}: multiply the exponents
  • tell which law to use: add, subtract or multiply?
  • simplify expressions that need both laws, brackets first
AN3 Demonstrate an understanding of powers with integral and rational exponents.

1

The Quotient Law

Recall the Product Law: a^{m}\times a^{n}=a^{m+n}, so 4^{2}\times4^{3}=4^{5}=1\,024. Division works the same way, but with subtraction. Expanding,

2^{5}\div2^{2}=\dfrac{2\times2\times2\times2\times2}{2\times2}=2\times2\times2=2^{3}=8,\qquad 5-2=3

Quotient Law  a^{m}\div a^{n}=\dfrac{a^{m}}{a^{n}}=a^{m-n}\quad(a\neq0)
Keep the base, subtract the exponents (top minus bottom). The bases must match.
!
Keep the brackets on a negative base. In \dfrac{(-3)^{8}}{(-3)^{5}} the base is -3, so the answer is (-3)^{3}, and three negative factors make it negative.
Example 1

Same base: subtract the exponents

Use the Quotient Law to simplify.

(a) 7^{5}\div7^{2}
7^{5-2}=\boldsymbol{7^{3}}
=343
(b) \dfrac{(-3)^{8}}{(-3)^{5}}
(-3)^{8-5}=\boldsymbol{(-3)^{3}}
=-27
(c) x^{9}\div x^{4}
x^{9-4}=\boldsymbol{x^{5}}
(d) \dfrac{y^{10}}{y^{6}}
y^{10-6}=\boldsymbol{y^{4}}
2

Quotient Law with Coefficients

When a coefficient sits in front, handle the coefficients and the powers separately: divide the coefficients normally, then subtract the exponents.

8y^{6}\div2y^{2}=(8\div2)\left(y^{6}\div y^{2}\right)=4y^{4}

!
Numbers divide; exponents subtract. Don’t subtract the coefficients: 8-2=6 is wrong here. And don’t divide the exponents: y^{6}\div y^{2} is y^{4}, not y^{3}.
Example 2

Divide the numbers, subtract the exponents

Simplify each expression.

(a) 12x^{5}\div4x^{2}
(12\div4)\,x^{5-2}
=\boldsymbol{3x^{3}}
(b) \dfrac{20y^{8}}{5y^{3}}
(20\div5)\,y^{8-3}
=\boldsymbol{4y^{5}}
(c) 18a^{9}\div6a^{4}
(18\div6)\,a^{9-4}
=\boldsymbol{3a^{5}}
3

The Power of a Power Law

An outer exponent says how many copies of the inner power to multiply: \left(10^{2}\right)^{3}=10^{2}\times10^{2}\times10^{2}=10^{6}=1\,000\,000, and 2\times3=6.

Power of a Power Law  \left(a^{m}\right)^{n}=a^{mn}
Keep the base, multiply the exponents. Check: 2^{3}=8 and 8^{2}=64. ✓
Example 3

Multiply the exponents

Use the Power of a Power Law to simplify.

(a) \left(3^{2}\right)^{3}
3^{2\times3}=\boldsymbol{3^{6}}
=729
(b) \left(2^{3}\right)^{2}
2^{3\times2}=\boldsymbol{2^{6}}
=64
(c) \left(x^{4}\right)^{3}
x^{4\times3}=\boldsymbol{x^{12}}
(d) \left(y^{2}\right)^{5}
y^{2\times5}=\boldsymbol{y^{10}}

Check it with numbers

With a number base you can check the law: work inside the brackets first, then compare with the law’s answer.

Example 4

More practice: multiply the exponents

Use the Power of a Power Law to simplify.

(a) \left(2^{4}\right)^{2}
2^{4\times2}=\boldsymbol{2^{8}}
=256
(b) \left(6^{2}\right)^{2}
6^{2\times2}=\boldsymbol{6^{4}}
=1\,296
(c) \left(p^{5}\right)^{3}
p^{5\times3}=\boldsymbol{p^{15}}
(d) \left(q^{3}\right)^{6}
q^{3\times6}=\boldsymbol{q^{18}}
Think For (b), Sam added instead of multiplying and still got the right answer. How?
By luck: 2+2=4 and 2\times2=4. Try \left(6^{2}\right)^{3}: adding gives 6^{5}, but the right answer is 6^{6}.

Add, subtract or multiply? Read the clue

same base ×add the exponents
a^{m}\times a^{n}=a^{m+n}
same base ÷subtract the exponents
a^{m}\div a^{n}=a^{m-n}

4

Putting Both Laws Together

Some expressions need both laws. Simplify inside the brackets first by multiplying the exponents, then subtract using the Quotient Law.

Example 5

Brackets first, then divide

Use both the Quotient Law and the Power of a Power Law to simplify.

(a) \dfrac{\left(x^{3}\right)^{4}}{x^{5}}
Top first: x^{3\times4}=x^{12}
x^{12-5}=\boldsymbol{x^{7}}
(b) \left(2^{2}\right)^{3}\div2^{4}
Brackets first: 2^{2\times3}=2^{6}
2^{6-4}=2^{2}=\boldsymbol{4}
(c) \dfrac{\left(a^{2}\right)^{5}}{\left(a^{3}\right)^{2}}
\dfrac{a^{10}}{a^{6}}=a^{10-6}
=\boldsymbol{a^{4}}
✓

Key ideas

Quotient Law: a^{m}\div a^{n}=a^{m-n}, same base only. The factors cancel in pairs: 2^{5}\div2^{2}=2^{3}.
With coefficients: divide the numbers, subtract the exponents: 12x^{5}\div4x^{2}=3x^{3}.
Power of a Power: \left(a^{m}\right)^{n}=a^{mn}. n copies of m factors: \left(2^{3}\right)^{2}=2^{6}, not 2^{5}.
Read the clue: × add, ÷ subtract, ( )ⁿ multiply. Both laws in one? Brackets first, then subtract.
