Factoring ax^2+bx+c (where a\neq1)

Today the x^2 has a number in front, like 2x^2+7x+3. The method is called decomposition, and it is the same six steps every time: multiply, find two numbers, split, group, factor out, check.

I can…
  • find two numbers that multiply to a\times c and add to b
  • factor ax^2+bx+c by decomposition: split the middle term, group in pairs, factor out the common bracket
  • handle negative signs, and take out a GCF first
  • check my answer by expanding

Why the Sum-Product Method Is Not Enough

In Lessons 3 and 4 every trinomial started with a plain x^2, and we looked for two numbers that multiply to c and add to b.

That only works when a=1. For 2x^2+7x+3 we would need two numbers that multiply to 3 and add to 7, and there aren’t any. When a\neq1 we use decomposition instead. The only change to the search: the two numbers multiply to a\times c, not just c.

2

The Decomposition Method, Step by Step

Factor 2x^2+7x+3. Here a=2, b=7, c=3.

1
Multiply a\times c.
2\times3=6
2
Find two numbers that multiply to ac and add to b.
multiply to 6, add to 7:  6 and 1
3
Split the middle term into those two.
2x^2+\textcolor{#c27a0e}{6x}+\textcolor{#c27a0e}{x}+3
4
Group in pairs. Take the GCF out of each pair.
2x\textcolor{#c2417a}{(x+3)}+1\textcolor{#c2417a}{(x+3)}
5
Factor out the bracket both pairs share.
\textcolor{#c2417a}{(x+3)}(2x+1)
6
Check by expanding.
2x^2+x+6x+3=2x^2+7x+3 ✓

Step 5 is the one that feels strange. You already know that 2xy+1y=y(2x+1), because both terms have a y. A bracket works the same way: it is one thing, and if both terms have it, it comes out the front.

Example 1

Every step, then check

Factor using every step: find ac, find the two integers, split, group, factor out the binomial, then check with FOIL.

(a) 3x^2+10x+3
ac=3\times3=9. Integers with product 9 and sum 10: 9 and 1.
3x^2+9x+x+3
=3x(x+3)+1(x+3)
=\boldsymbol{(x+3)(3x+1)}
Check: 3x^2+x+9x+3=3x^2+10x+3 ✓
(b) 2x^2+9x+10
ac=2\times10=20. Multiply to 20, add to 9: 4 and 5.
2x^2+4x+5x+10
=2x(x+2)+5(x+2)
=\boldsymbol{(x+2)(2x+5)}
Check: 2x^2+5x+4x+10=2x^2+9x+10 ✓

Practising Decomposition

The six steps are the same every time. Only the numbers change.

✓
Either order works. 2x^2+6x+x+3 and 2x^2+x+6x+3 both give (x+3)(2x+1).
!
Brackets don’t match? Go back to Step 2. Your two numbers must multiply to ac and add to b.
!
Take out the greatest factor. In 4x^2+10x the GCF is 2x, not 4x: 4x^2+10x=2x(2x+5).
Example 2

a, b and c all positive

Factor each trinomial using decomposition.

(a) 2x^2+11x+12
ac=24; numbers 8 and 3.
2x^2+8x+3x+12
=2x(x+4)+3(x+4)
=\boldsymbol{(x+4)(2x+3)}
Check: 2x^2+3x+8x+12 ✓
(b) 3x^2+17x+10
ac=30; numbers 15 and 2.
3x^2+15x+2x+10
=3x(x+5)+2(x+5)
=\boldsymbol{(x+5)(3x+2)}
Check: 3x^2+2x+15x+10 ✓
(c) 4x^2+12x+5
ac=20; integers 10 and 2.
4x^2+10x+2x+5
=2x(2x+5)+1(2x+5)
=\boldsymbol{(2x+5)(2x+1)}
Check: 4x^2+2x+10x+5 ✓
4

Decomposition with Negative b and/or c

Same six steps. Only the signs of the two numbers in Step 2 change.

ac positive: the two numbers have the same sign. Both are negative when b is negative.
ac negative: the two numbers have opposite signs. The bigger one takes the sign of b.
!
Taking out a negative flips the signs inside. -x+4=-1(x-4), not -1(x+4). The second bracket must match the first one exactly.
Example 3

ac positive, b negative

Factor each trinomial. Here ac is positive, so both integers have the same sign.

(a) 2x^2-9x+4
ac=8, sum -9: -8 and -1.
2x^2-8x-x+4=2x(x-4)-1(x-4)
=\boldsymbol{(x-4)(2x-1)}
Check: 2x^2-x-8x+4=2x^2-9x+4 ✓
(b) 3x^2-11x+6
ac=18, sum -11: -9 and -2.
3x^2-9x-2x+6=3x(x-3)-2(x-3)
=\boldsymbol{(x-3)(3x-2)}
Check: 3x^2-2x-9x+6=3x^2-11x+6 ✓
Example 4

ac negative

Factor each trinomial. Here ac is negative, so the two integers have opposite signs.

(a) 2x^2+5x-12
ac=-24, sum 5: 8 and -3.
2x^2+8x-3x-12
=2x(x+4)-3(x+4)
=\boldsymbol{(x+4)(2x-3)}
Check: 2x^2-3x+8x-12 ✓
(b) 3x^2-4x-4
ac=-12, sum -4: -6 and 2.
3x^2-6x+2x-4
=3x(x-2)+2(x-2)
=\boldsymbol{(x-2)(3x+2)}
Check: 3x^2+2x-6x-4 ✓
(c) 6x^2-5x-6
ac=-36, sum -5: -9 and 4.
6x^2-9x+4x-6
=3x(2x-3)+2(2x-3)
=\boldsymbol{(2x-3)(3x+2)}
Check: 6x^2+4x-9x-6 ✓
5

Removing a GCF First, Then Decomposing

Always check for a GCF before Step 1. It makes the numbers smaller, so the two numbers are easier to find. Write the GCF in front of the final answer.
Example 5

GCF first, then decompose

Factor each expression completely. Remove the GCF first, then decompose what remains.

(a) 6x^2+3x-30
GCF 3: 3(2x^2+x-10)
ac=-20, sum 1: 5 and -4
2x^2+5x-4x-10=x(2x+5)-2(2x+5)
=(2x+5)(x-2)
So 6x^2+3x-30=\boldsymbol{3(2x+5)(x-2)}
Check: 3(2x^2-4x+5x-10)=6x^2+3x-30 ✓
(b) 4x^3-2x^2-30x
GCF 2x: 2x(2x^2-x-15)
ac=-30, sum -1: -6 and 5
2x^2-6x+5x-15=2x(x-3)+5(x-3)
=(x-3)(2x+5)
So 4x^3-2x^2-30x=\boldsymbol{2x(x-3)(2x+5)}
Check: 2x(2x^2+5x-6x-15)=4x^3-2x^2-30x ✓
✓

Key ideas

GCF first. If every term shares a factor, take it out before you start, and keep it in front of the answer.
Steps 1 and 2: multiply a\times c. Find two numbers that multiply to ac and add to b.
Steps 3 to 5: split the middle term, group in pairs, take the GCF out of each pair, then factor out the bracket they share.
Step 6: check by expanding. Signs: ac>0 same signs, ac<0 opposite signs.
