Unit Review: Coordinate Geometry

One reminder and a quick check for each of the nine ideas, then mixed challenges for the test. One big idea ties the unit together: every slanted segment hides a right triangle. Its run and rise give you the length (Pythagoras), half of them give the midpoint, and \tfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} gives the slope.

I can…
  • use the Pythagorean theorem to find a missing side of a right triangle
  • find the length of a horizontal or vertical segment, and use the distance formula for a diagonal one
  • find a midpoint, and work backwards to a missing endpoint
  • calculate a slope and tell whether it is positive, negative, zero or undefined
  • use slopes to test for collinear points, and for parallel and perpendicular segments
  • chain these tools together to name a triangle or a quadrilateral
RF3 Demonstrate an understanding of slope with respect to: rise and run; line segments and lines; rate of change; parallel lines; perpendicular lines.

1

The Pythagorean Theorem

Legs a, b and hypotenuse c satisfy a^2+b^2=c^2: add the squares to find c, subtract to find a leg.
Example 1

A missing side

(a) A right triangle has legs of length 20 and 21. Determine the length of the hypotenuse.
c^2=a^2+b^2=20^2+21^2
c^2=400+441=841
c=\sqrt{841}=\boldsymbol{29}
(b) A right triangle has a hypotenuse of length 34 and one leg of length 16. Determine the length of the other leg.
a^2+16^2=34^2
a^2=1156-256=900
a=\sqrt{900}=\boldsymbol{30}
Square root in one go. \sqrt{\phantom{x}} is 2ndx², and it opens a bracket: √20x²+21x²)= gives 29. For a leg, subtract inside the root: √34x²−16x²)= gives 30.
2

Horizontal and Vertical Segment Lengths

Same y: horizontal, length |x_2-x_1|. Same x: vertical, length |y_2-y_1|. A length is never negative.
Example 2

Straight across, straight up

(a) Determine the length of the horizontal segment joining E(-7,\ 4) and F(5,\ 4).
Same y, so use |x_2-x_1|:
|5-(-7)|=|12|=\boldsymbol{12} units
(b) Determine the length of the vertical segment joining G(3,\ 9) and H(3,\ -6).
Same x, so use |y_2-y_1|:
|-6-9|=|-15|=\boldsymbol{15} units
3

The Distance Formula

For a diagonal segment, the run and rise are the legs of a right triangle, so d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}.
Example 3

Exact distances

(a) Determine the exact distance between M(-2,\ -1) and N(10,\ 4).
d=\sqrt{(10-(-2))^2+(4-(-1))^2}
=\sqrt{12^2+5^2}=\sqrt{144+25}
=\sqrt{169}=\boldsymbol{13} units
(b) Determine the exact distance, as a simplified mixed radical, between P(-4,\ 1) and Q(2,\ 5).
d=\sqrt{(2-(-4))^2+(5-1)^2}
=\sqrt{6^2+4^2}=\sqrt{36+16}=\sqrt{52}
=\sqrt{4}\cdot\sqrt{13}=\boldsymbol{2\sqrt{13}} units
!
Squaring removes the negative. (-5)^2=25, so it doesn’t matter which point you call the first one. Brackets around negatives: 10-(-2)=12.
Check a mixed radical. √52= and 2×√13= both give 7.211102551, so \sqrt{52}=2\sqrt{13}\approx7.2.
4

The Midpoint Formula

The midpoint is the average of the x’s and the average of the y’s: M\left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2},\ \dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right). On the grid: walk half the run and half the rise.
Example 4

The halfway point

Determine the coordinates of the midpoint of the segment joining A(-10,\ 3) and B(6,\ -9).
M=\left(\dfrac{-10+6}{2},\ \dfrac{3+(-9)}{2}\right)=\left(\dfrac{-4}{2},\ \dfrac{-6}{2}\right)=\boldsymbol{(-2,\ -3)}
5

Missing Endpoints and Applications

Know the midpoint and one endpoint? Run the formula backward: double the midpoint, subtract the known endpoint. On the grid, it is the same step again.
Example 5

The other end, and a rest stop

(a) M(5,\ -1) is the midpoint of segment \overline{CD}. If C(-3,\ 7), determine the coordinates of D.
Double the midpoint, subtract C:
x_D=2(5)-(-3)=10+3=13
y_D=2(-1)-7=-2-7=-9
\boldsymbol{D(13,\ -9)}
(b) Two trailheads are at P(2,\ 9) and Q(18,\ -5) on a park map, where each grid unit represents 10 m. A rest stop is built exactly halfway between them. Determine its coordinates, and state how far east and how far south it is from trailhead P.
M=\left(\dfrac{2+18}{2},\ \dfrac{9+(-5)}{2}\right)=\boldsymbol{(10,\ 2)}
East of P: 10-2=8 units =\boldsymbol{80} m. South of P: 9-2=7 units =\boldsymbol{70} m.
6

Slope: Rise Over Run

m=\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}: subtract in the same order top and bottom.
Example 6

The slope of a segment

Determine the slope of the segment joining R(-3,\ -2) and S(9,\ 4).
m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}=\dfrac{4-(-2)}{9-(-3)}=\dfrac{6}{12}=\boldsymbol{\dfrac{1}{2}}
The segment rises 1 unit for every 2 units right, so the slope is positive.
7

Types of Slope

Rising \Rightarrow positive; falling \Rightarrow negative; horizontal \Rightarrow zero; vertical \Rightarrow undefined (zero and undefined are not the same).
Example 7

Positive, negative, zero, or undefined?

Classify the slope of each segment below as positive, negative, zero, or undefined.

(a) A(-5,\ -3) and B(4,\ 3)
m=\dfrac{3-(-3)}{4-(-5)}=\dfrac{6}{9}=\dfrac{2}{3}
Rises left to right: positive.
(b) C(-2,\ 6) and D(3,\ 6)
m=\dfrac{6-6}{3-(-2)}=\dfrac{0}{5}=0
Same y, horizontal: zero.
(c) E(7,\ -4) and F(7,\ 5)
m=\dfrac{5-(-4)}{7-7}=\dfrac{9}{0}
Division by zero, vertical: undefined.
8

Collinearity

Three points lie on one straight line exactly when the two slopes through the shared point match: m_{AB}=m_{BC}.
Example 8

One straight line?

Determine whether the points X(-4,\ -2), Y(2,\ 8), and Z(8,\ 18) are collinear.
m_{XY}=\dfrac{8-(-2)}{2-(-4)}=\dfrac{10}{6}=\dfrac{5}{3}
m_{YZ}=\dfrac{18-8}{8-2}=\dfrac{10}{6}=\dfrac{5}{3}
The two slopes are equal and the segments share the point Y, so all three points lie on one straight line: X, Y, and Z are collinear.
9

Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

Parallel \Rightarrow equal slopes; perpendicular \Rightarrow the slopes multiply to -1 (negative reciprocals: flip it and change the sign).
Example 9

Compare the slopes

(a) Segment AB joins A(-6,\ -1) and B(2,\ 5); segment CD joins C(-3,\ 4) and D(5,\ 10). Are AB and CD parallel, perpendicular, or neither?
m_{AB}=\dfrac{5-(-1)}{2-(-6)}=\dfrac{6}{8}=\dfrac{3}{4}
m_{CD}=\dfrac{10-4}{5-(-3)}=\dfrac{6}{8}=\dfrac{3}{4}
Equal slopes, so AB and CD are parallel.
(b) Segment EF joins E(-2,\ 3) and F(4,\ -5); segment GH joins G(1,\ -4) and H(9,\ 2). Are EF and GH perpendicular?
m_{EF}=\dfrac{-5-3}{4-(-2)}=\dfrac{-8}{6}=-\dfrac{4}{3}
m_{GH}=\dfrac{2-(-4)}{9-1}=\dfrac{6}{8}=\dfrac{3}{4}
m_{EF}\times m_{GH}=-\dfrac{4}{3}\times\dfrac{3}{4}=-1, so yes, EF\perp GH.

Mixed Review: Putting It All Together

Each question below chains several skills together: exactly what a test question looks like. (Check Challenge 2 with the chip in the warm-up check explainer.)

Challenge 1

Triangle PQR

Triangle PQR has vertices P(-5,\ -3), Q(3,\ 1), and R(-1,\ 9).

(a) Determine the slope of PQ and the slope of QR, and show that the triangle has a right angle at Q.
m_{PQ}=\dfrac{1-(-3)}{3-(-5)}=\dfrac{4}{8}=\dfrac{1}{2}
m_{QR}=\dfrac{9-1}{-1-3}=\dfrac{8}{-4}=-2
\dfrac{1}{2}\times(-2)=-1, so PQ\perp QR and the angle at Q is \boldsymbol{90^\circ}.
(b) Determine the length of each side using the distance formula, and verify that the Pythagorean theorem holds.
PQ=\sqrt{8^2+4^2}=\sqrt{80}=\boldsymbol{4\sqrt5}
QR=\sqrt{(-4)^2+8^2}=\sqrt{80}=\boldsymbol{4\sqrt5}
PR=\sqrt{4^2+12^2}=\sqrt{160}=\boldsymbol{4\sqrt{10}}
Check: 80+80=160 ✓
(c) Determine the midpoint of the hypotenuse, PR.
M=\left(\dfrac{-5+(-1)}{2},\ \dfrac{-3+9}{2}\right)
=\left(\dfrac{-6}{2},\ \dfrac{6}{2}\right)=\boldsymbol{(-3,\ 3)}
Challenge 2

Name the quadrilateral

Quadrilateral KLMN has vertices K(-6,\ -2), L(-2,\ 4), M(6,\ 2), and N(2,\ -4).

(a) Determine the slope of each of the four sides.
m_{KL}=\dfrac{4-(-2)}{-2-(-6)}=\dfrac{6}{4}=\dfrac{3}{2}
m_{LM}=\dfrac{2-4}{6-(-2)}=\dfrac{-2}{8}=-\dfrac{1}{4}
m_{MN}=\dfrac{-4-2}{2-6}=\dfrac{-6}{-4}=\dfrac{3}{2}
m_{NK}=\dfrac{-2-(-4)}{-6-2}=\dfrac{2}{-8}=-\dfrac{1}{4}
(b) Determine the length of each of the four sides.
KL=\sqrt{4^2+6^2}=\sqrt{52}=\boldsymbol{2\sqrt{13}}
LM=\sqrt{8^2+(-2)^2}=\sqrt{68}=\boldsymbol{2\sqrt{17}}
MN=\sqrt{(-4)^2+(-6)^2}=\sqrt{52}=\boldsymbol{2\sqrt{13}}
NK=\sqrt{(-8)^2+2^2}=\sqrt{68}=\boldsymbol{2\sqrt{17}}
(c) What is the most precise name for quadrilateral KLMN? Justify your answer.
Both pairs of opposite sides have equal slopes (\tfrac32 and -\tfrac14), so both pairs are parallel, and opposite sides are equal in length. Adjacent sides are neither perpendicular (\tfrac32\times\left(-\tfrac14\right)=-\tfrac38\ne-1) nor equal in length, so it is a parallelogram: not a rectangle, rhombus, or square.
Challenge 3

A missing coordinate

Segment JK joins J(-4,\ -1) and K(2,\ q). Segment LM joins L(-3,\ 5) and M(3,\ -4).

(a) Determine the value of q so that JK\parallel LM.
m_{LM}=\dfrac{-4-5}{3-(-3)}=\dfrac{-9}{6}=-\dfrac{3}{2}
Parallel means m_{JK}=m_{LM}:
\dfrac{q-(-1)}{2-(-4)}=\dfrac{q+1}{6}=-\dfrac{3}{2}
2(q+1)=-18\ \Rightarrow\ q+1=-9\ \Rightarrow\ \boldsymbol{q=-10}
(b) Using your value of q, determine the exact length of JK.
J(-4,\ -1) and K(2,\ -10):
JK=\sqrt{(2-(-4))^2+(-10-(-1))^2}
=\sqrt{6^2+(-9)^2}=\sqrt{36+81}=\sqrt{117}
=\sqrt{9}\cdot\sqrt{13}=\boldsymbol{3\sqrt{13}} units

Challenge 3 check: slide K until the segments are parallel

K must stay on the vertical line x=2. Only one height makes JK point the same way as LM.

✓

Key ideas

Every diagonal segment hides a right triangle. Its legs are the run and the rise: d=\sqrt{\text{run}^2+\text{rise}^2}. Exact answers as a simplified mixed radical.
A midpoint is an average. Average the x’s, average the y’s. Backwards: double the midpoint, subtract the known end.
Slope =\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}, same order top and bottom. Rising +, falling -, flat 0, vertical undefined.
Compare slopes: collinear m_{AB}=m_{BC}; parallel m_1=m_2; perpendicular m_1\times m_2=-1. To name a shape: slopes first, then lengths.
