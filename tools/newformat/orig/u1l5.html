Perfect Squares, Perfect Cubes, and Estimating Roots

36 tiles make a 6 × 6 square, so \sqrt{36}=6. Today we learn the perfect squares and perfect cubes by heart and read their roots straight off the list. Then we trap the roots that land in between, like \sqrt{40}, which hides between 6 and 7.

I can…
  • recognize the perfect squares up to 225 and the perfect cubes up to 1 000
  • find a square root or a cube root of a perfect square or cube in my head
  • trap a square root between two whole numbers and say which one it is closer to
  • estimate a square root to one decimal place, then check it on a calculator
  • find the side of a square from its area, and the edge of a cube from its volume

Perfect Squares and Square Roots

A perfect square is a whole number multiplied by itself: 6\times6=36, so 36 is a perfect square. The square root undoes that, so \sqrt{36}=6.
Memorize this list. It is used for the rest of the course. Tap a ? to check yourself.
!
A square root is not half. \sqrt{36} is 6, not 18. Ask: what number times itself makes 36?

Perfect Cubes and Cube Roots

A perfect cube is a whole number multiplied by itself three times: 4\times4\times4=64. The cube root undoes that, so \sqrt[3]{64}=4. A number can be both: 64=8^2 and 64=4^3.
Perfect cubes. Tap a ? to check yourself.
!
Watch the little 3. \sqrt[3]{64}=4 (a cube root) but \sqrt{64}=8 (a square root). The small number in the root sign tells you how many times the number is multiplied by itself.

Estimating Square Roots of Non-Perfect Squares

If a number is not a perfect square, trap it between the perfect square just below and the one just above. Their roots are two whole numbers in a row. Then see which perfect square the number is nearer to.
36<40<49, so 6<\sqrt{40}<7
40 is 4 of the 13 steps from 36 to 49, about 0.3 of the way, so \sqrt{40}\approx6.3.
!
Compare with the perfect squares, not the roots. To place \sqrt{40}, ask how far 40 is from 36 and from 49, not how far it is from 6 and 7.

One decimal place: zoom in on the gap

The gap from 36 to 49 is 13 steps. 40 is 4 of those steps in, a bit less than a third of the way. So \sqrt{40} is a bit less than a third of the way from 6 to 7: about 6.3.

For cube roots, comparing with the neighbouring cubes is only a rough guide.

State the two consecutive whole numbers each root lies between, which one it is closer to, and a one-decimal estimate. In (c), plot your estimate of \sqrt{27} on the number line.

(a) \sqrt{27}
25<27<36, so 5<\sqrt{27}<6: between 5 and 6.
27-25=2 but 36-27=9: closer to 5.
27 is 2 of the 11 steps from 25 to 36, about 0.2 of the way:
\sqrt{27}\approx5.2
(b) \sqrt{95}
81<95<100, so 9<\sqrt{95}<10: between 9 and 10.
95-81=14 but 100-95=5: closer to 10.
95 is 14 of the 19 steps from 81 to 100, about 0.7 of the way:
\sqrt{95}\approx9.7
(c) Plot your estimate of \sqrt{27}. Tap the line.
From (a), \sqrt{27}\approx5.2: a little way past 5, well short of halfway to 6.

Using a Calculator to Approximate Roots

On the TI-30XIIS, press 2nd x² (the \sqrt{\phantom{x}} key), the number, then ENTER, and round to the place value asked for: \sqrt{40}=6.32455\ldots\approx6.32.

Always estimate first. If the calculator value is far from your estimate, you keyed something in wrong.

5

Application: Area and Volume

Roots run the area and volume formulas backwards.

A square with area A has side length
s=\sqrt{A}
because A=s\times s
A cube with volume V has edge length
e=\sqrt[3]{V}
because V=e\times e\times e
Cube root on the TI-30XIIS. Type the 3 first, then \sqrt[x]{\phantom{0}} (2nd of ^), then the number: 32nd^64ENTER gives 4.
Example 5

Side lengths from area and volume

Estimate to the nearest whole number first, then give the calculator value to the nearest hundredth.

(a) A square school garden has an area of 55\text{ m}^2. Find its side length.
s=\sqrt{A}=\sqrt{55}
49<55<64, so 7<s<8; 55 is 6 past 49 but 9 short of 64: s\approx7\text{ m}
Calculator: \sqrt{55}=7.41619\ldots\approx 7.42 m
(b) A cube-shaped storage crate has a volume of 90\text{ cm}^3. Find its edge length.
e=\sqrt[3]{V}=\sqrt[3]{90}
64<90<125, so 4<e<5; 90 is 26 past 64 but 35 short of 125: e\approx4\text{ cm}
Calculator: \sqrt[3]{90}=4.48140\ldots\approx 4.48 cm
Check: 4.48^3=89.9\ldots ✓

Key ideas

Perfect squares 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144, 169, 196, 225. The square root undoes squaring: \sqrt{144}=12 because 12\times12=144.
Perfect cubes 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343, 512, 729, 1 000. The cube root undoes cubing: \sqrt[3]{125}=5. 64 is both: 8^2 and 4^3.
Trap it: 36<40<49, so 6<\sqrt{40}<7. 40 is nearer 36, so \sqrt{40}\approx6.3. Then the calculator: 6.32455\ldots\approx6.32.
Backwards formulas: side of a square s=\sqrt{A}; edge of a cube e=\sqrt[3]{V}. Estimate first, then use the calculator.
